# Before you invest: an angel's checklist

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.**

## In one screen

This is for one person investing their own money in a private startup round, directly. If you are joining a special
purpose vehicle (a company formed only to hold one investment) or a group's fund, read
[How members invest](../groups/how-members-invest.md) too.

The plain answer on your own legal duties: buying in a private round triggers **no securities filing by you**. The
federal notice, Form D, is the company's to file within 15 days after its first sale ([17 CFR 230.503][e503]). What
falls on you is keeping records for tax, and checking what you are buying. Two things change the answer: being paid to
introduce other investors, and running a vehicle for others for pay. Both raise broker and adviser questions covered in
[How members invest](../groups/how-members-invest.md).

## The checklist

Work through it in order. Each line is something to do or to ask, with the reason.

### 1. Are you eligible to buy?

- [ ] Know which accredited investor category you meet, and be ready to show it. See
  [Accredited investor rules](accredited-investor.md). Most private rounds rely on Rule 506, and the company must
  believe or verify that you qualify ([17 CFR 230.501(a)][e501], [230.506][e506]).
- [ ] Ask the company which exemption it is using: `{{exemption}}`. Under Rule 506(b) the company may not advertise and
  needs a reasonable belief that you are accredited. Under Rule 506(c) it may advertise but must take reasonable steps
  to verify, so expect to give documents or a letter.
- [ ] If you are not accredited, ask before going further. Rule 506(b) allows up to 35 non-accredited purchasers who are
  sophisticated (counted across the company's Rule 506(b) sales in any 90-day period), but the company must then give them disclosure documents that most seed rounds don't prepare
  ([230.506(b)(2)][e506], [230.502(b)][e502]).

### 2. What are you buying?

- [ ] Name the instrument: `{{instrument}}`. A SAFE or convertible note converts later; preferred stock is a priced round.
  See [SAFE and convertible note explained](safe-and-convertible-note.md).
- [ ] Write down the economic terms in one line: valuation cap, discount, interest and maturity (notes), price per
  share and liquidation preference (preferred).
- [ ] Ask what else is outstanding that converts ahead of or alongside you: other SAFEs, notes, option pool promises.
  The company's full capitalization table is the only way to know what your percentage will be.
- [ ] Know the company's entity: `{{company_entity}}`. Units in an LLC taxed as a partnership produce a yearly Schedule
  K-1 and can't be qualified small business stock; stock in a C corporation can.
- [ ] Understand that you can't freely resell. Securities bought in a Rule 506 offering are restricted
  ([230.502(d)][e502]). Plan to hold until a sale of the company or a later round. If the company doesn't file reports
  with the SEC, Rule 144 says "a minimum of one year must elapse" from your purchase, counted from full payment, before
  you resell under it, and its other conditions apply ([230.144(d)][e144]).

### 3. Who are you dealing with?

- [ ] Look up the company and its founders. [What each side checks](what-each-side-checks.md) lists the public records,
  what each shows, and why "nothing found" clears no one.
- [ ] Ask who introduced the deal and whether anyone is paid for it: `{{introducer}}`. A person paid by the company on
  the deal (a percentage of money raised) may need to be a registered broker
  ([15 U.S.C. 78c(a)(4)][us78c], [78o(a)][us78o]) and licensed in Colorado ([C.R.S. 11-51-401][t11]). A contract made in
  violation of the Exchange Act can be void as to the violator ([78cc(b)][us78cc]). Colorado gives a buyer a claim
  against an unlicensed broker-dealer or sales representative, but generally not for sales in transactions exempt under
  11-51-308(1)(p), which is how Rule 506 rounds are exempt in Colorado, so don't count on it ([11-51-604(2)][t11]). Either
  way, a company that sold this way may have other problems.
- [ ] Know your Colorado claims if you are misled. A seller who makes an untrue statement or misleading omission is liable
  to the buyer unless the seller proves it couldn't have known with reasonable care; anyone who knowingly gives
  substantial help to such a violation can be liable too; and a clause making you waive these rights is void. No one may
  sue "more than three years after the discovery of the facts giving rise to a cause of action", or after they should
  have been discovered with reasonable diligence, "and in no event more than five years after the purchase or sale". A
  claim that the sale was unregistered or the seller unlicensed must be brought within 2 years after the contract of sale
  ([C.R.S. 11-51-604(4), (5), (8), (11)][t11]; the words quoted are 604(8), read 2026-11-07).
- [ ] Never send money on wire instructions received only by email. Confirm by phone with a number you already have.

### 4. What did you review?

Review the startup in its own data room. Write down what you looked at, so you can show later what you knew.

- [ ] Charter or articles, and any amendments. Bylaws or LLC agreement.
- [ ] Capitalization table, and the stock or unit ledger it comes from.
- [ ] Founder stock agreements: vesting, repurchase on departure, and whether each founder filed an 83(b) election.
- [ ] Intellectual property assignments from every founder, employee and contractor.
- [ ] Earlier financing documents: SAFEs, notes, side letters, and any rights they give earlier investors.
- [ ] Whether the company filed Form D for earlier rounds (search EDGAR, the SEC's filing system) and any state notices.
- [ ] Financial statements, bank balance, monthly spending, and how many months the money lasts.
- [ ] Material contracts, debt, and any claim or dispute, threatened or filed.

### 5. What do you want in writing?

- [ ] Information rights. A SAFE by itself gives you no right to financial information. If you want quarterly numbers,
  get it in a side letter. See [Side letter basics](side-letter.md).
- [ ] A right to invest in the next round (pro rata right), if you want one.
- [ ] Most favoured nation treatment, if others may get better terms later in the same round.
- [ ] A promise from the company to give you what you need for the tax items in step 6.

### 6. Tax records, from day one

The tax benefits below depend on facts you must be able to prove years later. Collect them at signing.

- [ ] **Qualified small business stock** ([26 U.S.C. 1202][us1202]). For stock acquired after 2025-07-04, part or all of
  the gain can be excluded after 3, 4 or 5 years (50%, 75%, 100%). It must be bought at original issue from a C
  corporation whose gross assets were at or under $75 million before and right after the issuance, among other tests.
  Keep: the signed purchase documents, proof of payment, the date, the company's written statement of its gross assets,
  and the company's agreement to give you what you need later. Whether a SAFE counts as stock for this purpose has no
  IRS answer; assume the clock may start only at conversion.
- [ ] **Rollover** ([26 U.S.C. 1045][us1045]). If you sell qualified small business stock held more than 6 months, you
  can defer the gain by buying other qualified small business stock during the 60-day period that begins on the date of the sale,
  but only if you elect it: report the sale on Form 8949 with code R ([Form 8949 instructions][i8949]). Diary the 60 days at any sale.
- [ ] **Loss on small business stock** ([26 U.S.C. 1244][us1244]). A loss on stock issued to you (or to your partnership) by a
  domestic corporation that had received $1 million or less for its stock when it issued yours can be ordinary rather
  than capital, up to $50,000 a year, or $100,000 on a joint return.
- [ ] **Basis records.** Keep what you paid and when, for Form 8949 when you sell ([IRS instructions][i8949]).
- [ ] **Colorado Advanced Industries investment tax credit.** A credit of 25%, or 35% if the company is in a rural or
  economically distressed area, up to $100,000 per investment, for an equity investment (convertible securities count)
  of at least $10,000 in a certified small Colorado company in an advanced industry. You must file the application
  "within ninety days after making a qualified investment in a certified and qualified small business" (read
  2026-11-07). Credits are granted in the order complete applications arrive, within a yearly state limit ($4 million a
  year through 2026, $2.5 million a year from 2027 through 2031), and investments must be made before 2032-01-01.
  Excluded: C corporations; founders, employees and contractors of the company and their spouses; anyone who controls
  the company in the six months before or after; and anyone who has already invested over $50,000 in it or owns over
  10% ([C.R.S. 24-48.5-112][t24], [39-22-532][t39]). The statute requires the company to be certified; the state's
  program page says to get the certification before investing ([program page][oedit]). Ask the company whether it is
  certified before you wire. Have your own tax professional review your claim before you file it.

### 7. Decisions that matter if there is a fight later

These terms are chosen now and decide what you can do if founders fall out, a founder behaves badly, or other investors
try to take control. See [What your documents should say beforehand](../fights/before-the-fight.md).

- [ ] Do you get a vote, a board seat or an observer seat? A SAFE gives none.
- [ ] Are you bound by a drag-along (an agreement to vote for a sale the majority approves)?
- [ ] Which decisions need investor approval (protective provisions)?
- [ ] What information can you see, and can the company's records be inspected by you under state law?
- [ ] If you will own or control 25% or more of a company that employs people in Colorado, know that the Colorado Wage
  Act can treat you as an employer for unpaid wages unless you show full delegation of day-to-day control
  ([C.R.S. 8-4-101(6)][t8]).

### 8. Before money moves

- [ ] You approve the final documents. Nothing is signed or sent for you without your approval.
- [ ] Wire instructions confirmed by phone.
- [ ] Countersigned copies received from the company, with the date of closing: `{{closing_date}}`.
- [ ] Your investment record updated: date, amount, instrument, company, basis, and where the documents are kept.

## Drafting notes

### Choice: invest directly, through a special purpose vehicle, or through a group's fund

| Option | What you get | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| Directly | Your name on the capitalization table, any rights the documents give investors, your own tax records | The company may set a minimum check above what you want to put in; you do your own paperwork |
| Through a special purpose vehicle | A smaller check can join, and one manager handles the paperwork | You hold an interest in the vehicle, not the startup, so you have no direct vote or information right; fees and carried interest; a Schedule K-1 each year |
| Through a group's pooled fund | Diversification across deals | Less choice per deal; fees and carried interest; qualified small business stock only if you were in the fund when it bought ([1202(g)][us1202]) |

**Recommendation:** invest directly when you can meet the company's minimum and want information rights or a direct
relationship. Use a vehicle when the check is small, you trust the manager, and you accept that the manager, not you,
holds the rights. Ask before joining any vehicle whether the manager is paid and how, because that decides whether the
manager needs to be a registered adviser or broker.

**Where a lawyer should look:** the vehicle's operating agreement (fees, carried interest, removal of the manager, what
happens at exit), and whether you would hold your interest on the date the vehicle buys, for the tax benefit.

### Choice: what to ask for in a side letter

See [Side letter basics](side-letter.md). **Recommendation:** at minimum ask for annual financial statements and a
promise to give the facts you need for qualified small business stock. A lawyer should read any most favoured nation
clause against the company's other side letters.
