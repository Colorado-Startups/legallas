# Side letter basics

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.**

## In one screen

A side letter is a short contract between the company and one investor that adds rights the main investment document
doesn't give. For an angel, it matters most with a SAFE, because a SAFE alone gives no vote and no right to
information. The usual asks are: financial information, a right to invest in the next round (pro rata), most favoured
nation treatment, and help with the tax records for qualified small business stock.

A side letter binds only the company and you. It can't take rights away from other investors, and if the company has
promised others a most favoured nation right, your better terms may flow to them too.

## What a side letter commonly covers

| Clause | What it gives you | Why you might want it |
|---|---|---|
| Information | Annual, sometimes quarterly, financial statements | A SAFE holder isn't a stockholder, so has no statutory inspection right ([C.R.S. 7-116-102][t7], [8 Del. C. 220][d8s7]) |
| Pro rata | The right to buy your share of the next round | Keeps your percentage if the company does well |
| Most favoured nation | The right to switch to better terms the company gives later investors on the same instrument | Protects you from a lower cap given to someone else |
| Qualified small business stock | The company's statement of facts and promise to provide information | The tax benefit turns on company facts you can't see ([26 U.S.C. 1202][us1202]) |
| Colorado tax credit | The company's help with your application | The credit has a 90-day window and needs company documents ([C.R.S. 39-22-532][t39]) |
| Confidentiality | Your promise to keep company information private | The company will ask for it in return for information |

The tax rows show the law with its source. Have your own tax professional review them for your own return.

Y Combinator publishes a pro rata side letter to go with its SAFE ([Y Combinator SAFE page][ycsafe]); the design map records it
as shareable only unmodified, so it is linked here, not copied. The form below is original and broader.

## Short-form investor side letter

> **Side letter**
>
> {{company_name}}, a {{company_state}} [corporation / limited liability company] (the "Company"), and {{investor_name}}
> (the "Investor") agree as follows, in connection with the Investor's {{instrument}} dated {{instrument_date}} for
> {{purchase_amount}} (the "Investment").
>
> **1. Information.** Until this letter ends, the Company will deliver to the Investor:
>
> (a) within {{annual_days}} days after the end of each fiscal year, a balance sheet as of year end and statements of
> income and cash flows for the year, which need not be audited; and
>
> (b) within {{quarterly_days}} days after the end of each of the first three fiscal quarters, an unaudited balance
> sheet and income statement for the quarter.
>
> The Company need not deliver information that it reasonably determines is a trade secret, or that would waive the
> attorney-client privilege.
>
> **2. Pro rata right.** If the Company sells shares of preferred stock in its next equity financing after the date of
> this letter, the Investor may buy up to its pro rata share of the shares sold, on the same terms as the other
> purchasers. The Investor's pro rata share is the number of shares the Investment converts into in that financing,
> divided by the Company's fully diluted capitalization immediately before the new money, as calculated in the
> Investment's conversion provisions. The Company will give the Investor written notice of the financing and its terms
> at least 10 days before it closes.
>
> **3. Most favoured nation.** If, before the Investment converts or ends, the Company issues any other instrument of the
> same kind as the Investment with a lower valuation cap, a greater discount, or other terms more favourable to the
> holder, the Company will tell the Investor in writing within 10 days, with a copy of the documents. The Investor may
> then, by written notice, amend the Investment to have those terms.
>
> **4. Qualified small business stock.** The Company states that, as of the date of this letter: (a) it is a domestic
> C corporation; and (b) its aggregate gross assets, as defined in section 1202(d)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code, did
> not exceed the limit in section 1202(d)(1) at any time before, and will not exceed it immediately after, the
> Investment. The Company will use commercially reasonable efforts to (i) answer the Investor's reasonable written
> requests for information needed to determine whether the Investor's stock is qualified small business stock, and
> (ii) file any reports the Secretary of the Treasury requires under section 1202(d)(1)(C). The Company does not promise
> that the stock will qualify. *(Delete this section if the Company is an LLC.)*
>
> **5. Colorado Advanced Industries credit.** *(Optional.)* The Company states that it holds a current certification as
> a qualified small business for the Colorado Advanced Industries investment tax credit, and will give the Investor, on
> request, the documents the Investor needs to apply within the time allowed.
>
> **6. Confidentiality.** The Investor will keep information received under this letter confidential and use it only
> to monitor the Investment, except information that is public through no fault of the Investor, or that the law
> requires the Investor to disclose. The Investor may share it with its own lawyers, accountants and tax advisers who
> keep it confidential. Nothing in this letter stops the Investor from reporting a possible violation of law to a
> government agency.
>
> **7. When this letter ends.** This letter ends on the earliest of: (a) the Investment converting into shares in a
> financing in which the Investor receives information and pro rata rights at least as good as these, except that
> section 2 applies to that financing and section 4 survives; (b) the Company's initial public offering; (c) a sale of
> the Company; and (d) the Company's dissolution.
>
> **8. General.** The Investor may assign this letter to an entity it controls, or that controls or is under common
> control with it, on written notice. This letter may be changed only in a writing signed by both parties. It is
> governed by the laws of {{governing_law}}. Notices to the Investor go to {{notice_address}}. It may be signed in
> counterparts and electronically.
>
> {{company_name}}  By: ____________________  Name and title: ________  Date: ________
>
> {{investor_name}}  By: ____________________  Date: ________

## Drafting notes

### Information rights

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| Annual statements only | Easy for the company to accept |
| Annual and quarterly | Better monitoring; some companies give quarterly only to larger investors |
| Inspection right like a stockholder's | Rarely given to a SAFE holder |

**Recommendation:** ask for annual and quarterly statements; settle for annual. **Where a lawyer should look:** the
confidentiality clause, so it doesn't stop you from talking to a regulator (the reporting carve-out in section 6) or to
your own advisers.

### Pro rata right

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| The next round only (as drafted) | Usual for a SAFE side letter |
| Every future round | Companies resist; later investors may insist it be cut back |
| Only if you are a "major investor" above a threshold | Common in priced rounds |

**Recommendation:** the next round only. **Where a lawyer should look:** how the pro rata share is calculated, and
whether the next round's lead can require you to waive it.

### Most favoured nation

**Recommendation:** ask for it if the company is still selling SAFEs. **Where a lawyer should look:** whether "more
favourable" covers a side letter given to someone else, and whether the company has promised the same right to others.

### Qualified small business stock

**Recommendation:** always ask, for a C corporation. A statement of facts at signing is worth more than a later request,
because the gross assets test looks at the moment of issuance ([26 U.S.C. 1202(d)][us1202]). **Where a lawyer should
look:** whether a SAFE is "stock" for the clock, which no IRS guidance settles.
