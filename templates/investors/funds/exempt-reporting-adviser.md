# Advisers Act exempt reporting

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.**

## In one screen

The management company of a venture fund is an investment adviser: it is paid to advise the fund about securities
([15 U.S.C. 80b-2(a)(11)][us80b2]). It doesn't have to register with the SEC if it advises **only venture capital
funds** (at any size) or **only private funds with under $150 million** in the United States ([80b-3(l), (m)][us80b3]).
Either way it is an **exempt reporting adviser**: it files a shorter Form ADV within 60 days and updates it every year,
and some rules still apply to it. Colorado separately exempts private fund advisers from a state licence if they file
and pay a fee. Delaware's position for a fund manager is unsettled.

## The two federal routes

| | Venture capital fund adviser | Private fund adviser under $150 million |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Advises solely venture capital funds | Advises solely qualifying private funds, with less than $150 million in private fund assets managed from a place of business in the United States |
| Size limit | None | Under $150 million, measured yearly with the Form ADV update |
| The catch | Every fund must meet the venture capital fund definition | Advising one separately managed account ends the route |
| Source | [80b-3(l)][us80b3]; [Rule 203(l)-1][e203l] | [80b-3(m)][us80b3]; [Rule 203(m)-1][e203m] |

**The venture capital fund definition** ([Rule 203(l)-1][e203l]), in short:

1. The fund represents to investors that it pursues a venture capital strategy.
2. Immediately after buying any asset, no more than 20% of the fund's capital contributions and uncalled commitments is
   in non-qualifying investments (short-term holdings such as cash aside). Qualifying investments are, mainly, equity
   bought directly from a private operating company.
3. The fund doesn't borrow or guarantee more than 15% of its capital contributions and uncalled commitments, and any such
   borrowing is for a non-renewable term of no more than 120 days (with a carve-out for guaranteeing portfolio company
   debt up to the fund's investment in it).
4. Investors have no redemption rights except in extraordinary circumstances.
5. The fund isn't a registered investment company or a business development company.

## What an exempt reporting adviser files

| Filing | When | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Form ADV, Part 1A, completing only the items exempt reporting advisers answer, through the federal adviser filing system (IARD) | Within 60 days after first relying on the exemption: `{{first_reliance_date}}` | [Rule 204-4][e204-4]; [Form ADV instructions][adv-i] |
| Annual updating amendment | Within 90 days after `{{fiscal_year_end}}` | [Rule 204-1][e204-1]; [Form ADV instructions][adv-i] |
| Other-than-annual amendment | Promptly when certain information becomes inaccurate | Same |
| No Part 2 brochure | Exempt reporting advisers don't deliver one | Same |
| Final report | When the adviser stops relying on the exemption | Same |

The report is public. It shows the adviser's owners, its private funds (with each fund's size and number of investors)
and any disciplinary history.

## What still applies to an exempt reporting adviser

| Rule | What it requires | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-fraud | No fraud or deceit on any client or prospective client; applies to every investment adviser, registered or not | [15 U.S.C. 80b-6][us80b6] |
| Pooled vehicles | No false or misleading statements to a fund's investors or prospective investors | [Rule 206(4)-8][e206-8] |
| Pay to play | A two-year time-out from being paid by a government entity after certain political contributions by the adviser or its covered associates; applies to exempt reporting advisers by its words. The SEC proposed on 2026-09-10 to rescind the rule entirely (comments close 2026-11-09); it stays in force until a final rule | [Rule 206(4)-5][e206-5]; [proposal, 91 FR 57698][p2p] |
| Insider trading policies | Written policies to prevent misuse of material nonpublic information, for advisers subject to the Act's record-keeping section, which excludes only advisers exempt under section 203(b), so on a reading of the cross-references it reaches exempt reporting advisers | [80b-4a][us80b4a]; [80b-4][us80b4] |
| Records and examination | The SEC may require records and reports of these exempt advisers, and may examine them | [80b-3(l), (m)][us80b3] |
| Anti-money laundering program | From 2028-01-01, not before | [31 CFR 1032.210][fincen] |

**What doesn't apply** to an adviser that isn't registered with the SEC, though many funds promise some of it by contract
anyway: the brochure, the compliance program rule, the code of ethics rule, the custody rule, the marketing rule, and the
performance fee limit in section 205, which now reaches only advisers "registered or required to be registered"
([80b-5(a)][us80b5]).

## Colorado

An exempt reporting adviser isn't a federal covered adviser, so Colorado's licensing law applies to it unless it is
exempt ([C.R.S. 11-51-201(5.5), 11-51-401][t11]). Since 2026-08-12 that is true whether or not the adviser has a place
of business in Colorado: the old limit was removed ([11-51-401(1.5)][t11]).

The statute's own exemption for advisers with no Colorado office ([11-51-402(5)][t11]) has three tests joined by "or":
the adviser is exempt under section 203(b) of the Advisers Act, its only Colorado clients are listed institutions, or it had at
most 5 other clients in the past 12 months. The first is hard to apply to fund managers, because venture and
private fund advisers rely on sections 203(l) and 203(m), not 203(b). The practical route is Colorado's rule
([3 CCR 704-1, Rule 51-4.11(IA)][ccr]). An adviser solely to private funds is exempt from a licence if:

- neither it nor an advisory affiliate has a Rule 506(d) disqualifying event;
- it files with Colorado each exempt reporting adviser report and amendment it files with the SEC, through the federal
  system; and
- it pays the fees the Securities Commissioner sets.

An adviser to a 3(c)(1) fund that isn't a venture capital fund must also keep that fund's investors to qualified clients
(net worth measured without the primary residence), give each investor written disclosures about services and duties,
and deliver audited financial statements every year. An SEC-registered adviser can't use the rule. The adviser's
representatives are exempt too, and an adviser that stops qualifying has 90 days to license or file notice. The rule
doesn't protect existing investors who aren't qualified clients. Place of business in Colorado:
`{{colorado_place_of_business}}`.

## Delaware

Delaware requires an adviser to register unless it has no place of business in Delaware and either its only Delaware
clients are institutions or it had no more than 5 other Delaware clients in the past 12 months
([6 Del. C. 73-301(c)][d73s3]). Delaware's private fund adviser exemption excludes every fund relying on 3(c)(1),
venture funds included ([Rule 711][derules]), so a manager with a Delaware office advising 3(c)(1) funds has no rule
exemption. Whether a fund formed in Delaware, or its investors, count as the adviser's Delaware clients isn't settled by
the text. A manager with any Delaware presence should get a lawyer's answer or ask the Delaware Investor
Protection Unit.

## When an exempt adviser must register

- A private fund adviser loses its exemption at $150 million in private fund assets, calculated yearly under the Form
  ADV instructions ([Rule 203(m)-1][e203m]). The rule sets no transition; the SEC's 2011 adopting release, read only
  through excerpts here, allows up to 90 days after the annual update that reports it.
- A venture capital fund adviser that starts advising a fund that isn't a venture capital fund loses that route.
- An adviser that isn't exempt is generally left to its state below $100 million in regulatory assets, may register
  with the SEC from $100 million to just under $110 million, and must register once past that band; once registered, it
  need not withdraw unless it falls below $90 million ([80b-3a][us80b3a]; [Rule 203A-1][e203a1]).
- An adviser registered or required to be registered with the SEC, doing business in Colorado, files a notice and pays a fee to the Securities
  Commissioner, unless the no-office exemption applies. The notice is "effective from its receipt by the securities
  commissioner until December 31 of each year" and is renewed each year
  ([C.R.S. 11-51-401(1.6), 11-51-403(3)(c), 11-51-404(1)(b)][t11]; 403(3)(c) quoted, read 2026-11-07).

## Drafting notes

### Choice: venture route or $150 million route

**Recommendation:** the venture capital fund route if every fund will invest mainly in primary equity of private
companies, because it has no size limit. Choose the $150 million route if a fund will buy secondaries, credit or other
non-qualifying assets beyond the 20% basket. **Where a lawyer should look:** bridge notes, secondary purchases, fund-of-
funds investments, and any separately managed account.

### Choice: optional rules to adopt anyway

**Recommendation:** adopt a written code of ethics and keep basic books and records, even though the rules for registered
advisers don't bind an exempt adviser. Limited partners expect them, and they make a later registration easier.
