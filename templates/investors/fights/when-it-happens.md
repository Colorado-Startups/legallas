# What you owe when it happens

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.** Get your own lawyer at the first sign of a
fight, and don't rely on the company's lawyer: the company's lawyer represents the company, not you.

## In one screen

What an investor owes in a fight depends on the role the investor holds, not on how much money is at stake:

| Your role: `{{investor_role}}` | What you owe | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Director appointed by an investor | Duties to the company and all its stockholders, not to your fund: good faith, care, and acting in the company's best interests | [C.R.S. 7-108-401][t7]; Delaware: judge-made ([In re Trados][trados]) |
| Observer | Generally no fiduciary duty; what your observer agreement and confidentiality terms say | Judge-made; contract |
| Stockholder with no control | Generally free to vote your shares in your own interest; bound by your voting agreement and other contracts | Contract; judge-made |
| Controlling stockholder, alone or as a group | Fiduciary duties to other stockholders in transactions where you have a conflicting interest (judge-made); Delaware's safe harbours for controller transactions | [8 Del. C. 144][d8s4] |
| Member or manager of an LLC | The duties the act and the operating agreement set | [C.R.S. 7-80-404][t7]; Delaware: the agreement ([6 Del. C. 18-1101][d18s11]) |
| SAFE holder | Only your contract | Contract |

And in every role: you may not lie to or mislead anyone you buy shares from or sell shares to.

## The four fights

### Founders in conflict with each other

- As a director, don't pick a founder; pick what serves the company. Write down why.
- Look first at the documents: who sits on the board, what a deadlock provision says, and each founder's vesting.
- Colorado corporations have a court route for deadlock and oppression, with a buyout election ([C.R.S. 7-114-301, 7-114-305][t7]).
  Delaware has a custodian route for deadlock ([8 Del. C. 226][d8s7]). A Colorado LLC can be dissolved by a court when
  it isn't reasonably practicable to carry on under the agreement ([C.R.S. 7-80-810][t7]).

#### Who owns the code and inventions

- A copyright transfer, other than by operation of law, needs a writing signed by the owner ([17 U.S.C. 204(a)][us204]),
  and a patent is assigned by a written instrument ([35 U.S.C. 261][us261]), so a founder who never signed an assignment
  may still own what they wrote or invented.
- For copyright, an employee's work within the scope of employment is a work made for hire, owned by the company unless
  both signed a writing saying otherwise; a contractor's is one only in listed kinds of commissioned work, with a signed
  agreement ([17 U.S.C. 101][us101], [201(b)][us201]).
- Joint authors co-own the copyright in a joint work ([17 U.S.C. 201(a)][us201]). Each co-owner of a patent may make, use
  and sell the invention without the others' consent and without accounting to them, unless they agreed otherwise
  ([35 U.S.C. 262][us262]). Without an assignment to the company, a departing co-founder may keep using what both built.
- Claims arising under the patent and copyright acts, such as infringement, go to federal court; a state court can't hear
  them ([28 U.S.C. 1338(a)][us1338]). A dispute over who owns a patent or copyright under a contract can be a state-law claim (judge-made).
- Time limits: a copyright claim within three years after it accrued ([17 U.S.C. 507(b)][us507]); a trade secret claim
  within three years after the misappropriation is discovered, or should have been, under the federal act and in Colorado
  and Delaware ([18 U.S.C. 1836(d)][us1836]; [C.R.S. 7-74-107][t7]; [6 Del. C. 2006][d6c20]). Patent damages reach
  back no more than six years before suit ([35 U.S.C. 286][us286]). Who owns what, and when a clock started, are for a lawyer.

The law's own words, read 2026-11-06:

| What | The law says | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Joint authors | "The authors of a joint work are coowners of copyright in the work." | [17 U.S.C. 201(a)][us201] |
| Joint patent owners | "each of the joint owners of a patent may make, use, offer to sell, or sell the patented invention ... without the consent of and without accounting to the other owners" | [35 U.S.C. 262][us262] |
| Copyright claim | "within three years after the claim accrued" | [17 U.S.C. 507(b)][us507] |
| Federal trade secret claim | "may not be commenced later than 3 years after the date on which the misappropriation ... is discovered or by the exercise of reasonable diligence should have been discovered" | [18 U.S.C. 1836(d)][us1836] |
| Colorado trade secret claim | "within three years after the misappropriation is discovered or by the exercise of reasonable diligence should have been discovered" | [C.R.S. 7-74-107][t7] |
| Delaware trade secret claim | "within 3 years after the misappropriation is discovered or by the exercise of reasonable diligence should have been discovered" | [6 Del. C. 2006][d6c20] |

### A founder removed

| Step | Who decides | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Removing the founder as an officer | The board, unless the bylaws say otherwise | Colorado: the board may remove an officer, and removal doesn't affect any contract rights ([C.R.S. 7-108-303, 7-108-304][t7]). Delaware: as the bylaws or board provide ([8 Del. C. 142][d8s4]) |
| Removing the founder as a director | The stockholders, as the statute and the voting agreement allow | Colorado: at a meeting called for that purpose, by more votes for than against; removal by written consent isn't explicit ([C.R.S. 7-108-108][t7]). Delaware: majority of voting shares, possibly by written consent ([141(k)][d8s4], [228][d8s7]) |
| Ending employment | The company | A Colorado worker's final wages are due on discharge, with penalties for late payment after a written demand ([C.R.S. 8-4-109][t8]); an individual owning or controlling 25% can be an "employer" ([8-4-101(6)][t8]) |
| Buying back unvested shares | The company, under the founder stock agreement | Follow the agreement's notice and timing exactly. Whether firing someone to trigger a repurchase breaches good faith is judge-made |
| Whether it was all valid | A court, if challenged | Delaware: the Court of Chancery decides the validity of director and officer elections and removals ([8 Del. C. 225][d8s7]). Colorado: no equivalent section; a court can order a meeting ([C.R.S. 7-107-103][t7]) |

What you owe: a director must act in good faith on an informed basis. Follow the notice rules in the bylaws and
statute, give the founder a chance to respond where the documents require it, and keep a record.

A removed founder who was an employee may claim discrimination. In Colorado the charge goes to the Civil Rights
Commission first, and court comes after. The law's words, read 2026-11-07:

| Step | The law says | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| The charge | "must be filed with the commission ... within three hundred days after the alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practice occurred, and if a charge is not timely filed, it is barred" | [C.R.S. 24-34-403][t24] |
| Commission first | No court action "without first exhausting the proceedings and remedies available to the person under this part 3", with a narrow exception for ill health | [C.R.S. 24-34-306(14)][t24] |
| Then court | "The complainant must file a civil action within ninety days after the date upon which the jurisdiction of the commission ceased." | [C.R.S. 24-34-306][t24] |

### Acting on a founder's bad behaviour

1. **Preserve first.** The duty to keep evidence can start once a dispute is reasonably anticipated. Federal courts can
   sanction lost electronic records ([Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)][frcp]); Delaware's Court of Chancery reaches reckless loss
   ([Chancery Rule 37(e)][chan37]) and publishes [guidelines for preserving electronic information][chanesi]; destroying
   records to obstruct a federal matter is a crime ([18 U.S.C. 1519][us1519]).
2. **Investigate through the board,** ideally a committee of directors without a conflict, with counsel who represents
   the company.
3. **Use your information rights, not self-help.** Request what you're entitled to ([C.R.S. 7-116-102][t7];
   [8 Del. C. 220][d8s7]). In Delaware the demand is in writing and under oath, made in good faith for a proper purpose,
   names with reasonable particularity the records sought and why, and asks only for records specifically related to
   that purpose ([220(b)(2)c][d8s7]); if the company refuses or doesn't reply within
   5 business days, the stockholder may ask the Court of Chancery to order it ([220(b), (c)][d8s7]). Don't take company
   documents you're not entitled to hold; trade secret law limits it
   ([18 U.S.C. 1836][us1836]), with a narrow protection for confidential disclosure to a government official or a lawyer
   to report a suspected violation ([1833(b)][us1833]).
4. **Anyone may report to a regulator.** A confidentiality agreement can't stop it ([17 CFR 240.21F-17][e21f17]).
5. **Decide on the record.** A finding of "cause" should follow the definition in the founder's agreements, with the
   steps those agreements require.
6. **Know the time limit.** In Delaware, most claims on a debt not evidenced by a record or an instrument under seal,
   on a promise or on a statute must be brought within 3 years from the accruing of the claim. A written contract of at least
   $100,000 may set a longer period, if suit is brought within 20 years from accrual ([10 Del. C. 8106][d10c81]). What
   counts as accrual, and how a court of equity treats delay, are for a lawyer.

### A control contest

If you are an investor seeking control, or one of a group:

- Use only the routes your documents and the statute give you: written consent where allowed, a special meeting, the
  drag-along, the protective provisions. In a Colorado corporation, written consent needs every shareholder unless the
  articles allow less ([C.R.S. 7-107-104][t7]); in Delaware, the holders of the votes needed can act by consent with
  prompt notice to the rest afterward ([8 Del. C. 228][d8s7]).
- If you or your group become a controller, transactions you're on both sides of (an insider financing, a squeeze-out, a
  sale where you get something others don't) carry fiduciary risk. Delaware's safe harbours set out how such transactions
  can be approved ([8 Del. C. 144][d8s4]); beyond them, review is judge-made.
- Under Delaware's 2025 text, a "controlling stockholder" includes anyone with a contractual right to elect a majority
  of the board, and a holder of at least a third of the voting power with managerial authority ([8 Del. C. 144][d8s4]).
  An investor whose voting agreement lets it name a board majority should assume it is a controller.
- In Colorado, illegal, oppressive or fraudulent conduct by those in control is a ground for court dissolution, and the
  company or other shareholders may elect to buy the petitioner's shares instead ([C.R.S. 7-114-301, 7-114-305][t7]).

If other investors are seeking control and you aren't, check your consent and approval rights, your seat's removal
protection, and your information rights, and get your own lawyer.

## What you owe other investors

- **Generally no fiduciary duty** among investors who don't control the company, as a matter of judge-made law. Your
  duties are in your contracts: the voting agreement, the investors' rights agreement, side letters and their most
  favoured nation terms.
- **Confidentiality.** Information shared among investors under an investors' rights agreement stays within its terms.
- **Fair dealing when you trade.** If you buy shares from another investor or a founder, or sell to them, securities
  fraud rules apply to private shares: no false statements and no misleading half-truths ([15 U.S.C. 78j(b)][us78j];
  [17 CFR 240.10b-5][e10b5]; [C.R.S. 11-51-501, 11-51-604][t11]). A person who controls a violator can be liable too
  ([15 U.S.C. 78t(a)][us78t]). Whether silence alone creates a duty to speak is judge-made. Colorado's time limits: a claim
  that a sale was unregistered or the seller unlicensed must be brought within 2 years after the contract of sale; no one
  may bring a fraud or misstatement claim "more than three years after the discovery of the facts giving rise to a cause
  of action" or after they should have been discovered with reasonable diligence, "and in no event more than five years
  after the purchase or sale" ([C.R.S. 11-51-604(8)][t11], read 2026-11-07).
- **Insider rounds.** If you lead a round only insiders are offered, other investors may claim unfair treatment. Offer
  everyone their pro rata share where you can, and use a disinterested approval.

  1. Any notice, consent, resolution or demand received, with dates (they start clocks).
  2. The charter or articles with amendments, and the bylaws or operating agreement.
  3. Your investment documents: purchase agreement, voting agreement, investors' rights agreement, side letters.
  4. Your board materials and minutes, if you are a director.
  5. Your own communications with the founders and other investors, unedited.
  6. The company's insurance policies for directors and officers; notice terms can be strict.

  give insurers any required notice.

## Drafting notes

### Choice: acting as a director or stepping back

**Recommendation:** if your fund's interests and the company's diverge sharply, consider recusing from the specific
decision, or resigning the seat, rather than acting in a conflicted role. **Where a lawyer should look:** whether the
decision needs a disinterested approval, and how recusal affects the board's quorum.

### Choice: going to court

**Recommendation:** try the documents' routes first (information requests, a board meeting, a demand). **Where a lawyer
should look:** which court and law apply (a Delaware corporation working in Colorado fights under Delaware corporate law
([C.R.S. 7-90-805(4)][t7])), any forum clause, and deadlines.
