# Angel group charter and membership agreement

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.** Have your own lawyer look before the group
adopts these.

## In one screen

A group needs two documents: a **charter** (or bylaws, or an LLC agreement if the group is an LLC) that says what the
group is and how it decides things, and a **membership agreement** each member signs. The two together should make
plain that the group is a forum where members decide for themselves, not a fund, broker or adviser, unless the group has
deliberately chosen to run a vehicle (see [How members invest](how-members-invest.md)). The clauses below are written
for a group whose members invest individually.

Rule 148 defines an "angel investor group" as accredited investors who hold regular meetings, have "defined
processes and procedures for making investment decisions, either individually or among the membership of the group as a
whole", and are not associated or affiliated with
brokers, dealers or investment advisers ([17 CFR 230.148][e148]). A group that wants to host demo days under that rule
should build its charter to meet the definition.

## First choice: the group's legal form

| Form | What it means | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| No entity (an informal network) | Cheapest. But two or more people carrying on as co-owners a business for profit form a partnership whether or not they mean to, and partners are jointly and severally liable ([C.R.S. 7-64-202, 7-64-306][t7]) | Small groups that only meet, with members investing on their own |
| Colorado unincorporated nonprofit association | A separate entity; a member isn't liable just by being a member ([C.R.S. 7-30-106][t7]). The act covers only associations with a nonprofit purpose ([7-30-101][t7]); a group whose members pool money or co-invest for profit falls outside it and can be a partnership | A group with a nonprofit purpose (education, meetings) and no investing through the group |
| Colorado nonprofit corporation | Formal; may not make distributions to members except as the act allows ([C.R.S. 7-133-101][t7]) | Education and meetings; never a vehicle for gains |
| Colorado LLC | Members and managers aren't liable for the LLC's debts as such ([C.R.S. 7-80-705][t7]); the operating agreement can restrict or eliminate duties only if not manifestly unreasonable ([7-80-108(1.5)][t7]) | A group that collects dues, hires staff or may later manage vehicles |
| Delaware LLC | Broadest freedom to shape duties by agreement ([6 Del. C. 18-1101][d18s11]); needs Colorado authority to do business here | A group expecting a Delaware series structure |

**Recommendation:** a Colorado LLC managed by a small board or manager, for any group that collects dues or signs
contracts. It gives liability protection, Colorado home-state simplicity and room to grow. A group that only meets and
never handles money can stay informal or use an unincorporated nonprofit association. Tax-exempt status (for example as
a business league) limits activities and is a question for your own tax professional.

**Where a lawyer should look:** the partnership risk of an informal group that shares costs or co-invests; tax-exempt
status; whether the group's activities need any Colorado licence.

## Charter outline

Each heading is a section of the charter. Fill-in text follows where the wording matters.

1. **Name and form.** `{{group_name}}`, a `{{group_entity}}`.
2. **Purpose.**
   > The purpose of the Group is `{{purpose}}`. The Group provides a forum for accredited investors to learn about,
   > review and discuss early-stage companies. Each member decides independently whether to invest. The Group does not
   > invest on behalf of members, hold members' money or securities, recommend investments to any member, or negotiate
   > for any member, and nobody is paid by the Group or by a company in connection with any member's purchase or sale of
   > securities.
3. **Members.** Who may join (accredited investors only); how applications are approved; categories (individual,
   entity, guest); resignation; removal (for breach of the membership agreement, non-payment, or conduct harmful to the
   Group, by a stated vote after notice and a chance to respond).
4. **Governance.** Who runs the group: `{{manager_or_board}}`. Officers and terms; how decisions are made (quorum,
   majority); records and minutes.
5. **Deal process.** Refer to the written [deal screening process](deal-screening.md), and state that it is the Group's
   defined process for evaluating investments.
6. **Meetings.** How often (Rule 148 needs regular meetings); who may attend; guests and the guest declaration; online
   attendance limits for demo days.
7. **Money.** Dues: `{{dues}}`. Company fees: `{{company_fee}}`.
   > No dues, fees or other money received by the Group will be used to pay any person a commission, bonus or other
   > payment that depends on whether a company raises money or whether any member invests. Any fee charged to a company
   > is a flat fee stated in advance, the same for every applicant, and is not refunded or increased based on the outcome.
8. **Conflicts.** Adopt the [conflicts of interest policy](conflicts-policy.md).
9. **Confidentiality.** Members keep company and member information confidential (see the membership agreement).
10. **No advice, no fiduciary duty between members.**
    > Nothing said or shared at a Group meeting or in a Group channel is investment advice from the Group or any member.
    > No member owes any other member a fiduciary duty because of their membership. Members who share diligence work do
    > so as a courtesy, without any promise that it is complete or correct.
11. **Vehicles.** State that the Group does not form or manage investment vehicles, or, if it does, that each vehicle is
    governed by its own documents, managed by a named manager, and offered only under its own securities exemption.
12. **Liability and indemnification.** Limit volunteer officers' liability to the extent the group's form allows, and
    indemnify them for good-faith acts.
13. **Amendment and dissolution.** By a stated member vote; on dissolution, remaining funds go to stated purposes (for a
    nonprofit, not to members).

## Membership agreement

> **Membership agreement**
>
> {{member_name}} (the "Member")*[, investing through {{member_entity}}]* applies to join {{group_name}} (the "Group") and
> agrees as follows.
>
> **1. Accredited status.** The Member is an accredited investor as defined in Rule 501(a) of Regulation D, under the
> category stated in the Member's certification, and will tell the Group promptly if that stops being true. The Member
> understands that a company selling under Rule 506(c) must verify the Member's status itself, and may ask for more than
> this agreement.
>
> **2. Independent decisions.** The Member makes every investment decision independently, after the Member's own review
> and with the Member's own advisers. The Group is not the Member's investment adviser, broker or fiduciary. Neither the
> Group nor any other member recommends any investment to the Member.
>
> **3. No pay for introductions.** The Member will not accept any payment from a company presenting to the Group, or from
> anyone else, for introducing members or for any member's investment, unless the Member first discloses it in writing to
> the Group and the Group's [board / manager] approves it.
>
> **4. Confidentiality.** The Member will keep confidential, and use only to evaluate a possible investment, all
> non-public information about companies and other members received through the Group. This does not apply to
> information that is public through no fault of the Member, or that the law requires the Member to disclose. Nothing in
> this agreement stops the Member from reporting a possible violation of law to, or communicating with, a government
> agency, including the staff of the Securities and Exchange Commission.
>
> **5. Conflicts.** The Member will follow the Group's conflicts of interest policy, including disclosing any interest in
> a company before it presents, and stepping out of screening and diligence decisions where the policy requires.
>
> **6. Dues.** The Member will pay dues of {{dues}}, which are not refundable.
>
> **7. Conduct.** The Member will treat founders, other members and guests professionally, will not solicit investors
> for any company in the Group's meetings except as the Group's process allows, and will not hold out the Group as having
> approved or endorsed any company.
>
> **8. Resignation and removal.** The Member may resign at any time by written notice. The Group may remove the Member as
> its charter provides. Sections 4 and 5 continue after membership ends.
>
> **9. Information about the Member.** The Member agrees that the Group may keep the Member's contact details and
> accreditation records, and share the Member's name and contact details with companies only when the Member asks.
>
> **10. General.** This agreement is governed by the laws of {{governing_law}}. It is the whole agreement on these
> subjects, together with the Group's charter and policies as amended from time to time.
>
> Member: ____________________  Date: ________
>
> For the Group: ____________________  Date: ________

## Drafting notes

### Choice: member-led or manager-led decisions

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| Members decide individually; a volunteer committee screens | Keeps the group furthest from adviser status |
| A paid manager screens and presents deals | Ask whether the manager is "in the business" of advising for pay ([15 U.S.C. 80b-2(a)(11)][us80b2]) |
| A committee decides for a pooled vehicle | The vehicle and its manager need the full analysis in [How members invest](how-members-invest.md) |

**Recommendation:** members decide individually, with a volunteer or cost-reimbursed screening committee. **Where a
lawyer should look:** any pay to the person who runs screening.

### Choice: charging companies to present

**Recommendation:** charge nothing, or a small flat fee that covers actual costs, stated in advance, the same for all,
and unrelated to the outcome. Under the platform carve-out, any compensation in connection with a sale removes the
protection ([15 U.S.C. 77d(c)][us77d]), and at a Rule 148 demo day the sponsor may charge attendees only reasonable
administrative fees and take no pay for introductions ([17 CFR 230.148][e148]). **Where a lawyer should look:** any
company fee at all.

### Choice: the accredited statement

**Recommendation:** have every member sign the self-certification in
[Accredited investor rules](../angels/accredited-investor.md) on joining and each year after. Tell members that a
company using Rule 506(c) will verify for itself ([17 CFR 230.506(c)][e506]).

### Choice: confidentiality

**Recommendation:** keep the carve-out for reports to government agencies. A confidentiality promise can't be used to
stop someone talking to SEC staff ([17 CFR 240.21F-17][e21f17]).
