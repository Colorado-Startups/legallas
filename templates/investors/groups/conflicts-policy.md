# Conflicts of interest policy

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.**

## In one screen

Members of an angel group often know the founders, advise other startups, or invest in competitors. That is normal. What
matters is that it is disclosed before it can affect what other members see or decide. This policy requires disclosure,
sets when a member steps out, bans undisclosed pay from companies, and adds stricter rules for anyone who runs a vehicle
for other members.

Two kinds of law sit behind it. If the group is a Colorado LLC, its managers (and members, if member-managed) owe the
group duties to account for benefits and not to deal adversely ([C.R.S. 7-80-404][t7]). If anyone in the group is an
investment adviser, including a vehicle manager, the Advisers Act's anti-fraud section applies to them whether or not
they are registered ([15 U.S.C. 80b-6][us80b6]), and a pooled vehicle's manager may not make misleading statements to the
vehicle's investors ([17 CFR 275.206(4)-8][e206-8]).

## The policy

> **{{group_name}}: Conflicts of interest policy**
>
> Adopted {{adopted_date}}.
>
> **1. Who it covers.** Every member, guest, officer, committee member, employee and contractor of the Group, and any
> manager of an investment vehicle connected with the Group ("Covered Persons").
>
> **2. What is an interest.** A Covered Person has an interest in a company if the Covered Person, or their spouse,
> partner, child, parent or sibling, or an entity any of them controls:
>
> (a) owns, or has a right to acquire, securities of the company or of a direct competitor;
>
> (b) is a founder, director, officer, employee, adviser, consultant or lender of the company;
>
> (c) has been offered or receives any payment, equity or other benefit from the company, or from anyone else, in
> connection with the company's fundraising; or
>
> (d) has any other relationship that a reasonable member would want to know about before relying on the Covered
> Person's views of the company.
>
> **3. Disclosure.** A Covered Person discloses each interest in writing to the {{policy_officer}}:
>
> (a) on joining, and each year in the yearly statement below;
>
> (b) before taking part in any screening, presentation or diligence of the company; and
>
> (c) promptly when a new interest arises.
>
> The {{policy_officer}} keeps a register of disclosed interests. Interests relating to a company are shared with members
> considering that company.
>
> **4. Stepping out.** A Covered Person with an interest in a company:
>
> (a) does not vote on whether the company moves forward in screening;
>
> (b) does not lead diligence on the company or write the diligence summary;
>
> (c) may answer questions about the company, after stating the interest; and
>
> (d) may invest in the company on the same terms as other members.
>
> **5. No undisclosed pay.** No Covered Person may accept any payment, equity or other benefit from a company, or from
> anyone else, for introducing the company to the Group or its members, or that depends on any member investing, unless
> it is disclosed under section 3 and approved in writing by the {{policy_officer}} after considering whether it requires
> a broker or adviser licence. The Group itself accepts no such payment.
>
> **6. Leads and diligence members.** A member who leads diligence or presents a company discloses whether they plan to
> invest, how much, and on what terms, including any side letter or term not offered to other members.
>
> **7. Vehicle managers.** A manager of a vehicle that invests members' money must also:
>
> (a) disclose in the vehicle's documents, before anyone subscribes, every fee, carried interest and other payment the
> manager or its affiliates receive;
>
> (b) disclose any other vehicle or account the manager runs that invests in the same company, and how opportunities
> are divided between them;
>
> (c) not buy from or sell to the vehicle, or cause it to deal with the manager's affiliates, without disclosure to and
> consent from the vehicle's investors as its documents require; and
>
> (d) tell the {{policy_officer}} promptly of any event that would disqualify the vehicle from Rule 506 under Rule
> 506(d).
>
> **8. Confidential information.** A Covered Person may not use information received through the Group to trade in
> securities of a public company, or to compete with or harm a company that presented.
>
> **9. Breach.** The Group may suspend or remove a Covered Person who breaks this policy.
>
> **10. Questions.** The {{policy_officer}} decides questions under this policy and records the decision. A Covered
> Person who is unsure should disclose.

## Drafting notes

### Choice: step out, or disclose and stay

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| Disclose and stay in everything | Simple; members may discount the conflicted person's views |
| Disclose and step out of votes and diligence leadership (as drafted) | Balanced; keeps knowledge available |
| Disclose and leave the room | Strict; loses the most informed voice |

**Recommendation:** as drafted. **Where a lawyer should look:** if the group is an LLC, whether the operating agreement
restricts managers' duties within what Colorado allows: not manifestly unreasonable ([C.R.S. 7-80-108(1.5)][t7]).

### Choice: what counts as a family interest

**Recommendation:** spouse or partner, children, parents and siblings, as drafted. Wider definitions are hard to apply.

### Choice: vehicle managers

**Recommendation:** keep section 7 even if the group has no vehicles yet, so the rule is in place before one starts.
**Where a lawyer should look:** the vehicle's own documents, which must carry these disclosures, and allocation between
vehicles investing in the same company.
