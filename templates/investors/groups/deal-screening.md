# Deal screening process

**Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything. No lawyer has reviewed this.**

## In one screen

A written screening process does three jobs: it gives members better deals, it records who decided what (useful if a
deal goes wrong), and it is part of what Rule 148 means by an angel investor group's "defined processes and procedures for
making investment decisions" ([17 CFR 230.148][e148]). The same definition requires a group of accredited investors
that holds regular meetings and is neither associated nor affiliated with brokers, dealers or investment advisers. A
manager paid as an adviser to a group vehicle may put that status at risk; ask your lawyer before relying on Rule 148
then. The process below is for a group whose members invest individually. Every step keeps the group as a forum, not a broker or adviser: the group screens and shares information;
each member decides.

## The stages

| Stage | Who | What happens | Record kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Application | The company | Submits a short application through the group's intake form or deal room | Application and date |
| 2. First screen | `{{screening_committee}}` | Checks fit with `{{criteria}}`; conflicts declared; a few companies move on | Screening notes, conflicts list, decision |
| 3. Screening meeting | Committee and company | A short pitch and questions | Attendance, decision on whether to present |
| 4. Presentation | Members and company | Full pitch at a members' meeting | Attendance, show of interest (not a commitment) |
| 5. Diligence | `{{diligence_lead}}` and interested members | Review in the company's own data room; a written summary shared with interested members | Diligence checklist and summary, with its limits |
| 6. Terms | The company's lead investor, or members individually | Members learn the terms; each decides | Terms received |
| 7. Closing | Each investing member and the company | Each member signs the company's documents directly | Who invested (kept by each member; the group may record it for statistics) |
| 8. Follow-up | A member who agreed to watch the company | Shares the company's updates with investing members, if the company allows | Updates received |

## Checklists for each stage

### 1. Application

- [ ] The intake form asks for: company name and entity, state, stage, amount raising, instrument, the securities
  exemption it is using (Rule 506(b) or 506(c)), use of money, team, and whether anyone is paid to raise money for it.
- [ ] Company fee: `{{company_fee}}`. If any, it is flat, published, the same for all, and owed whatever the outcome.
- [ ] The company is told that applying doesn't mean the group endorses it, and that members invest individually.

### 2. First screen

- [ ] Every committee member declares conflicts before discussing the company (see the
  [conflicts policy](conflicts-policy.md)). Conflicted members step out.
- [ ] Fit with the criteria.
- [ ] Quick public record checks on the company and founders, recorded with the date. A blocked check is "couldn't
  check", not "nothing found". See [What each side checks](../angels/what-each-side-checks.md).
- [ ] If the company uses Rule 506(b), it may not use general solicitation ([17 CFR 230.502(c)][e502]). The group should
  share its details only with members, not publicly. If it uses Rule 506(c), public posting is allowed, but the company
  must verify every buyer.

### 3 and 4. Meetings

- [ ] Members-only meetings are limited to members and declared guests.
- [ ] If the event is public or advertised (a demo day), check Rule 148's conditions first: more than one company; no
  advertising of a specific offering; the group gives no investment advice and doesn't negotiate for members; only
  reasonable administrative fees from attendees; the companies say only that they are raising, the type and amount of securities, the use of proceeds and the
  unsubscribed amount; online
  attendance limited as the rule says ([17 CFR 230.148][e148]).
- [ ] The chair reminds attendees that nothing said is advice and each decides independently.

### 5. Diligence

- [ ] Work in the company's own data room. Nothing is copied out without the company's consent.
- [ ] The diligence summary is labelled: prepared by volunteer members, not complete, not advice, each member must do
  their own review.
- [ ] Topics covered: corporate records and capitalization; founder vesting and invention assignments; earlier financings
  and their rights; Form D filings for earlier rounds; customers and contracts; financials and spending; market;
  claims; and the fight clauses in [What your documents should say beforehand](../fights/before-the-fight.md).
- [ ] The diligence lead declares any conflict and any plan to invest.

### 6 and 7. Terms and closing

- [ ] If a member negotiates terms, they negotiate as an investor for themselves (or as the round's lead), not for the
  group. Negotiating for other members, or being paid to, pushes toward broker and adviser status.
- [ ] Each member signs directly with the company. The group never holds investors' money.
- [ ] If members want to invest together through a vehicle, stop and use
  [How members invest](how-members-invest.md) first.

## Drafting notes

### Choice: how much the group does for members

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| Screening and meetings only | Lightest; members do their own diligence |
| Shared diligence summary | Most groups' value; label its limits clearly |
| A lead negotiates for the group | Raises broker and adviser questions; avoid unless the lead is the round's lead investor acting for themselves |

**Recommendation:** screen, host meetings and share a labelled diligence summary. Let the company's own lead investor
negotiate terms. **Where a lawyer should look:** any role in which one person acts for the group in a deal, and any pay
connected with it ([15 U.S.C. 77d(c)][us77d], [80b-2(a)(11)][us80b2]).

### Choice: posting deals online

**Recommendation:** keep deal details behind a members-only login unless the company confirms in writing that it is
using Rule 506(c). **Where a lawyer should look:** whether the group's own website or newsletter could be general
solicitation for a company using Rule 506(b).
