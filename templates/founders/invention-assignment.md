# Invention Assignment and Confidentiality Agreement

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

Without this, a founder or employee can own what they built. Copyright belongs to the author unless a work is made for hire or transferred in a signed
writing ([17 U.S.C. 201, 204(a)][us17]), and patent rights start with the inventor and transfer by a written instrument ([35 U.S.C. 261][us35]). Investors look
for this document.

- **Everyone who creates anything for the company signs it:** founders, employees and contractors, before they start or receive equity.
- **Founders also complete Part A,** which moves what they built before the company existed.
- **Check prior employers first.** A founder's old employment agreement may claim what the founder built, especially if it happened during that job or with its
  equipment. This assignment can't transfer what the founder doesn't own.
- **Contractors:** use it with care. Software by a contractor usually isn't a work made for hire, so the present assignment in section 3 does the work. Also, this doesn't decide
  whether someone is properly a contractor.

## The document

<!-- TEMPLATE START -->

**INVENTION ASSIGNMENT AND CONFIDENTIALITY AGREEMENT**

This Agreement is between {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a {{COMPANY_STATE_AND_TYPE}} (the "Company"), and {{PERSON_NAME}} ("you"), who works or will work for the Company as {{PERSON_ROLE}}.
It takes effect on {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}. "**Service**" means your work for the Company as an employee, officer or contractor. Serving only as a director isn't Service, though your duties as a director continue.

**Part A. What you built before**

[OPTION A: You are a founder, or you are bringing earlier work. Recommended for founders.]
**1. Assignment of prior work.** You hereby assign to the Company all your right, title and interest throughout the world in the work described in Schedule 1, and any other work you made before
your Service for the business the Company will carry on, other than what Schedule 2 lists ("Assigned Prior Work"), including all copyrights, patent rights, trade secrets, trademarks and domain names in it, and the right to sue for past infringement. You shall deliver all code, documents,
accounts, credentials and other materials for it within 10 days after signing. You make this assignment as a condition of receiving your stock or other interest in the Company. You represent that you own the Assigned Prior Work, apart from anything Schedule 3 discloses.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: You are not bringing any earlier work.]
**1. No prior work.** You are not assigning any earlier work.
[END OPTION B]

**2. What you keep.** Schedule 2 lists work you made before your Service and aren't assigning ("Excluded Prior Work"). If you use Excluded Prior Work in anything you do for the Company, or
if a Company product needs it, you grant the Company a non-exclusive, worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence, with the right to sublicense through several tiers, to use, copy, change and distribute it as part of
the Company's products and services.

**Part B. What you create while working for the Company**

**3. Assignment of Company Inventions.** You hereby assign to the Company all your right, title and interest throughout the world in every invention, work of authorship, design, software, data,
improvement, trade secret and other creation that you make, alone or with others, during your Service, and that:

(a) relates to the Company's business or its actual or demonstrably planned research or development;

(b) results from work you do for the Company; or

(c) uses the Company's equipment, supplies, facilities, money or confidential information,

(together, "**Company Inventions**"). You keep everything else. Something you make entirely on your own time, without any of the Company's equipment, supplies, facilities, money or confidential
information, that meets none of (a) to (c), is yours.

**4. Works made for hire.** Copyrightable work you create within the scope of your employment is a work made for hire, owned by the Company from creation. Section 3 covers whatever isn't.

**5. Keeping the Company informed.** During your Service, you shall promptly tell the Company about each Company Invention and keep accurate records of it. This section doesn't require you to disclose anything
that belongs to you under section 3, except as needed to determine whether it does, and the Company shall keep that information confidential.

**6. Helping to protect it.** During and after your Service, you shall sign documents and give reasonable help to register, protect and enforce rights in Assigned Prior Work and Company Inventions. After
your Service ends, the Company shall pay you a reasonable hourly rate and your expenses for this help. If the Company can't get your signature after reasonable effort, you appoint the Company as your attorney-in-fact for
the sole purpose of signing and filing those documents. To the extent the law allows, you waive and agree not to assert moral rights in Assigned Prior Work and Company Inventions.

**Part C. Confidential information**

**7. What's confidential.** "**Confidential Information**" means non-public information of the Company, or held by the Company in confidence for others, relating to its business, products, technology, customers,
finances, plans or people, that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential, including its trade secrets. It does **not** include:

(a) information that arises from your general training, knowledge, skill or experience, whether you gained it on the job or otherwise;

(b) information that is readily ascertainable to the public; or

(c) information you otherwise have a right to disclose as legally protected conduct.

**8. Your duty.** During your Service and afterward, as long as the information stays confidential, you shall use Confidential Information only to do your work for the Company and shall not disclose it to anyone except as
your work requires or the Company allows. Trade secrets stay protected for as long as they remain trade secrets. For other Confidential Information, this duty ends {{CONFIDENTIALITY_YEARS}} years after your Service ends.

**9. Other people's information.** You won't bring to the Company, use or disclose anything confidential belonging to a former employer or anyone else without permission. Schedule 3 lists any agreements you've signed that
may limit your work for the Company or claim rights in what you create. You represent that, apart from Schedule 3, nothing prevents you from signing and performing this Agreement.

**Part D. Your rights**

**10. What this Agreement does not limit.** Nothing in this Agreement or any other agreement or policy of the Company:

(a) stops you from reporting a possible violation of law to, filing a charge with, communicating directly with, or giving information or testimony to any government agency, including the Securities and Exchange Commission,
without notifying the Company;

(b) stops you from disclosing or discussing, orally or in writing, any alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practice, including with your family, a religious advisor, a medical or mental health provider, a support group, a
lawyer, a financial advisor or a tax preparer, or in response to legal process;

(c) stops you from inquiring about, disclosing, comparing or otherwise discussing your wages or wage rate, or stops an employee from discussing wages, hours or working conditions or engaging in other activity protected by section 7 of
the National Labor Relations Act;

(d) stops you from talking to your own lawyer, or from giving truthful testimony; or

(e) restricts you from competing with the Company or working for anyone else after your Service ends, as long as you keep your obligations in Parts A to C.

**11. Notice of immunity under the Defend Trade Secrets Act.** An individual shall not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for the disclosure of a trade secret that (A) is made (i) in confidence
to a federal, state or local government official, either directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, and (ii) solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law; or (B) is made in a complaint or other document filed
in a lawsuit or other proceeding, if such filing is made under seal. An individual who files a lawsuit for retaliation by an employer for reporting a suspected violation of law may disclose the trade secret to the attorney of the individual and
use the trade secret information in the court proceeding, if the individual (A) files any document containing the trade secret under seal and (B) does not disclose the trade secret, except pursuant to court order.

**Part E. When you leave, and general terms**

**12. When your Service ends.** You shall return Company property and Confidential Information, and shall tell the Company of any you can't. Delete a copy only after the Company confirms in writing, and don't delete anything while a dispute is reasonably foreseeable. You may keep copies of: this Agreement; your stock, option, and vesting documents; your pay,
benefit and tax records; and any other record of your own rights that doesn't contain trade secrets. The Company shall not condition your final pay on any of this.

**13. Remedies.** A breach of Parts A to C could cause harm that money doesn't fix, so the Company may ask a court for an injunction, in addition to other remedies. This Agreement has no liquidated damages.

**14. General.** This Agreement doesn't promise any period of Service. It's governed by the laws of {{GOVERNING_STATE}}, without its conflict of law rules. It binds your heirs and the Company's successors and assigns, and the Company may assign it to a successor
to its business. If a court finds any term too broad, it should narrow that term to the minimum extent needed, and the rest stands. Only a signed writing can amend it. Parts A, C, D and E, and sections 3 to 6 for anything created during your Service, survive after your Service ends. It may be
signed in counterparts and electronically.

Signed on {{SIGNING_DATE}}.

{{COMPANY_NAME}}

By: ______________________________

______________________________
{{PERSON_NAME}}

**Schedule 1. Assigned Prior Work** (founders): {{PRIOR_WORK_DESCRIPTION}}

**Schedule 2. Excluded Prior Work:** {{EXCLUDED_PRIOR_WORK}}. If this says "none", you confirm there's nothing you're holding back.

**Schedule 3. Other agreements:** {{PRIOR_OBLIGATIONS}}

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Hereby assign" | Present assignment; a promise to assign later | **Present assignment, as drafted.** A promise to assign may leave title with the person until they act, and a rival present assignment can win. That wording divided the parties in the Federal Circuit decision that the Supreme Court reviewed in [Stanford v. Roche][stanford], though the Supreme Court decided a different question. Founders should also sign Part A on day one, with Schedule 1 listing real things: repository names, domains, designs | Recording patent assignments ([35 U.S.C. 261][us35]) |
| Scope of what's assigned | As drafted, tied to the business or to company resources; everything created during Service | **As drafted.** Colorado has no statute limiting employee invention assignments that this search found, unlike some other states, but an everything clause invites a fight, and it can catch a side project unrelated to the business. Investors want a clean chain of title on the business, and this gives it | A person who keeps a job elsewhere while starting up |
| Trailing clause | None, as drafted; assign inventions for a period after leaving | **None.** A trailing clause reaches work for a next employer and starts to look like a restraint on working, which Colorado disfavors ([8-2-113(2)][t8]). Section 3 already captures what was made during Service | Whether a trailing clause could ever be enforceable here |
| Confidentiality exceptions | Keep (a) to (c); remove | **Keep.** Colorado protects only a reasonable confidentiality term that doesn't prohibit disclosure of general training, knowledge, skill or experience, information readily ascertainable to the public, or information a worker has a right to disclose as legally protected conduct ([8-2-113(3)(b)][t8]). The text tracks those words. Violating Colorado's limits can bring penalties | **Whether a clause broader than this becomes a void covenant,** and penalties in [8-2-113(8)][t8] |
| Length of the duty | Stated end date for non-trade-secret information; none | **State one, with trade secrets lasting as long as they qualify.** Unlimited duties on ordinary business information are harder to defend as reasonable. Fill in a period in section 8: five years is a common outer limit, and it's a judgment call | How long is reasonable for your information |
| Discrimination and harassment carve-out | Carve out, as drafted; sign a separate compliant nondisclosure | **Carve out.** Since August 7, 2023, a provision limiting an employee's disclosure or discussion of an alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practice is void unless it meets a list of conditions, including a signed addendum. A violation carries actual damages and a $5,000 penalty ([24-34-407][t24]). Section 10(b) takes those matters out of this Agreement, so it isn't that kind of provision | Whether the carve-out is enough, and how any settlement agreement should handle it |
| Non-disparagement | Leave out; add | **Leave out.** If added, [24-34-407(1)(d)][t24] has a rule that an employer who disparages loses enforcement, and the agreement needs the same addendum | Any clause like this, especially at separation |
| Non-compete and customer non-solicit | Leave out, as drafted; add | **Leave out.** A non-compete is void except for highly compensated workers, to protect trade secrets and only as narrowly as needed. A customer non-solicit has a 60% threshold. Each needs a separate signed notice ([8-2-113(2), (4)][t8]). The 2026 threshold is reported as $130,014, which was read only in a search excerpt. Check it. Using an out-of-state law or forum for a Colorado worker doesn't work ([8-2-113(6)][t8]) | **Anyone earning above the threshold** for whom you want a covenant: use a separate document |
| Trade secret immunity notice | Keep; cross-reference a policy | **Keep in full.** Without it or a cross-reference, the employer can't get exemplary damages or fees against that employee ([18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)][us1833]). The text tracks the statute | None |
| Wage discussion | Keep | **Keep.** Colorado bars prohibiting employees from discussing their wage rate ([8-5-102][t8]), and federal law protects non-supervisors discussing terms of work ([29 U.S.C. 157][us157]) | A supervisor's coverage under federal law |
| Delaware company or worker | As drafted | **As drafted.** Delaware makes an assignment clause in an employment agreement unenforceable for an invention made "entirely on the employee's own time without using the employer's equipment, supplies, facility or trade secret information", unless it relates to the business or results from work for the employer ([19 Del. C. 805][d19c8], read 2026-11-13). Section 3 leaves those inventions with the person, and (a) and (b) track the two exceptions. Nothing here turns on whether the company is an LLC or a corporation | Section 3(a) says "planned" where the statute says "anticipated" |
| Governing law | Colorado for a Colorado worker | **Colorado,** even if the company is Delaware: for a worker primarily in Colorado, the covenant statute controls regardless ([8-2-113(6)][t8]) | A person working elsewhere |
| Work made with AI tools | Say nothing; add a rule | **Say nothing in the text, and keep records.** Purely machine-generated output may not be protected by copyright, so a founder should note where AI tools did substantial work. This wasn't researched here in detail | How AI-assisted work affects ownership and open source terms |
| Founder's work if the company fails | Leave out here; add reversion in the founders' agreement | **Leave out here.** The [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md) has an option for it. A buyer of the company's assets expects clean title | Reversion after any investment |

**Fights.** What a person may keep and use after leaving is decided here. Taking company trade secrets to help a dispute carries its own risk ([18 U.S.C. 1836][us1836]), with a narrow protection for confidential disclosure to a lawyer or official ([1833(b)][us1833]). Section 12 lets a person
keep their own papers. Colorado's trade secret statute ([7-74-102][t7]) has its own definition.

## What couldn't be checked

- **That no Colorado statute limits invention assignments.** A text search of Title 8 found none. That isn't proof. A lawyer should confirm.
- **The $130,014 threshold for 2026** comes from a search excerpt of an official order, per the [document map][map].
- **How [24-34-407][t24] applies to a founder who isn't an employee.** The carve-out is drafted to work either way.
- **Case law on how narrowly Colorado reads confidentiality clauses,** including what counts as a covenant not to compete under the case law the statute preserves ([8-2-113(1)][t8]). Not researched.
- **Stanford v. Roche.** The reporter citation was confirmed in a research tool, but the account of the Federal Circuit's reasoning wasn't checked against that opinion. A lawyer should cite-check it, along with the line of Federal Circuit cases on present assignments.
