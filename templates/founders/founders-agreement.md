# Founders' Agreement

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template. One lawyer usually can't advise all of you against each other.

## Before you use this

Founders sign this while they still like each other, because that's when you can agree on what happens if you stop. It plans for four fights:

| The fight | What decides it | Where it's handled |
|---|---|---|
| **Founders disagree** | Who decides what, and the way out of a tie | Sections 3, 8 and 14 |
| **A founder is removed** | Who can do it, on what notice, and what the founder keeps | Sections 4, 5, 9 and 10, and the [stock agreement](founder-stock-purchase.md) |
| **A founder behaves badly, and the board or investors need to act** | A fair process that can actually reach a founder | Section 6, and Cause in the [stock agreement](founder-stock-purchase.md) |
| **Investors try to take over** | What founders disclose to each other, and what they agree to hold on to at a financing | Section 7, and the [certificate](delaware-corporation-certificate.md) or [articles](colorado-corporation-articles.md) |

**What this can't do.** It binds only the people who sign. An investor who doesn't sign isn't bound. Directors can't contract away their duties, so section 16 says their duties come first. When a real financing comes, what you
want to keep must go into the charter and the investors' voting agreement. See section 7(e).

**It works alongside the others.** Sign this with the [founder stock agreement](founder-stock-purchase.md) and [invention assignment](invention-assignment.md). For an LLC, the [operating agreement](colorado-llc-operating-agreement.md), with the [Delaware sections](delaware-llc-operating-agreement.md) for a Delaware LLC,
already covers much of this. Keep only what it doesn't, and say which controls.

**Before the company exists:** people carrying on a business together for profit can form a partnership without meaning to ([7-64-202][t7]), with personal liability. Form the entity quickly. Section 1 sets a deadline.

## The document

**FOUNDERS' AGREEMENT**

This Agreement takes effect on {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}. It is among the people listed below (the "Founders") and, once it signs, {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a {{COMPANY_STATE_AND_TYPE}} (the "Company").

**1. The Company and who holds what.**

(a) If the Company doesn't yet exist, the Founders shall form it within {{FORMATION_DEADLINE_DAYS}} days. They shall cause it to sign this Agreement at formation. Nothing here makes the Founders partners, and none may bind another.

(b) The Founders' roles, ownership, and vesting are:

{{FOUNDER_TABLE}}

(c) "**Equity**" means a Founder's shares or units in the Company. Each Founder shall buy Equity on a Founder Stock Purchase Agreement, or on the vesting terms of the operating agreement if the Company is an LLC (the "Equity Agreement"), and shall sign an Invention Assignment.

(d) A "**Continuing Founder**" is a Founder who holds at least {{FOUNDER_THRESHOLD_PERCENT}}% of the Company's outstanding Equity, counting vested and unvested Equity, and who hasn't been finally found under the Equity Agreement to have engaged in Cause under its paragraphs on crime or fraud. In applying the percentage, ignore a repurchase that is being challenged, and ignore any Equity the Company issues after the Effective Date.

(e) "**Service**" has the meaning in the Founder's Equity Agreement. "**Cause**" and "**Good Reason**" do too.

**2. Time, pay and conflicts.**

(a) Unless the table says otherwise, each Founder shall work full time for the Company once it's formed, and won't take on other paid work without the other Continuing Founders' written consent.

(b) Pay for a Founder needs approval under section 3. Anyone working as an employee must be paid at least what the law requires. Founders understand that under Colorado's wage law, a person who owns or controls at least 25% of an employer can be treated as an employer for unpaid wages.

(c) A Founder shall disclose to the other Founders any personal interest in a transaction with the Company, and any business opportunity related to the Company's business that comes to the Founder, before acting on it.

**3. Decisions all Founders must share.**

[OPTION A: Every Continuing Founder must agree. Recommended for two or three founders.]
While two or more Continuing Founders remain, each Founder shall vote their Equity, and the Company shall act, only so that none of the following happens without the written approval of every Continuing Founder:
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: A majority of Continuing Founders must agree. Recommended for four or more founders.]
While two or more Continuing Founders remain, each Founder shall vote their Equity, and the Company shall act, only so that none of the following happens without the written approval of a majority of the Continuing Founders by number, which must include {{TIED_VOTE_FOUNDER_NAME}} if the vote is tied:
[END OPTION B]

(a) selling the Company or substantially all its assets, merging it, dissolving it or filing for bankruptcy;

(b) issuing Equity, or rights to Equity, other than under an approved equity plan;

(c) changing a Founder's pay, role, title or vesting, or waiving a repurchase right in a Founder's favor;

(d) a transaction with a Founder, a Founder's relative or anyone connected to them;

(e) changing the size of the board, or amending the charter, bylaws or operating agreement in a way that affects a right in this Agreement;

(f) a term sheet or financing described in section 7(c);

(g) a debt or commitment above ${{SPENDING_LIMIT}}, outside the budget the Continuing Founders have approved; and

(h) licensing or transferring core technology outside the ordinary course.

A Founder doesn't take part in approving a removal or other action under section 5 or 6 that concerns that Founder. The other Continuing Founders give that approval.

Day-to-day decisions belong to the Founder whose role covers them, as the table shows.

**4. Board seats.**

(a) Each Founder shall vote all their Equity to elect each Continuing Founder, or a person that Continuing Founder names, to the board or as a manager, unless (b)(i) applies, and to keep the board at a size that allows it.

(b) No Founder shall vote their Equity, or sign a consent, to remove a Continuing Founder as a director or manager, unless (i) it's after a final finding of Cause under the removed Founder's Equity Agreement; (ii) that Founder has stopped being a Continuing Founder; or (iii) that Founder agrees.

(c) A Founder who stops being a Continuing Founder shall resign from the board promptly on request, unless the remaining Continuing Founders agree otherwise. A Founder who resigns from all Service, other than for Good Reason, shall do so too.

(d) This section is a voting agreement among stockholders or members, and each Founder may enforce it by court order, including an order requiring that shares be voted as it says. It applies to how Founders vote their Equity. It doesn't tell anyone how to vote as a director.

**5. Removing a Founder from their job.**

(a) **Notice first.** Before the board or managers vote to remove a Founder from an office or end their Service, the Company shall give that Founder at least 10 business days' written notice stating the proposal and the reasons, with the materials that will go to the people voting. The Founder may respond in writing and in person at the meeting, with a lawyer. The vote must happen at a meeting, not by consent, unless the Founder agrees.

(b) **Emergencies.** If a Founder's continued access presents an immediate risk of serious harm to people, to Company property or to the law's being followed, the other directors or managers may put the Founder on paid leave and limit system access until the vote. This doesn't shorten the notice. It doesn't remove any role.

(c) **What removal doesn't do.** Removal from a job doesn't remove a Founder's Equity, vested rights, rights under this Agreement or right to sit on the board under section 4. The Founder's Equity Agreement decides unvested Equity. If removal is without Cause, or the Founder resigns for Good Reason, as those terms are defined in the Equity Agreement:

[OPTION A: Severance. Recommended.]
the Company shall pay the Founder's base pay for {{SEVERANCE_MONTHS}} months, in regular payroll installments that start within 60 days after Service ends, and shall reimburse health coverage continuation for that period. The Founder need not sign a release for this payment, except one limited to claims the law allows to be released, that doesn't change any right to Equity or to advancement, and that doesn't include a non-disparagement term or a new restriction.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: No severance.]
no severance is owed.
[END OPTION B]

(d) **Money that's already due.** Wages, earned vacation and expenses are payable when the law requires, and that doesn't depend on any release or dispute.

(e) **What people are told.** The Company and the Founder shall try to agree on what's said to staff, investors and customers about a removal. If they can't agree within 3 business days, the Company may make a short factual statement. Nothing here limits section 13(c).

**6. When a Founder is accused of misconduct.**

(a) **Why this is here.** The Company must be able to act when a Founder behaves badly, and a Founder must not lose everything on an unfair accusation. Investors on the board act under this section as directors.

(b) **Who runs it.** Any complaint about a Founder involving harassment, discrimination, retaliation, fraud, theft, a safety risk or a serious violation of law goes to the directors or managers not named in it. If there are none, it goes to an independent lawyer the Continuing Founders not named in it choose, or, failing that, one chosen as section 8(b) says for a mediator. Whoever receives it shall promptly hire an independent investigator who hasn't worked for any Founder or investor involved. The Company pays.

(c) **While it runs.** The accused Founder doesn't take part in any decision on the complaint, doesn't supervise or contact the complainant about it, and shall not retaliate. The Founder may be placed on paid leave, which isn't Good Reason. Everyone shall preserve all records. The Founder may respond, see the substance of the allegations and have a lawyer, and the Company shall advance legal costs as section 11 says. Protect the complainant's identity as far as a fair process allows.

(d) **The result.** The investigator gives written findings to the people running the process. They decide what happens, from no action through removal. A finding of Cause follows the process in the Equity Agreement. A complaint made in good faith is protected, even if it isn't substantiated.

(e) **The limits.** This section doesn't limit anyone's right to go to a government agency or a court, or to act under any law that requires faster action. If the law requires something else, the law controls.

**7. When investors arrive.**

(a) **No hidden deals.** A Founder who discusses or agrees with an investor, or anyone acting for one, anything about: board control; removing or replacing a Founder; a Founder's pay, role, vesting or Equity; or a sale of that Founder's Equity, shall tell all the other Continuing Founders within 3 business days, and before signing anything. This doesn't require a director to disclose what the law or a duty to the Company forbids disclosing. If that applies, the Founder shall say that something can't be disclosed. This paragraph doesn't apply to a complaint under section 6, or to anything section 13(c) protects.

(b) **Time to read.** Each Continuing Founder gets full copies of every financing document and a one-page summary of the terms listed in Schedule 1 at least 5 business days before anyone signs. Each may have their own lawyer read them.

(c) **Lines that need all the Continuing Founders.** No Founder shall vote their Equity, or sign as a holder of Equity, in favor of a financing, or any other transaction or amendment, that would: give investors or their designees a majority of the board; end a Continuing Founder's seat; let anyone other than the board as then elected fire a Founder; add new vesting on a Founder's vested Equity; or give a person other than the Founder a proxy over the Founder's Equity, unless section 3 approval is given.

(d) **No surprise consents.** No Founder shall sign a stockholder or member consent to remove a director or manager, change the size of the board or amend the charter, without first giving every Continuing Founder 10 business days' written notice of what's proposed. This applies even if the law would let the consent take effect without notice.

(e) **At a priced round.** Investors may ask to end or change this Agreement. Before the Continuing Founders agree, they shall consider which of these they need written into the charter or the investors' agreements: board seats and who can remove them; advance notice of consents; acceleration; the process for Cause; and advancement of legal costs. Ending this Agreement takes the approval in section 15(c).

(f) **Selling alongside.** If a Founder proposes to sell Equity to a third party in one or a series of sales of more than 10% of that Founder's holding, each other Continuing Founder may sell the same proportion of their vested Equity on the same terms. This is in addition to any right of first refusal in the Equity Agreement.

**8. Ties and deadlock.** A deadlock is when the Founders or the board can't decide a matter for 10 business days after one of them gives written notice that it needs a decision, and the delay is harming the Company.

(a) **Talk.** The Continuing Founders shall meet within 5 business days.

(b) **Mediate.** If that fails, they shall mediate within the next 15 business days, with a mediator they agree on. If they can't agree within 3 business days, a recognized mediation provider in {{DISPUTE_COUNTY}} County chosen by the Founder who first gave notice picks one. The Company pays.

(c) **Then:**

[OPTION A: An independent director breaks the tie. Recommended.]
Each Founder shall vote their Equity to add one board seat, if needed, and to elect to it an independent director whom the Continuing Founders jointly choose within 15 business days. If they can't agree, each Continuing Founder names one candidate, and the mediator under (b) chooses among them. That director's vote decides the matter under the rules that apply to the board. For a matter in section 3 other than section 3(a), the board's decision with that director's vote counts as the approval section 3 requires. Section 3(a) still needs its full approval. Each Founder shall vote to remove that director only with the approval of all Continuing Founders.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: A named person breaks the tie on ordinary matters.]
{{TIE_BREAKER_NAME}} decides the matter, if it's an operating matter and not in section 3 or a matter on which a director must use their own judgment. Their decision binds the Founders for this Agreement.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: A buy-sell. Only with two Continuing Founders of similar means, and only with a lawyer.]
After 12 months from the Effective Date, and if no financing or sale is under way, a Continuing Founder may give written notice naming a price per unit of Equity. Within 60 days, each other Continuing Founder must choose either to sell all of their Equity to the proposing Founder at that price, or to buy all of the proposing Founder's Equity at that price. Silence is a choice to sell. Closing is within 60 days after that. Before giving notice, the proposing Founder shall disclose to the others all material facts they know about the Company's business, financing and value.
[END OPTION C]

[OPTION D: Leave it to the statutes.]
No further step. Each Founder keeps any right to go to court that the law gives.
[END OPTION D]

**9. Leaving.**

(a) A Founder who chooses to leave shall give at least 60 days' written notice, unless the others waive it or the Founder is resigning for Good Reason on the Equity Agreement's timing, and shall help with the handover. The Founder shall resign from their offices when Service ends.

(b) If a Founder dies or becomes unable to work for 180 days in a year, their Service ends, and the part of their Equity that would have vested in the next {{DEATH_DISABILITY_MONTHS}} months vests.

(c) Vested Equity stays with the Founder unless the Equity Agreement says otherwise.

**10. Information after leaving.** While a former Founder holds any Equity, the Company shall give them, within 120 days after each fiscal year, annual financial statements and a capitalization table; and notice of any financing or sale of the Company within 10 business days after it signs a term sheet or definitive agreement, whichever is first. The former Founder shall keep it confidential as the Invention Assignment requires, and may share it with their lawyers and tax and financial advisers. This adds to what the law gives holders. It doesn't replace or limit any statutory right to inspect records.

**11. Legal costs.** To the fullest extent the law allows, the Company shall indemnify each Founder and advance their reasonable legal costs if they are, or are threatened to be made, a party or a witness in a proceeding because of their role with the Company, current or former. Advances are paid within 30 days after a request with reasonable support, on the Founder's written undertaking to repay if finally found not entitled, and, for a Colorado corporation, the Founder's written affirmation the statute requires. No security is required, and financial ability doesn't matter. This section doesn't cover a claim a Founder brings against the Company, except to enforce this section. The Company shall buy directors' and officers' liability insurance by its first priced financing.

**12. Intellectual property.**

(a) Everything the Founders create for the business belongs to the Company under the Invention Assignment. No Founder keeps a private copy of a core asset, or a sole login to one.

[OPTION A: No reversion. Recommended if you plan to raise money.]
(b) If the Company ends, its assets go as the law requires.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Reversion, if the Company ends before any outside investment.]
(b) If the Company dissolves before any outside person has invested, the Company shall, after paying its debts, assign each Founder's Assigned Prior Work back to that Founder, and each Founder gets a non-exclusive licence to what the Founders created together.
[END OPTION B]

**13. Confidentiality and your rights.**

(a) Each Founder shall keep this Agreement and the Company's confidential information confidential as the Invention Assignment says, and may share this Agreement with their lawyers, tax and financial advisers and a prospective investor or buyer who is bound to confidentiality.

(b) This Agreement has no non-compete, no customer non-solicit and no non-disparagement term. While in Service, a Founder has the duties of loyalty the law imposes on their role.

(c) Nothing in this Agreement limits the rights in section 10 of the Invention Assignment, including communicating with a government agency, discussing an alleged discriminatory or unfair employment practice, discussing wages or giving truthful testimony. The immunity notice in section 11 of the Invention Assignment applies to this Agreement.

**14. Disputes.**

(a) **Stay in the room first.** A party with a dispute under this Agreement or the Equity Agreement shall give written notice. The parties shall meet within 10 business days, and if that fails, they shall mediate within 30 days after that, as section 8(b) says. Each pays their own lawyer, and the parties split the mediator. A party may go straight to court for urgent relief, or to protect a deadline.

(b) **Keep the records.** Once a dispute is reasonably foreseeable, each party shall keep all relevant documents, messages and data, and shall not delete or alter them. Each party shall gather only records they are entitled to hold.

(c) **Where:**

[OPTION A: Courts. Recommended.]
A dispute that isn't resolved goes to the state courts in {{DISPUTE_COUNTY}} County, Colorado, or a federal court in Colorado, for a Colorado company or a Founder working in Colorado. For a Delaware corporation's or LLC's internal affairs, the courts its charter, bylaws or operating agreement name control.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Confidential arbitration for disputes among Founders.]
A dispute among the Founders under this Agreement that isn't resolved goes to binding confidential arbitration before one arbitrator in {{DISPUTE_COUNTY}} County, Colorado, under the rules of a recognized provider. This doesn't cover a claim about the Company's internal affairs that the law requires to be open to the courts, a claim the law says can't be sent to arbitration, or a request for urgent relief.
[END OPTION B]

**15. How long this lasts and how it changes.**

(a) This Agreement starts on the Effective Date. A Founder's rights under sections 3, 4 and 7 end when that Founder stops being a Continuing Founder.

(b) This Agreement ends on the Company's first public offering, a sale of the Company or the completion of its dissolution. Sections 10, 12, 13 and 14 last until the former Founder holds no Equity, and for any claim arising before then. Section 11 lasts for any act or role before then, whether or not the person still holds Equity, for as long as a claim could be made.

(c) Only a writing signed by the Company and every Continuing Founder can amend or end it, and an amendment that takes away a right that section 5(c), 10 or 11 gives a former Founder also needs that Founder's signature.

**16. Duties come first.** Nothing here requires a person, acting as a director, manager or officer, to breach a duty owed to the Company or its owners, and each keeps their own judgment in that role. If an applicable law limits any term, including one in a state's corporate statute on what a contract may do, it applies as far as the law allows. To the extent the charter or bylaws conflict with this Agreement, that document controls as to the Company's own acts, and each Founder shall vote their Equity to amend it to match this Agreement. For an LLC, the operating agreement says which of the two controls.

**17. General.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of {{GOVERNING_STATE}}, except that the law of the Company's state of formation governs its internal affairs. Notices are in writing, by email with confirmation or by tracked courier, to: {{NOTICE_ADDRESSES}}. No one may assign rights here without the others' consent, except as part of a permitted transfer of Equity whose recipient signs on. If a court finds any term unenforceable, it should narrow that term as little as needed, and the rest stands. This is the Founders' whole agreement on these subjects, along with the Equity Agreements and Invention Assignments. If those conflict with this Agreement, this Agreement controls while it's in effect. It may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

Signed on {{SIGNING_DATE}}.

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who counts as a Continuing Founder | A percentage; any holder | **A low percentage such as 5%.** Rights should end when someone's stake has mostly gone, or they've been found to have committed crime or fraud. Later share issues and a repurchase under challenge don't count against the founder. **But a completed repurchase of unvested stock can drop someone below the line,** including a founder fired early: a 10% founder fired at month 20 with no acceleration holds about 4%. Set the percentage with that in mind | How this works after a repurchase |
| Shared decisions | A, everyone; B, majority | **A for two or three founders, B for four or more.** Unanimity gives each person a veto, which is a deadlock risk. Trim the section 3 list if it feels heavy | **Can this bind the board?** The clause binds stockholder votes and the company's own promise. Delaware expressly allows company contracts with stockholders limiting its acts, for the minimum consideration the board determines ([8 Del. C. 122(18)][d8s2]), so the board should say so when it approves this Agreement. Colorado has no counterpart found, so a Colorado company should put key vetoes in the articles ([7-107-208][t7]) or the bylaws for a board vote ([7-108-205(3)][t7]) too |
| Board seats | Voting agreement, as drafted; cumulative voting; class of founder stock | **Voting agreement.** It's specifically enforceable in Colorado ([7-107-302][t7]) and permitted in Delaware ([218(c)][d8s7]). It's the cleanest way to give a smaller founder an assured seat, and it doesn't stop majority consent as cumulative voting does ([7-107-104(1.5)][t7]) | Whether to add an irrevocable proxy ([212(e)][d8s7]) |
| Notice before removal | 10 business days, at a meeting; none | **Keep.** Without it, Delaware's default lets a board change happen by consent with no prior notice ([228][d8s7]). In Colorado, a board may remove an officer at any time ([7-108-303(4)][t7]). This clause doesn't limit the board's power to remove, only what the signers promise | **Whether breaching this clause voids the removal or only gives a claim for damages.** Probably the second, so put the protection in the bylaws or charter too |
| Severance | A, a stated period; B, none | **A, if the company can pay,** because it softens a removal and reduces the urge to fight. **Three months** is a modest start, and it's a judgment call. The payment timing is built to fit the short-term deferral and separation pay rules | [26 U.S.C. 409A](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s409A/). Severance that fails can bring 20% extra tax. A release can't waive wage rights ([8-4-121][t8]), and advance waivers of securities law compliance are void ([15 U.S.C. 78cc(a)][us78cc]) |
| Misconduct process | As drafted; board discretion | **Keep.** It lets a board reach a founder, which investors will want to see. It also protects the complainant and the founder. A founder shouldn't run the inquiry into themselves, and investors shouldn't be able to use a thin accusation as a lever. The process isn't a condition to reporting to an agency | **Before relying on it,** have employment counsel check this against Colorado's anti-discrimination law. It doesn't replace a company's complaint policy |
| Investor disclosure | Keep section 7(a); drop | **Keep.** A founder's quiet side deal is how a surprise usually starts. Investors may object to the part about discussions | Confidentiality in a term sheet; the director's duty if a board has a confidential matter |
| Control lines | Keep section 7(c); soften | **Keep at formation, and expect it to change at the raise.** Most priced rounds shift the board, add vetoes and may add a drag-along. These lines don't stop a deal, but they make all the founders see it before it happens | What the charter, voting agreement and investors' rights agreement actually do. Investor forms override this when signed, if this Agreement is amended |
| No surprise consents | Keep section 7(d); drop | **Keep,** and put the matching clause in the [certificate](delaware-corporation-certificate.md) or [articles](colorado-corporation-articles.md). Section 7(d) binds only the signers. An investor who signs a consent isn't bound unless they signed this too | Whether the charter clause is valid: see those templates |
| Tie-break | A, independent director; B, named person; C, buy-sell; D, statutes | **A.** It keeps the company running, except that a sale, merger, dissolution or bankruptcy still needs every Continuing Founder, because nobody should be forced out of the company by a tie-break. On a two-founder company, that's the one kind of tie it doesn't solve, and removal of a founder at a two-seat board is decided here too. **B** suits small operating disputes. **C** is fast, but it favors whoever has more money or more knowledge, and it's a securities sale, so fraud law applies ([Rule 10b-5][e10b5]; [11-51-501, -604][t11]; Delaware's own Act only with a real link to Delaware, per [FdG Logistics][fdg]), and in a Colorado closed corporation, an officer or director buying from a holder must disclose what's material ([Van Schaack][vanschaack]). **D** leaves dissolution for deadlock or oppression, with a buyout, in Colorado ([7-114-301, -305][t7]); a custodian ([226][d8s7]) or joint venture dissolution ([273][d8s10]) in Delaware; or not reasonably practicable to continue in an LLC ([7-80-810(2)][t7]) | Whether a Colorado corporation should change the buyout election ([7-114-305(1)][t7]); whether an independent director on a two-founder board solves it |
| Death and disability | A stated number of months; none | **12 months,** matching removal protection. The family keeps what vested | Life or key-person insurance |
| Information after leaving | As drafted; none | **Keep.** It's light, and it reduces records fights. In a Colorado corporation, records rights can't be limited by articles or bylaws ([7-116-102(5)][t7]), and an LLC agreement can't unreasonably restrict them ([7-80-108(2)(b)][t7]). Delaware's right is narrower ([220][d8s7]) | Whether a contract can waive those statutory rights |
| Legal costs | As drafted; leave to bylaws | **Keep, and match it in the bylaws.** A bylaw or charter right can't be taken away after the act ([7-109-109(2)][t7]; [145(f)][d8s4]), and a contract gives a second footing. Delaware's Court of Chancery can decide advancement summarily ([145(k)][d8s4]). Colorado needs an affirmation and undertaking ([7-109-104][t7]) | Officers ([7-109-107][t7]); indemnity for a former founder |
| Non-compete and non-disparagement | None, as drafted; add | **None.** See [8-2-113][t8] and [24-34-407(1)(d)][t24]. A void covenant carries a penalty of $5,000 per worker harmed ([8-2-113(8)(b)][t8]). A term that restricts where a removed founder may work is the kind a court would examine closely | A sale of the business ([8-2-113(3)(c)][t8]) |
| Where disputes go | A, courts; B, arbitration | **A.** Colorado's articles and bylaws can't require arbitration of internal claims ([7-102-108(3)][t7]), and Delaware's can't bar its courts ([115][d8s1]). A contract among founders is different, so B is possible, and it's private. But a court can issue a fast, public order on who controls, which a founder may badly need | **Whether B reaches internal claims at all, and whether an arbitrator can decide who's on the board.** In Delaware, Chancery decides director disputes ([225][d8s7]) |
| Delaware LLC | As drafted, the operating agreement controlling | **As drafted.** Records: a member asks by written demand stating a purpose ([18-305][d6s3]). Duties may be limited, but the agreement "may not eliminate the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing" ([18-1101(c)][d6s11]). Forum: a member who isn't a manager can't waive Delaware's courts on internal affairs, except by agreeing to arbitrate ([18-109(d)][d6s1]), so 14(c) defers to the operating agreement there | Whether 14(c) B reaches internal claims |
| Reversion | A, none; B, on early failure | **A if you'll raise money,** since investors want clean title. **B** suits early teams who want their work back if nothing happens | Tax on a reversion; the rights of creditors |

### How each fight plays out

| Fight | Without this agreement | With it |
|---|---|---|
| Two 50% founders stop agreeing | Delaware: custodian ([226][d8s7]). Colorado: dissolution, with a possible buyout ([7-114-301, -305][t7]). Slow and costly | Mediation, then an independent director |
| The majority fires a minority founder | Allowed, with or without cause. Unvested stock goes back. A Delaware majority can also take the board seat that day by consent | 10 business days' notice, a hearing, severance and acceleration if no Cause, and a board seat that a signer can't vote away |
| A founder faces a serious complaint | The founder might run the response, or others might act on rumor | An independent investigator, with the founder stepping back, and consequences including a Cause finding |
| An investor courts one founder to replace another | Nobody else need know until a consent is signed | That founder must tell the others within 3 business days, and all see the documents and the one-page summary before signing |
| Investors have the votes at a later round | The documents they negotiated decide it | This Agreement's lines only bind signers. Section 7(e) makes you carry what matters into those documents |

**Removal and repurchase in bad faith.** Whether firing a founder just to trigger a repurchase breaches good faith is judge-made in each state. For a corporation, Colorado's conduct standard is in [7-108-401][t7], its conflict rule in [7-108-501][t7], and a shareholder's route for oppression in [7-114-301(2)(b)][t7], which courts have read to cover harsh and wrongful conduct ([Polk][polk]). Delaware's rewritten [144][d8s4] is part of that fight there.

**Preservation.** Deleting records once a fight is foreseeable can bring serious consequences, and it's a crime to alter or destroy records to obstruct a federal matter ([18 U.S.C. 1519](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t18/s1519/)). Taking company trade secrets carries its own risk ([18 U.S.C. 1836][us1836]). A confidentiality agreement can't stop you talking to SEC staff ([Rule 21F-17][e21f17]).

## What couldn't be checked

- **Enforceability of the whole approach** at formation. No Colorado or Delaware decision on a founders' agreement like this was checked.
- **Whether contract notice requirements bind a removal,** or only give rise to damages.
- **Whether Colorado allows a company to agree to limit its board by contract.** No counterpart to Delaware's section 122(18) was found in a text search.
- **Whether a founder is an employee at all** under Colorado's wage law ([8-4-101][t8]) depends on the facts, and a lawyer should check it.
- **Tax on severance, reversion and the buy-sell.** For your own tax professional to review.
- **Investor practice.** What investors will accept at a priced round varies, and no data measures it.
- **[Polk][polk] and [Van Schaack][vanschaack]** were read through passages and summaries from a research tool. A lawyer should cite-check them.
