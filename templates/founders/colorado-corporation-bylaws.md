# Bylaws (Colorado corporation)

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

- **Start with the [articles](colorado-corporation-articles.md).** Bylaws can't contradict them ([7-102-106(2)][t7]). Written consent,
  cumulative voting, cause-only removal and who fills a board vacancy can only be changed in the articles. These bylaws follow whatever
  the articles say on those points.
- **Who adopts them.** The incorporator or the board adopts the first bylaws ([7-102-106(1)][t7]), usually in the
  [incorporator action](incorporator-action.md) or the [initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md).
- **Authorize shares without certificates separately.** Section 6.2 assumes the board has done it ([7-106-207(1)][t7]). The initial board
  consent is the place.
- **What bylaws can't do.** They set the company's process. A promise that a particular founder keeps a seat belongs in a
  [voting agreement](founders-agreement.md), which a court can order the parties to follow ([7-107-302][t7]). Severance and vesting belong in that
  founder's own agreements, which survive removal from office ([7-108-304][t7]).
- **Two seats have a limit.** On a two-seat board, a director accused of misconduct can block an investigation committee and any freeze of authority, by voting no or staying
  away (Sections 3.9 and 3.15). If that risk matters to you, use three seats.
- **Expect requests at a fundraise.** When investors buy shares at a set price (a "priced round"), they may ask to rewrite the board, transfer and notice sections. No data
  measures how often or how much. Decide now, knowing that.
- **Words.** Colorado says shareholder and articles of incorporation where Delaware says stockholder and certificate of incorporation. A "voting group" is the shares that vote
  and are counted together on a matter. All shares entitled to vote generally on it are one voting group ([7-101-401(36)][t7]).

**BYLAWS OF {{COMPANY_NAME}}**

A Colorado corporation (the "Corporation")

### Article 1. Offices, notices and signatures

**1.1 Offices.** The Corporation must keep a principal office, in or outside Colorado, and a registered agent in Colorado. The board may change either by making
the required filing.

**1.2 How notice is given.** Notice under these bylaws must be in writing unless oral notice is reasonable in the circumstances. It may be
given in person, by telephone, by electronic transmission, by mail or by private carrier. Notice to a shareholder may be sent to the mailing
address or email address in the Corporation's records. An email notice to a shareholder must include a prominent statement that it is an
important notice regarding the Corporation. Notice takes effect as section 7-90-105 of the Colorado Revised Statutes provides.

**1.3 Signatures and electronic documents.** A document may be signed in any way that section 7-90-102 of the Colorado Revised Statutes
treats as signing, including by electronic signature. The Corporation must keep its records in written form.

### Article 2. Shareholders

**2.1 Annual meeting.** The Corporation must hold a shareholders' meeting each year, on a date and at a time the board sets, within six
months after the end of the fiscal year. Failing to hold it does not affect the validity of any corporate action.

**2.2 Special meetings.**

(a) The Corporation must hold a special meeting if it is called by the board, the chair of the board or the chief executive officer.

(b) The Corporation must also hold a special meeting if it receives one or more written demands that state the purpose of the meeting and
are signed and dated by holders of shares representing at least ten percent of all the votes entitled to be cast on any issue proposed to
be considered at the meeting.

(c) The Corporation must give notice of a meeting demanded under paragraph (b) within thirty days after it receives the last demand needed.

(d) Only business within the purposes described in the notice may be conducted at a special meeting.

**2.3 Place and remote participation.** The board sets the place of each meeting, in or out of Colorado. If the board sets none, the meeting
is held at the principal office. The board may let shareholders take part by remote communication, or may hold the meeting only by remote
communication, if the Corporation takes the reasonable measures section 7-107-108 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires: verifying
that each remote participant is a shareholder, and giving each a reasonable opportunity to participate and vote, including to read or hear
the proceedings substantially at the same time they occur.

**2.4 Notice of meetings.**

(a) The Corporation must give each shareholder entitled to vote notice of the date, time and place, if any, of each annual and special
meeting no fewer than ten and no more than sixty days before the meeting. If the number of authorized shares is to be increased, the
Corporation must give at least thirty days' notice.

(b) Notice of a special meeting must describe the purposes of the meeting. Notice of an annual meeting need not, except where the law or
these bylaws require it. Those include a meeting to remove a director (Section 3.6) and a meeting to vote on an amendment to the articles of
incorporation, whose notice must include a copy or summary of the amendment.

(c) If a meeting is adjourned to a different date, time or place, new notice is not needed if the new date, time or place is announced before
adjournment. If a new record date is or must be set for the adjourned meeting, the Corporation must give notice to the shareholders as of the
new record date.

**2.5 Waiver of notice.** A shareholder may waive notice before or after the meeting by a signed writing delivered to the Corporation. A
shareholder who attends waives an objection to lack of notice or defective notice, unless the shareholder objects at the beginning of the
meeting. A shareholder who attends also waives an objection to considering a matter outside the purposes in the notice, unless the shareholder
objects when the matter is presented.

**2.6 Record date.** The board may set a future record date, no more than seventy days before the meeting or action. If the board sets none,
the record date for notice of and voting at a meeting is the day before the first notice is given. The record date for a demand under
Section 2.2(b) is the later of the date of the earliest demand and the date sixty days before the first demand is received. The board must set
a new record date if a meeting is adjourned to a date more than one hundred twenty days after the date set for the original meeting. Section
2.11 sets the record date for action without a meeting.

**2.7 Shareholders' list.** After setting a record date for a meeting, the Corporation must prepare a list of the shareholders entitled to
notice, arranged by voting group and by class or series, alphabetical within each, showing each holder's address and shares. The list must be
open to any shareholder, from the earlier of ten days before the meeting and two business days after notice is given, through the meeting and
any adjournment. The Corporation must make it available as section 7-107-201 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires.

**2.8 Quorum.** A majority of the votes entitled to be cast on a matter by a voting group is a quorum of that group for that matter, unless the
law or the articles of incorporation provide otherwise. A share represented for any purpose at a meeting counts toward the quorum for the rest
of the meeting and any adjournment, unless the articles provide otherwise or a new record date is or must be set.

**2.9 Voting.**

(a) Each outstanding share has one vote on each matter, unless the articles of incorporation provide otherwise.

(b) If a quorum exists, a matter other than the election of directors is approved if the votes cast within the voting group in favor exceed the
votes cast against, unless the law, the articles or a bylaw allowed by Section 12.2(c) requires more. Where two or more voting groups vote on a
matter, each must approve it, counted separately.

(c) In an election of directors, the candidates receiving the highest number of votes cast in favor, up to the number of seats, are elected.
Shareholders may cumulate votes only if the articles leave cumulative voting in effect.

**2.10 Proxies.** A shareholder may vote in person or by proxy. A proxy may be appointed by a signed form or by an electronic transmission from
which it can be determined that the shareholder authorized it. An appointment takes effect against the Corporation when received. It lasts for
the term it states or, if none, eleven months. It may be revoked unless the form conspicuously states that it is irrevocable and it is coupled
with an interest, as section 7-107-203 of the Colorado Revised Statutes provides.

**2.11 Action without a meeting.**

(a) **When allowed.** Shareholders may act without a meeting only as section 7-107-104 of the Colorado Revised Statutes and the articles of
incorporation allow. Unless the articles expressly allow the holders of the minimum number of votes needed to act, every shareholder entitled
to vote must consent. Any advance notice the articles require must also be given.

(b) **Electing or removing directors if votes can be cumulated.** If shares may be voted cumulatively, shareholders may elect or remove directors
without a meeting only if every shareholder entitled to vote in the election or removal signs a writing describing and consenting to the election
or removal of the same directors.

(c) **Form.** Each consent must be a signed document that describes the action and consents to it.

(d) **Delivery.** A consent is delivered by actual receipt at the registered office or principal office, by the Secretary, or by another person the
board authorizes. The Corporation designates {{CONSENTS_EMAIL}} as its information processing system for receiving consents by electronic
transmission. The Secretary must record the date and time each consent is received, and no officer may delay or refuse that record.

(e) **Time limit.** An action fails unless, within sixty days after the Corporation first receives a signed consent describing it, the Corporation
has received, and not had revoked, consents signed by holders of enough shares.

(f) **When it takes effect.** The action takes effect when the Corporation receives the last document needed, unless all the documents needed state
another date.

(g) **Revocation.** A shareholder may revoke a consent by a signed, dated document that describes the action and states that the consent is revoked,
if the Corporation receives it before the action takes effect.

(h) **Record date.** Unless a record date is otherwise set as the statute allows, the record date is the date the Corporation first receives a
document on which the action is taken.

(i) **Notice afterward.** If fewer than all shareholders entitled to vote consent, the Corporation or the shareholders taking the action must give
notice of the action to every shareholder entitled to vote who did not consent, with the material that a notice of a meeting on the action would
have had to include. That notice must be given promptly after the Corporation receives the last document needed, and in any case within two business
days. The Corporation must send a copy to every director at the same time.

(j) **Effect.** Action under this Section has the same effect as action at a meeting.

The board may not amend this Section.

### Article 3. Directors

**3.1 Powers.** All corporate powers are exercised by or under the authority of the board, and the business and affairs of the Corporation are managed
under its direction, subject to the articles of incorporation.

**3.2 Number.**

The board has {{BOARD_SIZE}} directors. Only the shareholders may change that number. The board may not amend this Section.

[OPTION B: A range the board can move within.]
The board has at least {{BOARD_SIZE_MINIMUM}} and at most {{BOARD_SIZE_MAXIMUM}} directors. Within that range the shareholders or the board may fix or
change the number. The board may not amend this Section.
[END OPTION B]

**3.3 Qualifications.** Each director must be an individual at least eighteen years old. A director need not be a Colorado resident or a shareholder.

**3.4 Election and term.** Directors are elected at each annual meeting, unless the articles of incorporation stagger their terms. A director's term ends
at the next annual meeting after the election, but the director serves until a successor is elected and qualifies. A decrease in the number of directors
does not shorten a sitting director's term.

**3.5 Resignation.** A director may resign at any time by notice to the Corporation. The resignation takes effect when the Corporation receives it, unless
it states a later date.

**3.6 Removal.**

(a) The shareholders may remove one or more directors, with or without cause, unless the articles of incorporation allow removal only for cause.

(b) If a director was elected by a voting group, only that voting group may vote on removing that director.

(c) A director may be removed only if the votes cast in favor of removal exceed the votes cast against it. If cumulative voting is in effect, a director may
not be removed if the number of votes sufficient to elect the director under cumulative voting is voted against removal.

(d) Shareholders may remove a director at a meeting called for that purpose, and the notice must state that the purpose, or one of the purposes, is removing
the director. Section 2.11 applies to any action on removal without a meeting.

(e) The Corporation must send the director who is the subject of removal the notice of the meeting, or the notice under Section 2.11(i), whether or not the
director holds voting shares. That director may speak at the meeting before the vote. The board may not amend this paragraph.

(f) The Corporation or holders of at least ten percent of the outstanding shares of any class may ask the district court to remove a director as section
7-108-109 of the Colorado Revised Statutes provides.

**3.7 Vacancies.** Unless the articles of incorporation provide otherwise, the shareholders or the board may fill a vacancy, including one created by an increase
in the number of directors. If the directors remaining are fewer than a quorum, they may fill it by a majority of all the directors remaining. If the seat was
held by a director elected by a voting group, only that group's remaining directors or shareholders may fill it.

**3.8 Meetings.** The board may hold regular and special meetings in or out of Colorado, and by remote communication. Any director may take part by any means
through which all participating directors can hear each other, and is then present in person. The chair of the board, the chief executive officer or any two
directors may call a special meeting. If the board has fewer than three directors, any director may call one.

**3.9 Notice of board meetings.**

(a) **Regular meetings.** Once the board has set a schedule, regular meetings need no notice, except as this Section provides.

(b) **Special meetings.** A special meeting needs at least two days' notice of the date, time and place, if any, or the means of remote access.

[OPTION A: Longer notice for matters that decide a fight. Recommended for two or more founders.]
(c) **Sensitive Matters.** The board may act on a Sensitive Matter at a meeting, regular or special, only if every director received at least
{{SENSITIVE_NOTICE_DAYS}} days' notice describing the matter. "Sensitive Matter" means:

(1) removing, suspending or replacing an officer, or reducing the title, authority or pay of a Founder;

(2) calling a shareholders' meeting, or asking for shareholder consents, to remove a director or change the number of directors;

[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Two days for everything. Recommended for a single founder.]
(c) This Section applies to every matter. The notice need not describe the purpose of the meeting.
[END OPTION B]

The board may not amend this Section.

**3.10 Waiver of notice.** A director may waive notice before or after the meeting by a signed writing. Attending or taking part in a meeting waives notice, unless
the director objects at the beginning of the meeting or promptly on arriving, and does not then vote for or assent to the action taken. Where notice of a particular
purpose was required, a director also keeps the objection by objecting to business on that purpose and not then voting for or assenting to action on it.

**3.11 Quorum and voting.**

(a) A quorum is a majority of the number of directors fixed or, if a range applies and no number is fixed, of the number in office immediately before the meeting begins.

(b) If a quorum is present when a vote is taken, the affirmative vote of a majority of the directors present is the act of the board, unless the law or these bylaws
require more.

[OPTION A: No greater vote. Recommended.]
(c) A tied vote fails. No director has a second or casting vote.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Two-thirds for the matters that decide a fight. Use only with Option A in Section 3.9.]
(c) A tied vote fails. No director has a second or casting vote. The affirmative vote of two-thirds of the directors in office is required to act on a matter in Section
3.9(c)(2), (5), (7) or (8).
[END OPTION B]

(d) A director present at a meeting when action is taken is deemed to have assented unless the director objects at the beginning or promptly on arriving and does not then
vote for or assent, asks at the time for the director's dissent or abstention to be entered in the minutes, or causes notice of it to reach the presiding officer before
adjournment or the Corporation promptly afterward. A director who votes in favor can't later dissent.

The board may not amend this Section.

**3.12 Action without a meeting.** The board may act without a meeting if all directors consent in writing, unless these bylaws require a meeting. The action is taken when
the last director signs a writing describing it, unless a director has first revoked by a signed writing received by the Secretary or another person authorized to receive it.
It takes effect then unless the directors set a different date, and has the same effect as action at a meeting.

**3.13 Committees.**

(a) The board may create committees and appoint one or more directors to serve on them, by the greater of a majority of all directors in office and the number these bylaws
require to act. The rules for board meetings, notice, waiver, quorum, voting and consent apply to committees.

(b) A committee has the authority the board gives it, but it may not: authorize distributions; approve or propose to shareholders an action that the law requires shareholders
to approve; fill vacancies on the board or a committee; amend the articles of incorporation; adopt, amend or repeal bylaws; approve a plan of conversion or merger not requiring
shareholder approval; authorize or approve a reacquisition of shares, except by a formula or method the board prescribes; or authorize or approve the issuance or sale of shares,
or a contract for it, or set the terms of a class or series, except that the board may authorize a committee or officer to do so within limits it specifically prescribes.

**3.14 Conflicts of interest.**

(a) A director, an officer, or a holder of ten percent or more of the votes who has an interest in a transaction with the Corporation, or in an opportunity the Corporation might
take, must disclose the material facts to the board before the board acts.

(b) The board must act on a conflicting interest transaction, as section 7-108-501 of the Colorado Revised Statutes defines it, by the affirmative vote of a majority of the
disinterested directors, even if they are fewer than a quorum. Alternatively, it may submit the transaction to a vote of the disinterested shareholders after disclosing the material
facts. If there are no disinterested directors or shareholders, the transaction must be fair to the Corporation.

(c) Interested directors may be counted toward a quorum.

The board may not amend this Section.

**3.15 Looking into a report of misconduct.**

(a) If the board receives a credible report that a director or officer has engaged in fraud, dishonesty, a serious breach of duty or a serious violation of law, it must appoint a
committee of one or more disinterested directors to look into it. If there is no disinterested director, the board must hire independent counsel to report to the shareholders. If fewer than two directors other than the person concerned
could vote and the board has not acted, shareholders holding a majority of all the votes entitled to be cast may direct the Corporation to hire that counsel. Paragraphs (b) and (c)
apply to that counsel as they do to a committee.

(b) The committee may hire lawyers and accountants at the Corporation's expense and may see any record of the Corporation.

(c) The person concerned does not sit on the committee. The person must get a fair description of the concerns and a chance to respond before the committee reports a finding against
that person.

(d) The committee reports to the board. The person concerned does not vote on what the board does about the report. If the board can't then act because of Section 3.11, the report
goes to the shareholders, who may act as the law and these bylaws allow.

(e) This Section does not take away any right under Article 5 or Article 9.

The board may not amend this Section.

**3.16 Information for directors.** Each director may inspect the Corporation's books, records and property, and speak with its officers and advisers, for any purpose reasonably related
to the position of director. Access may be withheld only for legal advice, and committee files under Section 3.15, on a matter where the director's interests are adverse to the
Corporation's, and only by a written decision of a majority of the other directors. A removal from office does not end this right while the person remains a director. The board may not amend this Section.

**3.17 Pay and expenses.** The Corporation must reimburse a director's reasonable expenses of serving. Any other pay for serving as a director is a matter under Section 3.14.

### Article 4. Officers

**4.1 Offices.** The Corporation has a chief executive officer, a Secretary and a Treasurer, and any other officers the board designates. An officer must be an individual at least
eighteen years old. The same individual may hold more than one office.

**4.2 Authority and duties.** The chief executive officer manages the day-to-day business under the board's direction. The Secretary prepares and keeps the minutes of board and
shareholder meetings and the records section 7-116-101 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires, and authenticates the Corporation's records. The Treasurer keeps the accounting
records. Each officer also has the authority and duties the board assigns, consistent with these bylaws. Each officer with discretionary authority must act in good faith, with care,
and in a manner the officer reasonably believes to be in the best interests of the Corporation.

**4.3 Appointment.** The board appoints officers. An officer the board authorizes may appoint assistant officers.

**4.4 Resignation.** An officer may resign at any time by notice to the Corporation. The resignation takes effect when received unless it states a later date. If it states a later date,
the board may let the officer stay until then, fill the coming vacancy with a successor who starts on that date, or remove the officer before that date and fill the vacancy.

**4.5 Removal.**

[OPTION A: By the other directors. With Option A in Section 3.9, also with longer notice. Recommended for two or more founders.]
The board may remove an officer at any time, with or without cause, by the affirmative vote of a majority of the directors in office, not counting the officer being removed if that
officer is a director. The vote must also satisfy Section 3.11(b). If fewer than two directors could then vote, only shareholders holding a majority of all the votes entitled to be cast
may remove the officer. An officer may remove an officer the board did not appoint.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: The Colorado default. Recommended for a single founder.]
The board may remove any officer at any time, with or without cause. An officer may remove an officer the board did not appoint.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: A higher bar for a named founder.]
The board may remove any officer at any time, with or without cause, except that removing a Founder as chief executive officer requires the affirmative vote of two-thirds of the
directors in office, not counting that Founder. The vote must also satisfy Section 3.11(b). If fewer than two directors could then vote, only shareholders holding a majority of all the
votes entitled to be cast may remove that Founder as chief executive officer. "Founder" means each of {{FOUNDER_NAMES}}.
[END OPTION C]

The board may not amend this Section.

**4.6 Contract rights.** Appointing an officer does not itself create contract rights. Removing an officer does not affect the officer's contract rights with the Corporation, if any,
and an officer's resignation does not affect the Corporation's contract rights with the officer. Removal from office is not itself a finding of cause under any agreement. A person's
shares, vesting and severance are governed by that person's own agreements. The board may not amend this Section.

**4.7 Handing over.** A person who stops being an officer must promptly hand over the Corporation's funds, records, credentials and other property in that person's control. This doesn't
reduce any right the person has as a director or shareholder, or the right to keep copies of the person's own agreements and pay and equity records, or any right the law protects.

### Article 5. Indemnification and advancement

**5.1 Indemnification.** The Corporation must indemnify each individual who is or was a director or officer to the fullest extent article 109 of title 7 of the Colorado Revised Statutes
permits for a director. That includes indemnifying a person who was wholly successful, on the merits or otherwise, in defending a proceeding the person was a party to because of that position, against
reasonable expenses. An officer's rights under this Article are the same as a director's, and no greater.

**5.2 Advancement.**

(a) The Corporation must pay or reimburse the reasonable expenses, including counsel fees, of an individual who is a party to a proceeding because the individual is or was a director or
officer, before the proceeding ends. It must do so within {{ADVANCE_PAYMENT_DAYS}} days after it receives:

(1) a written affirmation of the individual's good faith belief that the individual met the standard of conduct in section 7-109-102 of the Colorado Revised Statutes, or that the proceeding
involves conduct for which liability has been eliminated under the articles of incorporation; and

(2) a written undertaking to repay the funds advanced if the individual is not entitled to mandatory indemnification and it is ultimately determined that the individual did not meet that
standard. The undertaking is an unlimited general obligation and need not be secured.

(b) This obligation is made in advance of any act or omission. It satisfies the requirement for authorization as section 7-109-109 of the Colorado Revised Statutes provides.

[OPTION A: Advancement applies even when the Corporation is the one suing. Use with Option A in article 11 of the articles of incorporation. Recommended.]
(c) This Section applies whoever brings the proceeding, including the Corporation.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Not when the Corporation sues. Use with Option B in article 11 of the articles of incorporation.]
(c) This Section does not apply to a proceeding the Corporation brings against the individual, if a majority of the disinterested directors approved bringing it.
[END OPTION B]

**5.3 Decisions.** Any determination that indemnification is permissible must be made as section 7-109-106 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires, by disinterested directors, independent
legal counsel or disinterested shareholders.

**5.4 Actions the person starts.** To the extent the articles of incorporation and the law allow, Sections 5.1 and 5.2 do not apply to a proceeding the individual starts, unless the board authorized it, or it is brought to enforce this Article.

**5.5 Rights survive.** These rights continue after a person leaves office. A right under this Article in effect at the time of an act or omission can't be eliminated or impaired for that act
or omission by a later change to the articles of incorporation or these bylaws, or by a board or shareholder resolution.

**5.6 Notice to shareholders.** If the Corporation indemnifies or advances expenses to a director in a proceeding by or in the right of the Corporation, it must give shareholders the notice
section 7-109-110 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires.

**5.7 Insurance.** The Corporation may buy insurance as section 7-109-108 of the Colorado Revised Statutes allows.

**5.8 Others.** The Corporation may indemnify and advance expenses to an employee, fiduciary or agent to the extent section 7-109-107 of the Colorado Revised Statutes allows, as the board decides.

The board may not amend this Article.

### Article 6. Shares

**6.1 Issuing shares.** The board may issue shares for consideration consisting of any tangible or intangible property or benefit to the Corporation, including cash, promissory notes, services
performed and other securities of the Corporation. Before issuing shares, the board must determine that the consideration is adequate. A promissory note of a subscriber or the subscriber's
affiliate counts only if it is negotiable and secured by collateral, other than the shares, with a fair market value at least equal to the principal amount.

**6.2 Shares without certificates.** Shares are issued without certificates if the board has so authorized. Within a reasonable time after each issuance or transfer of shares without certificates,
the Corporation must send the shareholder a written statement of the information section 7-106-207 of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires, including any restriction on transfer under Article 7.

**6.3 Certificates.** If shares are certificated, each certificate must state the Corporation's name and that it is incorporated under Colorado law, the person's name, and the number and class of

These bylaws set the process. They slow a bad move and give people notice. The result is decided mostly by who holds the votes, and by the [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md), the [stock agreement](founder-stock-purchase.md) and, at a fundraise, the investor documents.
