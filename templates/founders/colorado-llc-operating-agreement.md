# Operating Agreement (Colorado LLC with two or more members)

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template. Have your own tax professional review its tax choices.

## Before you use this

- **It fits** a Colorado LLC with two or more members, taxed as a partnership, which is the default for two or more members
  ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)(i)][e7701]). It doesn't fit a single-member LLC, an LLC electing corporate tax treatment, or a company about
  to take venture fund money. See [the comparison](entity-comparison.md).
- **Why you need one.** Without an agreement, the Colorado Act decides by a majority of members counted per head, shares profits and
  distributions by the value of contributions ([7-80-503][t7], [7-80-504][t7]), and makes every member agree to amend the agreement, admit
  a member or act outside the ordinary course ([7-80-401][t7], [7-80-701][t7]). The Act also gives no
  procedure for written consent, no vesting, no buyout and no way to remove a member. This agreement supplies them
  ([7-80-108(1)(a)][t7]).
- **Match your articles.** Your articles of organization must say whether the members or managers manage ([7-80-204(1)(e)][t7]). Pick the
  same answer in Section 3.1.
- **30-day deadline.** Anyone receiving Units that can be repurchased should have their own tax professional look at it right away. An election under section
  83(b) is due within 30 days after the transfer and can't be fixed later ([26 U.S.C. 83(b)(2)][us83]). See Section 6.9.
- **If founders pay different prices per Unit,** read the note on Section 13.4 first. Section 13.4 returns contributions before anything is
  shared by Units, so that value doesn't move between founders at signing, which could be taxed as pay.
- **With exactly two Members,** a Disinterested Majority is one person, so either founder alone can suspend the other or remove the other for
  Cause (Sections 5.2 and 5.4), within the limits in Sections 5.4 and 5.7. A buyout leaves one owner, so the Company stops being a
  partnership for tax ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(f)(2)][e7701]) and this agreement stops fitting. Have your own tax professional review any buyout first, and plan to
  replace the agreement after it.
- **Sign the companion papers:** the [invention assignment](invention-assignment.md) and, if you're using it, the
  [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md). Section 18.3 says this agreement controls how the Company is governed if they conflict.
- **Delete** every option you don't choose, along with its markers, and everything outside the template markers.

# Operating Agreement of {{COMPANY_NAME}}

This Operating Agreement is made as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} by the people who sign it, and by {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a Colorado limited liability
company (the "Company").

## 1. The Company

**1.1 Formation.** The Company was formed by filing articles of organization with the Colorado Secretary of State on
{{ARTICLES_FILING_DATE}}, under the Colorado Limited Liability Company Act, C.R.S. 7-80-101 and following (the "Act").

**1.2 Name and office.** The Company's name is {{COMPANY_NAME}}. Its principal office is at {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_ADDRESS}}. The Company
keeps a registered agent in Colorado as the law requires.

**1.3 Purpose.** The Company may carry on any lawful business. Its current business is {{BUSINESS_DESCRIPTION}}.

**1.4 Effect of this Agreement.** This Agreement is the Company's operating agreement. It takes effect on the date above, and the Company
is bound by it. Where this Agreement and the Act differ, this Agreement controls unless the Act says it can't be changed. Where this
Agreement is silent, the Act applies.

**1.5 What this Agreement changes from the Act.** The members mean to change these defaults:

- Decisions and consents are counted by Units, not per head (Section 4.1).
- Every decision takes the vote this Agreement states, instead of every member's consent: Sections 3.3 and 3.4 for the matters listed there, and
  Section 3.1 for everything else, whether or not it's in the ordinary course of business.
- Profits, losses and distributions follow Article 13, instead of the value of contributions.
- New members can be admitted, and this Agreement and the articles amended, by the votes stated here.
- Officers can be given authority beyond the ordinary course by the votes stated here.
- Members can be paid for their services (Section 3.7).

**1.6 Definitions.**

- **Capital Unit** means a Unit that isn't a Profits Interest Unit.
- **Cause** is defined in Section 6.6.
- **Change of Control** means a sale of all or substantially all of the Company's assets, or a merger, conversion or sale of Units after which
  the people who held the Voting Units just before no longer hold a majority of the votes. A financing whose main purpose is raising money
  for the Company isn't a Change of Control.
- **Departure** means the end of a holder's Service for any reason.
- **Disinterested Majority** means Members holding more than 50% of the Voting Units, leaving out the Units of the person whose conduct, removal,
  repurchase, deal or dispute is being decided, and of anyone related to or acting with that person.
- **Economic Holder** means a person who holds Units without being a Member. An Economic Holder receives allocations and distributions on those
  Units but has no vote and no part in management, except as this Agreement says.
- **Fair Value** is defined in Section 8.7.
- **Founder** means each Member marked as a Founder on Schedule A.
- **Good Reason** is defined in Section 6.7.
- **holder** means anyone who holds Units, whether a Member or an Economic Holder.
- **Majority in Interest** means Members holding more than 50% of the Voting Units.
- **Member** means a person listed on Schedule A as a Member, or later admitted as one, until the person stops being a Member.
- **Neutral** means the independent person chosen under Section 17.3.
- **Profits Interest Unit** means a Unit that shares only in growth in the Company's value after it's issued, as described in Section 13.9.
- **Protected Matter** is defined in Section 3.3.
- **Repurchase Price** means, per Unit, the lesser of the capital actually paid for that Unit that hasn't been returned, and its Fair Value on the
  date of the Departure. For a Profits Interest Unit it is zero.
- **Service** means work for the Company as an employee, officer, Manager, director, consultant or other service provider.
- **Two-Thirds in Interest** means Members holding at least two-thirds of the Voting Units.
- **Unit** means a unit of ownership in the Company, with the rights this Agreement gives it.
- **Unvested Units** and **Vested Units** are defined in Article 6.
- **Voting Units** means Capital Units held by Members, whose votes haven't ended under Section 6.2 or 8.10. A Unit held by anyone who isn't a
  Member counts neither as a vote nor in the total that votes are measured against. Unvested Capital Units vote until the holder's Departure.
  Profits Interest Units don't vote.

## 2. Units and contributions

**2.1 Units.** Each holder's Units, contribution and vesting terms are on Schedule A. The Company keeps Schedule A current and gives every
holder a copy when it changes.

**2.2 Contributions.** Each Member has made or will make the contribution shown on Schedule A. A promise to contribute binds a Member only
if it's in a writing the Member signed. No Member has to contribute more unless the Member agrees in writing.

**2.3 New Units and new Members.** Issuing Units or admitting a Member is a Protected Matter. A new Member, or anyone else receiving Units, first
signs an agreement to be bound by this Agreement, in the form of Exhibit 1. The Company may issue Profits Interest Units to service providers without a further
vote, up to {{EQUITY_POOL_PERCENT}}% of all Units on a fully issued basis, with terms a Majority in Interest approves.

**2.4 Right to buy new Units.** Before the Company issues Units, it must offer each Member the chance to buy a share equal to that Member's share
of the Capital Units, on the same price and terms, with at least 15 business days to accept. This doesn't apply to Profits Interest Units under
Section 2.3, or to Units issued for an acquisition that a Protected Matter vote approved and that a Disinterested Majority finds is fair to the
Members who aren't taking part.

**2.5 Securities.** Each Member acquiring Units confirms that the Member: is acquiring them for the Member's own account and not to resell them;
understands they aren't registered under federal or state securities law and can't be resold unless registered or exempt; and has had the
chance to ask questions and get the information the Member wanted. The Company records the exemption it relies on for each issue.

## 3. Management

**3.1 Who manages.**

[OPTION A: Member-managed. Recommended where every owner works in the business.]
The articles of organization vest management in the Members. Decisions are made by a Majority in Interest, whether or not they're in the ordinary course of business, except where this Agreement requires a

Each Member may act for the Company in the ordinary course, but as between the Members, no Member may commit the Company to more than {{SPENDING_LIMIT}} in a single matter without approval from a Majority in Interest. A Member whose Service has ended, or who is suspended under Section 5.4, has no authority to act for the Company in its business, though the Member keeps any votes Section 8.10 allows, and the Company may tell others so.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Manager-managed, with Founder seats. Use where any owner doesn't work in the business, or where the team wants a board.]
The articles of organization vest management in Managers. The Company has {{MANAGER_SEAT_COUNT}} Manager seats. Each Founder holding at least
{{FOUNDER_SEAT_MINIMUM_PERCENT}}% of the Capital Units may fill one seat and may replace the person in it. A Majority in Interest fills any other

Each Manager is an individual aged 18 or older. Managers decide by a majority of all Managers in office, whether or not the decision is in the ordinary course of business, except where this Agreement requires a different vote. A meeting takes at least 3 business days' notice
to every Manager, unless a Manager waives notice or attends without objecting. Managers may act without a meeting if a majority of all Managers

A person filling a Founder's seat can be removed only by that Founder, or under Article 5 for Cause. A Founder's right to a seat ends when
the Founder holds less than the percentage above, on the Founder's Departure, or on expulsion under Article 7. If a Founder is removed from a seat for Cause, that Founder's right to the seat also ends, and the Founder can't fill it with anyone, unless the Neutral or a court finds there was no Cause. A person removed from a seat for Cause can't fill any seat again. A seat whose Founder right has ended is filled like any other seat. Any other Manager can be removed by a Majority in Interest.

Members who aren't Managers aren't agents of the Company and can't bind it.
[END OPTION B]

**3.2 Founder consent.**

[OPTION A: No separate Founder veto. Recommended for three or more Founders.]
Protected Matters are decided only by the vote in Section 3.3.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Founder veto. Use for two Founders, or where a smaller Founder wants a block.]
A Protected Matter also needs the consent of each Founder holding at least {{FOUNDER_VETO_PERCENT}}% of the Capital Units who is still in Service.
This right ends for a Founder on that Founder's Departure.
[END OPTION B]

**3.3 Protected Matters.** These need Two-Thirds in Interest:

1. amending this Agreement or the articles of organization;
2. issuing Units, except under Section 2.3, or admitting a Member, except under Section 8.1;
3. creating any interest with rights ahead of the Capital Units;
4. a merger, a conversion, a Change of Control, or a sale or exclusive license of all or substantially all of the Company's assets or intellectual property;
5. dissolving the Company;
6. borrowing or guaranteeing more than {{DEBT_LIMIT}} in total;
7. changing the Company's tax classification;
8. changing the number of Manager seats; and
9. starting a bankruptcy or similar proceeding.

**3.4 What needs the affected person's own consent.** No vote can do any of these to a Member or Economic Holder unless that person agrees in writing:

1. increase the person's contribution or make the person personally liable for a Company debt;
2. reduce the person's share of distributions or allocations in a way that doesn't apply to all Units of that kind in the same proportion;
3. change the vesting, Repurchase Price or acceleration terms of the person's Units, or take Vested Units other than as this Agreement already allows;
4. remove a Founder's right to a seat in Section 3.1, or a veto in Section 3.2, other than as those Sections provide;
5. cut the person's rights in Article 10 or Article 11 for anything that has already happened; or
6. change this Section 3.4, or the vote needed for a Protected Matter.

**3.5 Related-party matters.** A deal between the Company and a Member, Manager or officer, or someone related to or acting with one of them, needs a
Disinterested Majority after full disclosure of the material facts. That includes pay, loans, guarantees, licenses and leases. If every
Member is interested, the deal must be fair to the Company, and the Company keeps a written record of why it's fair.

**3.6 Officers.** The Company may have officers with the titles and authority set by a Majority in Interest, or by the Managers if the Company has them under Section
3.1. Officers are individuals aged 18 or older. An officer's authority covers the ordinary course of business. An officer may have authority beyond the
ordinary course only if it's given by the vote this Agreement requires for the act in question.

**3.7 Pay for services.** A Member may be paid for services to the Company on terms approved under Section 3.5. The Company reports those payments
as guaranteed payments, or as tax law otherwise requires.

## 4. Votes, meetings and written consent

**4.1 Votes are by Units.** Every vote or consent of Members, including any the Act calls for, is counted by Voting Units held, not per head.

**4.2 Meetings.** A Manager, or Members holding at least 10% of the Voting Units, may call a meeting of Members. The caller gives every Member at
least 5 business days' written notice of the date, time, place or remote link, and the purpose. A Member may waive notice in writing, or by
attending without objecting at the start. Only matters in the notice may be decided. There is no separate quorum: every vote is counted against all Voting Units, whether or not their holders attend.

**4.3 Taking part.** A Member may attend by any means that lets those present hear each other, and may vote by written proxy.

**4.4 Written consent.** Members may act without a meeting by a written consent that describes the action and is signed by Members holding the votes
needed if all Voting Units were present and voted. These rules apply:

1. **Notice first.** At least {{CONSENT_NOTICE_BUSINESS_DAYS}} business days before a consent on a Protected Matter, a removal or an expulsion takes effect, the person proposing it must give every Member a copy of it. If immediate action is needed to stop imminent and material harm to the Company, 24 hours' notice is enough, and the notice must say why.
2. **Notice after.** Within 2 business days after any other consent takes effect, the Company gives a copy to every Member who didn't sign it.
3. **Time limit.** A consent lapses unless enough signatures are delivered to the Company within 60 days after the first signature.
4. **Withdrawing.** A Member may withdraw a signature by written notice to the Company before the consent takes effect.
5. **Failure of notice.** A consent on a Protected Matter, a removal or an expulsion doesn't take effect until the notice in point 1 has been given and its time has run.

**4.5 Signatures and records.** Signatures and notices may be electronic. The Company keeps every consent, notice and set of minutes with its records.

## 5. Removal from a role

**5.1 Roles and Units are separate.** Being removed as a Manager or officer, or from a job, doesn't by itself take away anyone's Units or status as a Member. Only Article 6, 7 or 8 can do that, as they say.

**5.2 Who can remove.**

- **An officer:** a Majority in Interest, or the Managers if the Company has them under Section 3.1, with or without Cause.
- **A job or other Service:** the same vote, with or without Cause.
- **A Manager in a Founder's seat:** that Founder; or, for Cause, a Disinterested Majority following Section 5.3.
- **Any other Manager:** a Majority in Interest.
- **Any role, for Cause:** a Disinterested Majority following Section 5.3 may end a person's office, Manager seat, job or other Service for Cause, whatever vote this Section otherwise requires.

**5.3 Process.** Before removing a person, those proposing it must give the person written notice of the proposed removal and the reasons at least 5 business days ahead, and a real chance to respond in writing or at a meeting, with a lawyer if the person wants one. If the removal is for Cause, the notice must state the facts relied on, and Section 6.8 applies.

**5.4 Suspension.** If a Disinterested Majority reasonably believes a person's continued access or authority poses imminent, material harm to the Company, or that the law requires action, it may suspend the person's authority and system access for up to 30 days, on full pay. It must give the reasons in writing the same day. A second suspension on the same facts needs the Neutral's written approval. A suspension doesn't affect vesting, votes or rights under Articles 10 and 11.

**5.5 Investigation.** If a complaint raises possible misconduct by a Member, Manager or officer, a Disinterested Majority may appoint an independent investigator, paid by the Company. The person concerned must cooperate. Before any decision relying on the investigation, that person must receive a written summary of the findings and a chance to respond. No one may retaliate against a person for making a complaint in good faith or taking part in an investigation.

**5.6 What survives.** A person's contract rights survive removal. So do the person's rights under Article 10 while holding Units, under Article 11, and to the person's own pay and personal records.

**5.7 Cross-accusations.** If a person facing a suspension, a removal for Cause or a Cause decision answers within 5 business days with written notice of facts that would be Cause against a person proposing it, neither of them counts as disinterested on either accusation. The other Members decide both as a Disinterested Majority. If no other Member holds Voting Units, the Neutral decides both together under Section 17.3, even if Section 6.8 sends other Cause disputes to a court. In that case, a suspension already in place continues until the Neutral says otherwise, no new suspension or removal between them takes effect without the Neutral's approval, and the Neutral decides any request to impose or lift a suspension within 5 business days after being chosen. A person who makes an accusation in bad faith pays the Neutral's fees and the other side's reasonable legal costs.

## 6. Vesting and repurchase

**6.1 Vesting.** Units shown as unvested on Schedule A ("Unvested Units") become vested ("Vested Units") while the holder remains in continuous Service. Unless Schedule A says otherwise, they vest over {{VESTING_MONTHS}} months from the vesting start date, with nothing vesting until {{CLIFF_MONTHS}} months have passed, then that many months' share at once, then an equal monthly share. Under this Agreement, the holder receives allocations and distributions on Unvested Units from the date of issue. Who owns them for tax is a separate question: Section 6.9 covers Capital Units and Section 13.9 covers Profits Interest Units. The Company reports each holder's share as tax law requires.

**6.2 What happens at Departure.** When a holder's Service ends, vesting stops and the holder's Unvested Units stop being Voting Units. The Company repurchases all Unvested Units at the Repurchase Price, unless a Disinterested Majority decides within 90 days after the Departure not to. The Company pays the Repurchase Price in cash within 30 days after those 90 days end. The Company may assign this right to the other Members in proportion to their Capital Units.

**6.3 Acceleration.**

[OPTION A: Double trigger, plus limited protection on removal. Recommended.]
If the holder's Service is ended by the Company without Cause, or the holder resigns for Good Reason, the Unvested Units that would have vested in the next {{ACCELERATION_MONTHS}} months vest at once. If that happens at the time of a Change of Control or within 12 months after it, all of the holder's Unvested Units vest at once.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Double trigger only.]
If the holder's Service is ended by the Company without Cause, or the holder resigns for Good Reason, at the time of a Change of Control or within 12 months after it, all of the holder's Unvested Units vest at once. There is no other acceleration.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: No acceleration.]
Vesting doesn't speed up for any reason.
[END OPTION C]

**6.4 Vested Units after Departure.**

[OPTION A: Vested Units are kept, except for fair value purchase. Recommended.]
A holder keeps Vested Units after any Departure, including one for Cause, unless they're bought under Section 8.6. That purchase can follow any Departure, even a removal without Cause. It's at Fair Value, with as little as 25% paid at closing and the rest over time under Section 8.8, and the holder can't refuse it.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Discounted purchase after serious Cause.]
A holder keeps Vested Units after any Departure, and the Company may buy them at Fair Value under Section 8.6. But if Cause is finally determined under Section 6.8 and rests on clause (a) or (b) of Section 6.6, the Company may buy the holder's Vested Units within 90 days after the determination at the lesser of the capital actually paid for them and {{CAUSE_PURCHASE_PERCENT}}% of their Fair Value.
[END OPTION B]

**6.5 No removal to defeat vesting.** The Company and its Members must not end a holder's Service for the main purpose of preventing vesting or acceleration, or of buying Units at a lower price. If this Section is breached, the holder is treated as having been removed without Cause on the date that would have given the holder the greater benefit.

**6.6 Cause.** "Cause" means only that the person:

(a) was convicted of, or pleaded guilty or no contest to, a felony, or a crime involving dishonesty;

(b) committed fraud, embezzlement or intentional misappropriation against the Company;

(c) intentionally and materially breached this Agreement, the invention assignment or a confidentiality duty to the Company, and didn't cure it within 30 days after written notice, if it can be cured;

(d) intentionally and repeatedly refused to carry out lawful duties reasonably assigned, and didn't correct this within 30 days after written notice;

(e) engaged in intentional misconduct or gross negligence that caused the Company material harm; or

(f) engaged in harassment, discrimination or retaliation that violates the law or the Company's written policy, as found after a good-faith investigation under Section 5.5.

**These aren't Cause:** a disagreement about strategy or how to run the business; disappointing results; a good-faith vote or legal position; asserting a right under this Agreement; demanding pay; making a complaint or report that is protected by Section 12.3; a disability or a protected leave.

**6.7 Good Reason.** "Good Reason" means any of these without the holder's written consent: a material cut in pay; a material reduction in title, authority or duties; removal from a Manager seat the holder is entitled to, other than for Cause; moving the holder's main place of work more than 50 miles; or a material breach of this Agreement by the Company. To rely on it, the holder must give written notice within 60 days after first learning of the event, the Company then has 30 days to fix it, and if it isn't fixed, the holder must resign within 30 days after that.

**6.8 Who decides Cause.**

[OPTION A: Disinterested vote, with a Neutral if contested. Recommended.]
A Disinterested Majority decides whether Cause exists, after following Section 5.3. The person may contest the decision by written notice within 20 days. If contested, the Neutral decides under Section 17.3, without any weight given to the vote. Until the Neutral decides, or the 20 days pass with no contest: no repurchase or expulsion that depends on Cause takes effect; the person's Units stay in the person's name; and those Units keep their votes, other than Unvested Units after a Departure (Section 6.2), and other than on the Cause question itself. Distributions on the disputed Units, other than tax distributions, are held by the Company and paid to whoever is finally entitled to them. A removal from a role, or a suspension under Section 5.4, may take effect in the meantime.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Disinterested vote, reviewable in court.]
A Disinterested Majority decides whether Cause exists, after following Section 5.3. The person may ask a court to review whether the facts amount to Cause, by filing suit within 60 days after written notice of the decision. If no suit is filed in that time, the decision is final. Until those 60 days pass with no suit, or a court decides, no repurchase or expulsion that depends on Cause takes effect.
[END OPTION B]

**6.9 Tax elections.** A holder of Unvested Capital Units is responsible for deciding whether to file an election under section 83(b) of the Internal Revenue Code within 30 days after the transfer, and for filing it. The holder gives the Company a copy. If the holder files, the holder is taxed at the transfer on any value above the price paid, and later growth isn't taxed as pay. If the holder doesn't file, the Company, or whoever else transferred the Units, is treated as the owner for tax until the Units vest, anything the holder receives on them before then is pay, and the holder is taxed as they vest on their value then, less the price paid. The Company doesn't give tax advice, and isn't responsible if an election is missed.

## 7. Expulsion

**7.1 Expulsion is limited.**

[OPTION A: Expulsion only on stated grounds, by a stated process. Recommended for three or more Members.]
A Member may be expelled only under this Article, and only because (a) Cause has been finally determined under Section 6.8 and rests on clause (a), (b) or (f) of Section 6.6, or (b) the Member has been finally found by a court or the Neutral to have intentionally and materially breached Article 12 in a way that seriously harmed the Company.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: No expulsion. Use with two Members, where expulsion would hand one Member the whole Company.]
A Member can't be expelled. Article 6, Section 8.6 and Article 14 are the remedies between the Members.
[END OPTION B]

**7.2 Process.** Where Section 7.1 allows expulsion, a Disinterested Majority may expel after at least 30 days' written notice stating the ground, with a real chance for the Member to respond with a lawyer. Expulsion takes effect only when the ground is final.

**7.3 Effect.** An expelled Member's Service ends. The Member stops being a Member and becomes an Economic Holder of the Member's Vested Units, and the Company may buy them under Section 8.6. Expulsion doesn't affect any right under Section 5.6.

## 8. Transfers, buyouts and leaving

**8.1 Limits on transfer.** A holder may not transfer Units, or an interest in them, unless a Majority in Interest consents or this Article allows it. A Member may transfer Vested Units, without consent, to a trust or a company wholly for estate planning, if the Member keeps control of the votes and the transferee signs Exhibit 1. That transferee is admitted as a Member without a further vote, and this Agreement applies to those Units as if the Member still held them, including Service, Departure, Founder rights and duties. A transfer that breaks this Article isn't valid against the Company.

**8.2 What a transferee gets.** A transferee is an Economic Holder unless admitted as a Member under Section 2.3 or 8.1. A Member who transfers all of the Member's Units stops being a Member. Units an Economic Holder holds aren't Voting Units.

**8.3 Right of first refusal.** Before selling Vested Units to a third party, a holder must give the Company written notice of the buyer, price and terms. The Company has 30 days to buy on those terms. Then the other Members have 15 days to buy what's left, in proportion to their Capital Units. ## 13. Capital accounts, allocations, distributions and tax

**13.1 Tax treatment.** The Members intend the Company to be taxed as a partnership. No one may elect otherwise without a Protected Matter vote. **13.3 Allocations.** After the allocations those regulations require, including a qualified income offset and minimum gain chargebacks under 26 CFR 1.704-1 and 1.704-2, the Company allocates net profit and net loss each year so that, as closely as possible, each holder's capital account equals what the holder would receive if the Company sold all its assets at book value, paid its debts and distributed the rest under Section 13.4. Section 704(c) of the Internal Revenue Code applies to contributed or revalued property, by a method a Majority in Interest chooses, with any related-party effect handled under Section 3.5.

(a) first, to Capital Units in proportion to the contributions for them that haven't been returned, until the cash and agreed value of property contributed for each Capital Unit, as shown on Schedule A, has been returned; and

(b) then, to all holders in proportion to Units held.

A Profits Interest Unit shares under (b) only after the Units that existed when it was issued have together received, after its issue, the threshold set for it under Section 13.9.

**13.5 Tax distributions.** To the extent cash is reasonably available and the law allows, the Company distributes to each holder, at least 15 days before each quarterly estimated tax date and the annual filing date, enough for the tax on income allocated to the holder, at an assumed rate of {{ASSUMED_TAX_RATE_PERCENT}}%. This takes account of earlier losses allocated to the holder that are still usable. A tax distribution is an advance against later distributions to that holder. It must be made equally to all holders in proportion to income allocated, including holders in a dispute.

**13.7 No deficit repayment.** No holder has to restore a negative capital account.

| Protected Matter vote (3.3) | Everyone, which is the Act's default ([7-80-401(2)][t7]); two-thirds; majority | **Two-thirds.** Everyone's consent gives any 1% holder a veto over a financing. A bare majority leaves a smaller holder exposed. With two equal founders, two-thirds means both must agree. Everything else takes the 3.1 vote, so an unlisted act outside the ordinary course doesn't fall back to every member's consent under [7-80-401(2)(c)][t7] | Change the threshold when one holder alone reaches two-thirds. It would then decide everything by itself |
| Admitting members (2.3) | Everyone ([7-80-701(1)][t7]); a stated vote | **Stated vote plus a right to buy (2.4).** It protects against dilution without a veto | Securities exemption for each issue |
| Officer authority (3.6) | All members for non-ordinary acts ([7-80-403(2)][t7]); the vote for that act | **The vote for that act.** Otherwise one small holder could block routine delegation | Authority letters for banks |
| Founder veto (3.2) | None; a veto for larger or active Founders | **None with three or more Founders; a veto with two.** A veto protects against takeover, but also creates hold-up. It ends on Departure, so a departed founder can't block the Company | Whether a veto will survive an investor's terms |
| Written consent (4.4) | No notice; notice after; notice first | **Notice first for removals and Protected Matters.** The LLC Act has no consent procedure, so the agreement must supply one. Advance notice stops a surprise takeover. It gives up some speed, so 24-hour notice is allowed in a real emergency. Pick 3 to 5 business days | Whether a court would void an action taken without notice |
| Removal from a role (5.2) | An ordinary vote only; an ordinary vote, plus a Disinterested Majority for Cause | **Both.** A majority can remove officers and Managers by default ([7-80-402][t7], [7-80-403(1)][t7]), and the agreement can change that. Without the second route, at 50/50 a person accused of misconduct could block that person's own removal, and the Departure that starts 6.2 and 8.6 would never come | Employment law on ending a job, and any pay or employment document |
| Two Members (5.4, 5.7, 13.1) | A rule for cross-accusations; none | **A rule.** With two Members, a Disinterested Majority is one person. 5.4 stops a suspension being renewed on the same facts without the Neutral, and 5.7 sends accusations from both sides to the Neutral. It isn't perfect: whoever suspends first holds that position until the Neutral rules, and an accusation can buy time. A buyout ends partnership tax status ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(f)(2)][e7701]), so 13.1 ends the partnership terms going forward | Tax on a buyout that leaves one owner. Whether the Neutral's power over a suspension holds up under Colorado's arbitration statute |
| Cause (6.6) | Tight list; broad list; "as the board determines" | **Tight list, with what isn't Cause spelled out.** A broad definition lets a majority turn a dispute over strategy into a cheap buyback. A lawyer should test it against the pay and employment documents | The same definition should appear in every document. Whether firing someone to trigger a forfeiture breaches good faith is judge-made |
| Who decides Cause (6.8) | Vote with a neutral; vote with court review | **Vote with a neutral.** It's faster than court, and takes the decision from the person who gains. Removal can still happen at once, but money waits. Option B gives 60 days to sue, so a repurchase isn't frozen for the whole time the law allows for a suit | Colorado's arbitration statute, which hasn't been read for this template; whether the Neutral's scope is clear enough; and whether a court would enforce a 60-day limit on suing |
| Vesting (6.1) | None; 48 months with a 12-month cliff; credit for time already served | **48 months with a 12-month cliff.** Give credit for time already worked if that's fair. Vesting protects the people who stay | Tax on Units for services (see the next row). A capital interest for services can be taxed ([26 U.S.C. 83][us83]); only property contributions fit [26 U.S.C. 721][us721] |
| Founders paying different prices (6.1, 6.9, 13.4) | Return of capital, then by Units; an equal price per Unit; by Units alone | **Return of capital first.** The Act shares profits, losses and distributions by the value of contributions ([7-80-503][t7], [7-80-504][t7]). 13.4 replaces that with Units. By Units alone, a founder who paid less per Unit would get part of another founder's money the day the Units issue, and that share can be taxed as pay under [26 U.S.C. 83][us83]. The first tier stops that shift. An equal price per Unit does too, and then the tier changes nothing. **If a holder files an 83(b) election,** the holder is taxed at the transfer on value above the price paid, owes no pay tax at vesting, and later growth isn't pay. Forfeiting the Units later doesn't get that tax back ([26 U.S.C. 83(b)(1)][us83], [26 CFR 1.83-2(a)][e1832]). **If not,** "the transferor shall be regarded as the owner" until vesting ([26 CFR 1.83-1(a)(1)][e183]), anything the holder receives before then is pay, and the holder is taxed at vesting on value then, less the price paid | Tax law tests a Unit by what it would receive if the assets were sold at fair market value and the Company liquidated when the Unit is issued, not by its name ([Rev. Proc. 2001-43][rp0143], section 2). After the first tier, a founder's Capital Unit might receive nothing on that test and be a profits interest for tax. A Unit issued when the Company is worth more than its contributions can still be a capital interest. Have your own tax professional test each founder's Units before the 30 days run |
| Acceleration (6.3) | Double trigger plus limited removal protection; double trigger only; none | **Double trigger plus 6 to 12 months on removal without Cause.** It makes removal cost something, without giving a windfall on a sale | Tax effects, including section 409A if pay is involved |
| Vested Units (6.4) | Kept, with a Fair Value purchase; discounted purchase on serious Cause | **Kept, with a Fair Value purchase.** Earned ownership shouldn't turn on a contested finding. Option B is there for teams that want a real cost to fraud. **"Kept" has a limit under either option.** A majority can end a founder's Service without Cause (5.2). A Disinterested Majority can then have the Company buy all of that founder's Vested Units at Fair Value (8.6), with as little as 25% down and the rest over time (8.8), and the founder can't refuse. If your team doesn't want that, take Departure without Cause out of 8.6 | Whether a discount is an unenforceable penalty |
| Votes after Departure (8.10) | Follow the reason; stay; end | **Follow the reason.** Ending all votes rewards removing a founder. Keeping all votes lets a person who quit block the company forever. Under every option, Unvested Units stop voting at Departure (6.2), a person who stops being a Member has no vote, and an executor or guardian has only a transferee's powers ([7-80-704(1)][t7]) | How this reads with a Founder's veto in 3.2 |
| Resigning (8.11) | Allowed by notice, with a warning; silent | **Allowed, with a warning.** The Act lets a member resign at any time ([7-80-602][t7]) and leaves only the economic rights ([7-80-603][t7]). A person who resigns can't bring or keep a derivative claim ([7-80-713(1)][t7]), and can't ask a court to dissolve the Company unless the person is a manager ([7-80-810(2)][t7]). So a founder in a dispute should talk to a lawyer before resigning | Whether a former member has any other remedy |
| Expulsion (Article 7) | Grounds and process; none | **Grounds and process with three or more Members; none with two.** The Act gives no expulsion right at all, so nothing happens without this | Whether expulsion plus repurchase is fair on the facts |
| Fair Value discount (8.7) | None; minority and marketability discounts | **No discount.** A discount rewards the buyer for the seller's weak position | Tax valuation can differ from this contract price |
| Buyout payment (8.8) | Part cash and a secured note; all cash | **25% down, the rest on a secured note.** All cash can be more than a young company has. Units can't secure a purchase by the Company, because they're cancelled, so 8.8 uses the Company's assets instead | The security agreement and filing a lien needs, and any lender's consent |
| Disclosure in a buyout (8.9) | Required, with or without price protection; "big boy" no-reliance clause | **Required.** A person buying from a holder may owe disclosure. Colorado's Supreme Court required it of officers and directors of a closely held corporation buying from a shareholder ([Van Schaack][vanschaack]); that's a corporate case, not an LLC one. Fraud law applies if Units are securities ([17 CFR 240.10b-5][e10b5], [C.R.S. 11-51-501, -604][t11]). A waiver may not work. Price protection (Option A) stops a buyer timing a purchase just ahead of a better deal. Choose Option B if you won't agree to it | Whether Units are securities, which is judge-made |
| Duties (9.1) | Keep; release investing Members; eliminate | **Keep them for those who run the business.** The Act's no-compete duty runs until dissolution for a managing member ([7-80-404(1)(c)][t7]), and its duty on Company opportunities has no stated end ([7-80-404(1)(a)][t7]). A departed founder who keeps Units shouldn't have to offer the Company work in its own line of business, since that could be a void covenant with a penalty ([8-2-113(8)][t8]). So 9.1 and 9.4 end both at Departure, to match 12.5. The agreement may restrict or eliminate duties only if not manifestly unreasonable ([7-80-108(1.5)][t7]), and can't eliminate good faith and fair dealing ([7-80-108(2)(d)][t7]). Eliminating duties helps whoever holds control | Whether a limit is manifestly unreasonable on your facts ([7-80-108(2.5)(c)][t7]). 9.2 sets a standard for good faith, and that has its own test: the standard must be "not unreasonable" ([7-80-108(2)(d)][t7]) |
| Records (Article 10) | Track the Act; expand; cut | **Track the Act, and add timing.** The agreement can't unreasonably restrict these rights ([7-80-108(2)(b)][t7]). Former members have no statutory right, so 10.5 gives a limited one | Limits on trade secrets ([7-80-408(3)][t7]) |
| Advancement (11.3) | Mandatory, including fights with the Company; outside claims only | **Mandatory.** A removed founder without legal fees has no real rights. It costs the Company money in a fight. The Act covers reimbursement ([7-80-407][t7]), not advancement, so the agreement decides | Whether insurance covers it. The Act's power to indemnify refers to [7-80-407][t7] ([7-80-104(1)(k)][t7]), so a lawyer should confirm that the broader indemnity in 11.2 is allowed under [7-80-108(1)(a)][t7] |
| Non-compete (12.5) | None; a covenant only if the law allows | **None, by design.** Most are void, and presenting a void one carries a penalty of $5,000 per worker plus damages ([8-2-113(2)(a), (8)(b)][t8]). The exceptions need high pay at signing and at enforcement, trade secrets, and a separate signed notice ([8-2-113(2)(b), (4)][t8]). Customer non-solicits have a lower pay line ([8-2-113(2)(d)][t8]). The sale-of-business exception has a cap for minority holders ([8-2-113(3)(c)][t8]) | Any covenant you want to add |
| Confidentiality (12.1 to 12.3) | With or without carve-outs | **With them.** The law protects general skill, public information and protected conduct ([8-2-113(3)(b)][t8]). A clause limiting talk of discriminatory or unfair employment practices is void unless it meets strict conditions, with a penalty of $5,000 ([24-34-407][t24]); 12.3(a) is written so that nothing here limits that talk. The trade secret notice preserves remedies ([18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3)][us1833]). No one may impede reports to the SEC ([17 CFR 240.21F-17][e21f17]) | Any non-disparagement clause you want to add triggers more of 24-34-407, so none is included |
| Tax distributions (13.5) | Required if cash allows; none | **Required if cash allows.** Otherwise a majority can leave a minority holder paying tax on income they never received | Assumed rate; Colorado income tax; state filings |
| Partnership representative (13.8) | A Founder; a Manager; a professional | **A Founder or Manager, with limits.** The representative alone binds the Company and all partners in an audit ([26 U.S.C. 6223][us6223]), so 13.8 adds consent and notice | Whether the Company can elect out, since a partnership or trust as a member can prevent it ([26 U.S.C. 6221(b)][us6221]) |
| Allocations (13.3) | Follow distributions (target); layered | **Target allocations.** They fit Profits Interest Units and are easier to read, but they aren't within the safe harbor of the regulations by themselves | Have your own tax professional review this before signing |
| Paying members (3.7) | Guaranteed payment; wages | **Guaranteed payment,** taxed under [26 U.S.C. 707(c)][us707]. Whether a member can also be a wage employee affects payroll and benefits | Payroll and benefits. Rev. Rul. 69-184 says bona fide partners aren't employees of the partnership for employment tax, as [T.D. 9766][td9766] describes it. The ruling itself hasn't been read |
| Profits interests (13.9) | Profits Interest Units; options, which an LLC treated as a partnership doesn't grant as incentive stock options | **Profits Interest Units for hires.** Rev. Proc. 2001-43 tests a nonvested grant at grant if the holder is treated as owner, and no one deducts (its sections 4.01 and 4.02), and 13.9 says both. A threshold counts every Unit then in existence, including earlier Profits Interest Units, so 13.4 measures it against those same Units, not only Capital Units. Exceptions in Rev. Proc. 93-27 section 4.02 aren't listed in Rev. Proc. 2001-43 and couldn't be read, as "What couldn't be checked" explains | Have your own tax professional check section 4.02, and whether a protective 83(b) election makes sense |
| Conversion vote (15.1) | Everyone by default; a stated vote | **A stated vote with conditions.** If the agreement states a vote, it controls ([7-90-201.4(2)(a)][t7]). If it were silent the fallback could be the most stringent amendment rule, or every owner ([7-90-201.4(2)(b)-(d)][t7]). The company stays the same entity ([7-90-202][t7]). Stock received for property starts a new qualified small business stock clock at conversion ([26 U.S.C. 1202(i)(1)][us1202]). Its basis is at least the property's value, but that rule opens "For purposes of this section": it serves only the section 1202 exclusion, and gain built up before conversion doesn't qualify for the exclusion. So if a fund is likely, convert early, or start as a corporation | The plan's contents ([7-90-201.3][t7]); tax on conversion, especially debt above basis |
| Appraisal rights (15.2) | Denied if conditions are met; silent | **Denied if the conditions are met,** since [7-90-206(2)][t7] can give owners appraisal rights unless the documents expressly deny them. The conditions replace that protection | Whether the wording is an express denial for all purposes |
| Deadlock (14.3) | Tie-breaker plus buyout; shotgun | **Tie-breaker plus buyout.** A shotgun favors whoever has more cash. Name a tie-breaker both sides trust. Offers compare by the premium's percentage, so they still work per Unit when contributions per Unit differ | The buyout election can force a sale, so test it against your funding. A court dissolution isn't waived ([7-80-810(2)][t7]) |
| Drag-along (8.5) | None; with protections | **None until investors ask.** It can force a founder out in a sale | Enforceability is judge-made |
| Forum (17.4) | Colorado courts; arbitration | **Colorado courts.** Public, with rights of appeal. Arbitration is private and may be faster | Colorado's arbitration statute. Any non-compete dispute for a Colorado worker must stay in Colorado ([8-2-113(6)][t8]) |

## What couldn't be checked

- **Rev. Proc. 93-27** itself couldn't be opened, as the [document map](../../docs/design/document-set-and-law.md) also records. It's known only from how Rev. Proc. 2001-43 restates it, which doesn't list the section 4.02 exceptions. Those exceptions are reported, unverified, to include disposing of the interest within two years of receiving it. If so, a purchase under Section 8.6 or a conversion under Section 15.1 within two years could take a grant outside Rev. Proc. 93-27.
- **Whether a member can be a wage employee.** Rev. Rul. 69-184, 1969-1 C.B. 256, wasn't read directly, only as T.D. 9766 describes it. 26 U.S.C. 707(c) was read.
- **Treating unvested capital interests** for services. Sections 6.1 and 6.9 rest on 26 U.S.C. 83 and 26 CFR 1.83-1 and 1.83-2. Federal proposals on how those rules apply to partnership interests weren't read.
- **A buyout that leaves one owner.** Only 26 CFR 301.7701-3(f)(2) was read. How that sale is taxed wasn't researched.
- **The 60-day limit on suing** in Section 6.8, Option B. No authority on whether a court would enforce it was read.
- **Target allocations** weren't tested against the economic effect regulations in full.
- **Whether Units are securities** is judge-made and depends on the facts. C.R.S. 11-51-201(17) lists "investment contract", and the exemptions likely to fit are [15 U.S.C. 77d(a)(2)][us77d] and [C.R.S. 11-51-308(1)(i)][t11].
- **Case law** beyond the short passages read for Van Schaack and Gagne wasn't reviewed. No Colorado decision was found on: voiding action for lack of notice under an operating agreement; whether removal to defeat vesting breaches good faith; or how far a court would enforce 15.2. Neither Van Schaack nor Gagne was fully read or fully cite-checked.
- **Colorado's arbitration statute** wasn't read. A lawyer should confirm Section 17.3.
- **The 2026 pay threshold** under 8-2-113 is $130,014 in a search excerpt of the official order, not read directly, as the [document map](../../docs/design/document-set-and-law.md) records. This template contains no covenant that depends on it.
- **The Secretary of State's online articles form** wasn't opened. The site's terms say it's for natural persons and prohibit automated access, as the [document map](../../docs/design/document-set-and-law.md) records. Check the form by hand when filing.
