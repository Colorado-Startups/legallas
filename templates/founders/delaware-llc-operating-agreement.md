# Operating Agreement (Delaware LLC with two or more members)

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template. Have your own tax professional review its tax choices.

## Before you use this

| | |
|---|---|
| **It fits** | A Delaware LLC with two or more members, taxed as a partnership, the default for two or more members ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)(i)][e7701]). One member: use the [one-member agreement](delaware-llc-agreement.md). A company about to take venture fund money: see the [Delaware LLC comparison](delaware-llc-comparison.md) |
| **How it's built** | Start from the [Colorado two-member agreement](colorado-llc-operating-agreement.md). Put the opening paragraph and each section below in place of the Colorado ones with the same number, and make the word changes in the next table. Every other section, field, schedule and exhibit stays word for word |
| **Why that works** | Delaware's Act gives "maximum effect to the principle of freedom of contract and to the enforceability of limited liability company agreements" ([18-1101(b)][d6s11]). Votes, vesting, buyouts, deadlock and tax terms are contract terms, so they carry over. What changes: the Act named, the filings, duties, forum and governing law |
| **Without an agreement** | Members owning more than 50% of the profits interest decide ([18-402][d6s4]). Profits, losses and distributions follow the agreed value of contributions ([18-503, 18-504][d6s5]). A new member needs every member's consent ([18-301(b)(1)][d6s3]), and so does an amendment ([18-302(f)][d6s3]). A member can't resign before dissolution and winding up ([18-603][d6s6]) |
| **Read the Colorado notes too** | Its "Before you use this" applies here: the 83(b) deadline, different prices per Unit, exactly two Members. Its drafting notes apply too, except the rows on the sections replaced here |
| **Your certificate** | Delaware's certificate of formation doesn't say who manages ([18-201(a)][d6s2]), so Section 3.1 alone decides. See the [certificate worksheet](delaware-llc-certificate.md) |
| **The yearly tax** | $400 ([18-1107(b)][d6s11]), due June 1 ([18-1107(c)][d6s11]). See the [annual tax record](delaware-llc-annual-tax.md). Doing business in Colorado also takes [Colorado authority](foreign-qualification.md) |
| **Profits Interest Units** | Section 13.9 carries over. Legallas holds back profits interest grants in a Delaware LLC with the equity plan, so have your lawyer review a grant before making it |
| **Delete** | Every option you don't choose, with its markers, both footers, and everything outside the template markers |

<!-- TEMPLATE START -->

This Operating Agreement is made as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} by the people who sign it, and by {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a Delaware limited liability
company (the "Company"). It is the Company's limited liability company agreement under the Act.

**1.1 Formation.** The Company was formed by filing a certificate of formation with the Delaware Secretary of State on
{{CERTIFICATE_FILING_DATE}}, under the Delaware Limited Liability Company Act, 6 Del. C. 18-101 and following (the "Act").

**1.2 Name and office.** The Company's name is {{COMPANY_NAME}}. Its principal office is at {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_ADDRESS}}. The Company
keeps a registered office and registered agent in Delaware as the Act requires.

**1.5 What this Agreement changes from the Act.** The Members mean to change these defaults:

- Decisions and consents are counted by Voting Units (Section 4.1), instead of by share of profits.
- Every decision takes the vote this Agreement states: Sections 3.3 and 3.4 for the matters listed there, and Section 3.1 for everything else.
- Profits, losses and distributions follow Article 13, instead of the agreed value of contributions.
- New Members can be admitted, and this Agreement and the certificate of formation amended, by the votes stated here, instead of by every Member.
- A Member may resign as Section 8.11 allows, instead of only after dissolution and winding up.

**9.1 Duties.**

[OPTION A: Set duties for those who manage. Recommended.]
Each Manager, and each Member while Members manage the Company, owes the Company these duties and no other fiduciary duty: to account for
benefits and hold them for the Company, including from a Company opportunity; not to deal with the Company on behalf of an adverse party,
other than under Section 3.5; not to compete with the Company's business while in Service; and to avoid grossly negligent or reckless
conduct, intentional misconduct and knowing violations of law. A person's duties not to compete and not to take a Company opportunity end
on that person's Departure, even if the person keeps Units. Section 12.1 still protects the Company's confidential information.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: The same duties, but release investing Members. Use when a Member only invests.]
Each Manager, and each Member who has Service with the Company, owes the Company these duties and no other fiduciary duty: to account for
benefits and hold them for the Company, including from a Company opportunity; not to deal with the Company on behalf of an adverse party,
other than under Section 3.5; not to compete with the Company's business while in Service; and to avoid grossly negligent or reckless
conduct, intentional misconduct and knowing violations of law. A Member marked as an Investing Member on Schedule A, who holds no Manager
seat and no office, owes no duty to the Company or other Members other than under this Agreement and the implied contractual covenant of
good faith and fair dealing. That Member may invest in and work with other businesses, including competitors. A person's duties not to
compete and not to take a Company opportunity end on that person's Departure, even if the person keeps Units.
[END OPTION B]

**9.2 Good faith.** Nothing in this Agreement eliminates the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing, or limits
liability for a bad faith violation of it.

**16.2 Winding up.** A Majority in Interest, or a person a court names, winds up the Company. They collect assets, pay or provide for
liabilities, and dispose of what won't be distributed in kind. When winding up is complete, they file a certificate of cancellation with
the Delaware Secretary of State. If a holder of at least 25% of the Capital Units is in a dispute with the person running the wind-up and
asks for it, an independent person runs it instead.

**17.4 Forum for everything else.**

[OPTION A: Delaware courts. Recommended.]
Every other dispute under this Agreement, or about the Company's organization or internal affairs, goes only to the Court of Chancery of
the State of Delaware, or, if that court lacks jurisdiction, to another state or federal court in Delaware. Each party accepts their
jurisdiction.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Arbitration in Colorado.]
Every other dispute under this Agreement, or about the Company's organization or internal affairs, is decided only by binding arbitration
in {{COURT_COUNTY}} County, Colorado, before one arbitrator chosen as Section 17.3 says, applying the law Section 18.6 chooses. This
doesn't stop anyone asking a court for urgent relief in support of the arbitration, or a court dissolution under Section 14.4.
[END OPTION B]

**17.6 Rights not given up.** Nothing in this Agreement gives up rights under the wage law of the state where a person works, or any
other right the law says can't be waived.

**18.6 Law.** Delaware law governs this Agreement, without regard to conflict of laws rules.

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duties (9.1) | A, set duties; B, release investing Members; leave Delaware's default | **A; B where a Member only invests.** Where the Act is silent, "the rules of law and equity, including the rules of law and equity relating to fiduciary duties" govern ([18-1104][d6s11]). The agreement may expand, restrict or eliminate duties, but "may not eliminate the implied contractual covenant of good faith and fair dealing" ([18-1101(c)][d6s11]). A and B list what a working founder owes, and nothing else. The Colorado text's "manifestly unreasonable" sentence is a Colorado test, so it's gone | Delaware's default fiduciary duties of an LLC manager weren't researched |
| Good faith (9.2) | Keep the Colorado standard; drop it | **Drop it.** The covenant can't be eliminated ([18-1101(c)][d6s11]), and liability for a bad faith violation can't be limited ([18-1101(e)][d6s11]). A contract test for meeting it could be read as a limit | Whether Delaware courts honor a contract test for the covenant |
| Indemnification (Article 11) | Keep the Colorado text; widen it | **Keep it.** The Act lets the Company "indemnify and hold harmless any member or manager or other person from and against any and all claims and demands whatsoever", subject to the agreement's own limits ([18-108][d6s1]). Article 11 names no Colorado statute, so it carries over | Insurance, and how it fits Article 11 |
| Forum (17.4) | A, Delaware courts; B, arbitration in Colorado | **A, unless the founders want a local, private forum.** A member who isn't a manager "may not waive its right to maintain a legal action or proceeding in the courts of the State of Delaware" on internal affairs, except by agreeing to arbitrate ([18-109(d)][d6s1]). So the Colorado text's Colorado-courts option wouldn't bind every Member. The Court of Chancery hears actions on the agreement ([18-111][d6s1]) and decrees dissolution ([18-802][d6s8]). A judgment call | Enforcing an award on internal affairs |
| Court dissolution (14.4) | Keep; waive | **Keep.** The Court of Chancery may dissolve an LLC "whenever it is not reasonably practicable to carry on the business in conformity with a limited liability company agreement" ([18-802][d6s8]). 14.4 uses the same words | Delaware cases on that test weren't read |
| Resigning (8.11) | Keep 30 days' notice; the Act's default | **Keep.** By default a member "may not resign" before dissolution and winding up ([18-603][d6s6]). 8.11 lets a Member leave management, and the Units stay under the Agreement | None |
| Distributions (13.6) | Keep | **Keep.** "The Act" now means Delaware's, which bars a distribution that leaves liabilities above the fair value of assets ([18-607(a)][d6s6]) | How to value assets for the test |
| Records (10.1) | Keep | **Keep.** The list matches what a member may obtain, on a written demand stating its purpose ([18-305(a), (e)][d6s3]) | None |
| Conversion (15.1, 15.2) | Keep the Colorado conditions; the Act's default | **Keep them.** If the agreement "specifies the manner of authorizing a conversion", that controls ([18-216(b)][d6s2]). Without a clause, "no appraisal rights shall be available" ([18-210][d6s2]), so 15.2 adds the consent rule by contract | The corporation side, under the new state's law |
| Winding up (16.2) | Statement of dissolution; certificate of cancellation | **Certificate of cancellation.** The certificate of formation "shall be canceled upon the dissolution and the completion of winding up" ([18-203(a)][d6s2]). Members owning more than 50% of profits wind up by default ([18-803(a)][d6s8]), which matches a Majority in Interest | None |
| Law (18.6) | Delaware | **Delaware.** An agreement choosing Delaware law "shall be governed by and construed under the laws of the State of Delaware in accordance with its terms" ([18-1101(i)][d6s11]). 17.6 keeps each worker's own wage law | A worker outside Colorado and Delaware |

**Fights.** The Colorado text's table of fights applies, with three changes. Its court-dissolution cite is a Colorado case, and a Delaware court applies [18-802][d6s8]. Duties come from 9.1 here. Disputes go where 17.4 here sends them.

## What couldn't be checked

- **Delaware case law** on default fiduciary duties, the implied covenant, [18-802][d6s8] and [18-109(d)][d6s1] wasn't read.
- **Profits Interest Units** in a Delaware LLC weren't reviewed: Legallas holds them back with the equity plan.
- **Colorado authority, Colorado tax and Colorado wage law** for a Delaware LLC working in Colorado weren't researched here beyond the [foreign qualification](foreign-qualification.md) record.
