# Short-Form IP Assignment for Recording

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

The [invention assignment](invention-assignment.md) is the full agreement. This short form is the short document that goes on the public
record, so the full agreement's terms stay private. Use it when a founder, or anyone else, moves a filed patent application, a patent, a
trademark application or registration, or a registered copyright to the company.

| Office | Record it, in the law's words (read 2026-11-09) | What happens if you don't | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| USPTO, patents | "within three months from its date or prior to the date of such subsequent purchase or mortgage" | The assignment is void against a later buyer or lender who paid and had no notice | [35 U.S.C. 261][us261] |
| USPTO, trademarks | "within 3 months after the date of the assignment or prior to the subsequent purchase" | The assignment is void against a later buyer who paid and had no notice | [15 U.S.C. 1060(a)(4)][us1060] |
| Copyright Office | "within one month after its execution in the United States or within two months after its execution outside the United States, or at any time before recordation in such manner of the later transfer", once the work is registered and identified | A later transfer recorded first wins if taken in good faith, for value and without notice | [17 U.S.C. 205(d)][us205] |

- **Record with each office separately.** The USPTO needs a cover sheet for patents and another for trademarks, each with a copy
  ([37 CFR 3.28, 3.31][e3]). The Copyright Office records it under its own procedure ([37 CFR 201.4][e2014]).
- **The date that counts.** If the full agreement already made the assignment, the time to record may run from its date, not this
  form's. Have a lawyer check.
- **Recording gives notice only of what it identifies.** For copyright, the work must be identified so a search would find it, and
  registered ([17 U.S.C. 205(c)][us205]).
- **An intent-to-use trademark application can't be assigned yet,** before use is alleged, except to a successor to the business it
  belongs to, if that business is ongoing and existing ([15 U.S.C. 1060(a)(1)][us1060], [37 CFR 3.16][e3]). A company that takes over that
  business, or the part the mark pertains to, can be that successor; use Option A, and see the drafting note if it hasn't launched.
- **This can't fix a void trademark application.** One filed by someone who didn't own the mark on the filing date is void, and the
  applicant can't be changed to another entity ([37 CFR 2.71(d)][e271]). File again in the company's name.
- **It must stand on its own.** The Copyright Office records a document that is complete by its terms ([37 CFR 201.4(d)(2)][e2014]), so
  this form makes the assignment itself, rather than pointing to the full agreement.
- **Unpublished patent applications.** Anything filed with a trademark recording becomes public ([37 CFR 3.31(b)][e3]), and the
  Copyright Office makes recorded documents available for public inspection ([37 CFR 201.4(d)][e2014]). If Schedule A lists one,
  sign a separate short form for the patents.
- **For the Copyright Office,** send the original signed document, or a copy with a sworn certification by a party
  ([37 CFR 201.4(d)(1)][e2014]). An electronically signed document counts as a copy, so it needs the certification. For a click-to-sign, also
  send a description of the signature and evidence of it.

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separate short form | Record this short form; record the full agreement | **This short form.** The USPTO accepts a copy of the original document ([37 CFR 3.24][e3] for patents, [3.25(a)][e3] for trademarks), and the Copyright Office records a document complete by its terms ([37 CFR 201.4(d)(2)][e2014]). The full agreement's other terms stay off the public record | Whether a court or a buyer will want the full agreement too |
| Acknowledgment | Notary; none | **Notary.** A certificate of acknowledgment by a person who can give oaths is prima facie evidence that the assignment was signed ([35 U.S.C. 261][us261], [15 U.S.C. 1060(a)(3)][us1060], [17 U.S.C. 204(b)][us204]). It isn't needed for the assignment to be valid | None |
| Goodwill | Keep; remove | **Keep.** A trademark is assignable only with the goodwill of the business it symbolizes ([15 U.S.C. 1060(a)(1)][us1060]). Without it, the transfer can be challenged | None |
| Intent-to-use recital | Option A; Option B | **Option A whenever the schedule lists an intent-to-use application** that hasn't had use alleged ([37 CFR 3.16][e3]) | Whether the startup business is "ongoing and existing" if it hasn't launched |
| One signature or two | Assignor alone; both parties | **Both.** A copyright transfer needs the owner's signature ([17 U.S.C. 204(a)][us204]); the Company's signature shows it accepted, and for trademarks the USPTO accepts a statement signed by both parties as an alternative ([37 CFR 3.25(a)(3)][e3]) | None |

## What couldn't be checked

- **The offices' forms and fees.** The USPTO records through its Assignment Center and the Copyright Office through its recordation
  system; neither system's forms nor the current fees were opened. Check each office's pages before filing.
- **Whether a court reads "for value received under the Agreement" as enough consideration** in every state. A lawyer should confirm.
- **The notary block** is a placeholder; use the certificate form of the notary's state.
- **Recording outside the United States,** and foreign patent or trademark offices. Not covered.
