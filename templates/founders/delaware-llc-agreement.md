## Before you use this

| | |
|---|---|
| **It fits** | A Delaware LLC with exactly one member. By default the IRS disregards it, so its income goes on the owner's return ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)(ii)][e7701]). Section 6.3 also covers electing corporate tax treatment |
| **It doesn't fit** | Two or more members: use the [Delaware agreement for two or more members](delaware-llc-operating-agreement.md). A Colorado LLC: use the [Colorado one-member agreement](colorado-llc-single-member-operating-agreement.md). A company about to take venture fund money: see [the comparison](entity-comparison.md) |
| **One member is enough** | A one-member agreement isn't unenforceable for having only one party ([18-101(9)][d6s1]). The Act also counts oral and implied agreements, so Section 4.3 says which writings change this one |
| **Without it, death can end the company** | A company with no members dissolves unless, within 90 days, the last member's personal representative agrees to continue it, or a member is admitted as the agreement provides ([18-801(a)(4)][d6s8]). Article 9 does both, and stretches the window to one year |
| **The liability shield** | The Company's debts are its own, not the Member's ([18-303(a)][d6s3]). Article 5 lists the habits that keep the Company separate from its owner |
| **Creditors of the owner** | A charging order is a creditor's only remedy against the Units, even with one member ([18-703(d)][d6s7]). Section 8.4 restates it |
| **The yearly tax** | $400 ([18-1107(b)][d6s11]), due June 1 ([18-1107(c)][d6s11]). Section 6.4 puts it on the calendar |
| **Match your estate papers** | Name the Successor in Section 9.1. Leave the Units to the same person in your will, trust or transfer-on-death papers, or Section 9.2 runs two steps instead of one |
| **Delete** | Every option you don't choose, with its markers, and everything outside the template markers |

# Limited Liability Company Agreement of {{COMPANY_NAME}}

This Limited Liability Company Agreement is made as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} by {{MEMBER_NAME}} (the "Member") and by {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a
Delaware limited liability company (the "Company").

## 1. The Company

**1.1 Formation.** The Company was formed by filing a certificate of formation with the Delaware Secretary of State on
{{CERTIFICATE_FILING_DATE}}, under the Delaware Limited Liability Company Act, 6 Del. C. 18-101 and following (the "Act").

**1.2 Name and office.** The Company's name is {{COMPANY_NAME}}. Its principal office is at {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_ADDRESS}}. The Company
keeps a registered office and registered agent in Delaware as the law requires.

**1.3 Purpose.** The Company may carry on any lawful business. Its current business is {{BUSINESS_DESCRIPTION}}.

**1.4 Effect of this Agreement.** This Agreement is the Company's limited liability company agreement, and the Company is bound by it. It
takes effect as of the filing of the certificate of formation. Where this Agreement and the Act differ, this Agreement controls unless the
Act says it can't be changed. Where this Agreement is silent, the Act applies.

**1.5 One member.** This Agreement is written for a Company with one member. Section 8.3 says what happens before a second member joins.

## 2. Units and contributions

**2.1 Units.** The Member's limited liability company interest is divided into Units. The Member holds {{MEMBER_UNITS}} Units, which are
all the Units issued, as Schedule A shows. Units carry every economic and management right in the Company.

**2.2 Contributions.** The Member has contributed {{MEMBER_CONTRIBUTION}}. The Member needn't contribute more. A promise to contribute
more binds the Member only if it is in a writing the Member signs.

**2.3 Loans.** The Member may lend money to the Company. A loan is a debt of the Company, not a contribution, if a note signed for the
Company states its interest and repayment terms.

**2.4 No certificates.** Units aren't certificated unless the Member decides otherwise under Section 4.1.

## 3. Management

**3.1 Who manages.**

[OPTION A: Member-managed. Recommended where the Member runs the business.]
The Member manages the Company and may bind it.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Manager-managed. Use where someone other than the Member runs the business day to day.]
{{MANAGER_NAME}} is the Company's manager (the "Manager"), runs the business and may bind the Company. The Member may not bind the Company
just for being the Member. The Manager needs the Member's written decision to: spend, borrow or guarantee more than {{SPENDING_LIMIT}} in
one transaction or a series of related ones; sell, lease or pledge all or substantially all of the Company's assets; merge, convert or
dissolve the Company; admit a member or issue Units; amend the certificate of formation; or start a bankruptcy. The Member may remove or
replace the Manager at any time by a written decision. The Manager stops being the Manager on removal, resignation, death or incapacity.
[END OPTION B]

In this Agreement, the "Managing Person" means the Member if the Member manages, or the Manager if a Manager does.

**3.2 Officers.** The Managing Person may appoint officers, give them titles and authority, and remove them. An officer has only the
authority given in writing.

**3.3 Pay.** Pay for services to the Company, including the Member's, is set in a written decision. The Member may also take distributions
under Section 6.2.

## 4. Decisions and records

**4.1 How the Member decides.** The Member decides by signing a written decision, on paper or electronically. No meeting or notice is
needed. A decision takes effect when signed, or at a later time it states.

**4.2 What needs a written decision.** Each of these needs one:

- admitting a member or issuing Units;
- selling, leasing or pledging all or substantially all of the Company's assets;
- a merger, conversion or dissolution;
- amending the certificate of formation or this Agreement;
- a tax classification election;
- naming or removing a Manager or a Successor; and
- any act a bank, lender or other party asks the Company to show was authorized.

**4.3 What changes this Agreement.** The Act counts any agreement of the Member about the Company's affairs, written, oral or implied, as
part of the limited liability company agreement. This Agreement can be changed only by a signed writing that says it amends this
Agreement. Other writings, statements and conduct, such as emails, contracts and filings, record decisions and don't amend this Agreement.

**4.4 Records.** The Company keeps, in one place, paper or electronic: its certificate of formation and amendments; this Agreement and
amendments; every written decision; Schedule A; its tax returns and financial statements for each year; and a record of each contribution,
distribution and loan between the Company and the Member. The Member may inspect and copy any record at any time.

## 5. Keeping the Company separate

**5.1 A separate entity.** The Company is a legal entity separate from the Member, whatever its tax classification.

**5.2 Habits the Company keeps.** The Company:

- (a) holds its money in its own accounts, in its own name, and never mixes it with the Member's money;
- (b) pays its own debts from its own money;
- (c) signs contracts, invoices and filings in its own name, with the signer's title;
- (d) keeps its own books, recording each contribution, distribution and loan between it and the Member;
- (e) puts any deal with the Member in writing, on terms fair to the Company;
- (f) uses its own name with customers, suppliers and the public;
- (g) keeps capital and insurance reasonable for its business; and
- (h) doesn't guarantee the Member's debts. The Member guarantees the Company's debts only by signing a separate guarantee.

**5.3 Effect.** A failure to follow this Article doesn't by itself make the Member liable for the Company's debts. This Article creates no
rights for anyone else.

## 6. Money and tax

**6.1 Profits and losses.** All profits, losses and distributions belong to the holder of the Units.

**6.2 Distributions.** The Managing Person may make distributions to the Member at any time. The Company makes no distribution if,
afterwards, its liabilities would exceed the fair value of its assets, each counted as the Act counts them. A Member who knowingly receives
a distribution that breaks this rule must return it.

**6.3 Tax classification.**

[OPTION A: Disregarded, the default. Recommended unless a tax professional advises otherwise.]
For federal income tax, the Company is disregarded as separate from the Member, as the default rule provides. The Company makes no
election to change that without a written decision. For employment taxes the Company is treated as a corporation, so it reports and pays
those taxes for its employees as their employer.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Taxed as a corporation or an S corporation.]
The Company elects to be classified as an association taxable as a corporation, either by filing Form 8832 or by a timely S corporation
election, which counts as that election. The Member chooses which in a written decision, after advice from the Member's own tax
professional.
[END OPTION B]

Every Article other than this Section applies whatever the Company's tax classification.

**6.4 Delaware tax.** The Company pays the Delaware annual tax on limited liability companies by June 1 each year.

**6.5 Fiscal year.** The Company's fiscal year is the calendar year, unless a written decision changes it.

## 7. Liability, duties and indemnification

**7.1 No personal liability.** Neither the Member nor any Manager is liable for a debt, obligation or liability of the Company just for
being the Member or acting as a Manager. The Member takes on a Company debt only by signing a separate guarantee.

**7.2 Duties.**

- (a) The Member owes no duties to the Company as a member, fiduciary or otherwise, beyond the implied contractual covenant of good faith
  and fair dealing.
- (b) A Manager owes the Company a duty of loyalty. Without the Member's written consent, the Manager doesn't take the Company's property,
  profits or opportunities, doesn't deal with the Company for an adverse party, and doesn't compete with it.
- (c) A Manager also owes the Company a duty of care, limited to avoiding grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct
  and knowing violations of law.
- (d) No Member, Manager or officer is liable to the Company for a breach of duty, except for a breach of (b) or (c), or a bad faith
  violation of the implied covenant.
- (e) A deal between the Company and the Member that meets Section 5.2(e) is allowed.

**7.3 Indemnification.** The Company indemnifies and holds harmless each current or former Member, Manager, officer and Successor, to the
fullest extent the law allows, against claims, liabilities and reasonable costs of a legal proceeding they're made part of because of
their role. It doesn't cover conduct that Section 7.2(d) leaves the person liable for, as finally decided by a court.

**7.4 Advancing legal costs.** The Company pays the reasonable legal costs of each current or former Manager, officer and Successor in any
proceeding about the role, as they're incurred, within 30 days after each invoice. The person must first give a written promise to repay
if it's finally decided that the person isn't entitled to indemnification. The promise needs no security.

**7.5 Lasting effect.** These rights continue after a person leaves. A later amendment can't reduce them for anything that has already
happened. The Company pays them only from its own assets, and the Member doesn't have to contribute to fund them. The Company may buy
insurance for them.

## 8. Transfers, new members and creditors

**8.1 Transfers.** The Member may transfer any or all Units. A person who receives Units becomes a member only as Section 8.2 or Article 9
provides. Until then, that person has only an assignee's rights: distributions and allocations, but no vote or role in management.
Pledging Units doesn't end the Member's membership.

**8.2 Transfer of all Units.** If the Member voluntarily transfers all Units to one person, that person becomes the sole member at the
transfer, and signs Exhibit 1. The Member then stops being a member.

**8.3 A second member.** The Member's consent to admit a second member, or to transfer only some Units, takes effect only when the Member
and each new member have signed a limited liability company agreement written for two or more members. Once the Company has two or more
members, it is a partnership for federal income tax unless it has elected corporate treatment.

**8.4 Creditors of the Member.** A court may charge the Member's Units with a judgment against the Member. To the extent charged, the
creditor has only the right to receive distributions on them. A charging order is the creditor's only remedy against the Units. The
creditor has no right to the Company's property.

**8.5 No resignation without a replacement.** While the Member is the only member, the Member may resign only if another member is
admitted at the same time.

**8.6 Bankruptcy doesn't end membership.** The Member stays a member if any event listed in 6 Del. C. 18-304 happens to the Member.

## 9. Death or incapacity of the Member

**9.1 Successor.** The Member names {{SUCCESSOR_NAME}} as the Successor. If that person has died, declines or can't act,
{{ALTERNATE_SUCCESSOR_NAME}} is the Successor. The Member may change either name at any time by a written decision. The latest one
controls.

**9.2 Admission at death.** This Section specifically provides for admitting a member after the Company no longer has a remaining member.
The Member consents now to each admission in it.

- (a) The Successor is admitted as a member on signing Exhibit 1, effective as of the moment of the Member's death. If the Successor
  doesn't take Units from the Member, the Successor is a member without Units or any economic right, and holds only the powers in
  Section 9.3.
- (b) Each person who takes Units from the Member, by will, trust, beneficiary or transfer-on-death designation, or by law, is admitted as
  a member for those Units on signing Exhibit 1. The Member's personal representative signs for an estate that holds Units.
- (c) When a person is admitted under (b), a Successor who holds no Units stops being a member.
- (d) If two or more people are admitted under (b), they sign a limited liability company agreement written for two or more members
  within 90 days. Until then, "the Member" in this Agreement means the holders of a majority of the Units, and distributions are shared
  by Units.

**9.3 Running the Company meanwhile.** From the Member's death until a person is admitted under Section 9.2(b), the Successor makes the
decisions this Agreement gives the Member. If the Member was the Managing Person, the Successor also runs the business in the ordinary
course. The Successor needs the written consent of the Member's personal representative to: sell, lease or pledge all or substantially all
of the Company's assets; merge, convert or dissolve the Company; issue Units or admit anyone except under Section 9.2; or amend this
Agreement or the certificate of formation. Distributions go only to the holders of the Units. The Successor reports to the personal
representative each month and gives it access to every record. The Company may pay the Successor reasonably for this work.

**9.4 More time before dissolution.** If the Company ever has no members, for any reason, it isn't dissolved if, within one year after the
event that ended the last member's membership, either:

- (a) a member is admitted under Section 9.2, effective as of that event; or
- (b) the last member's personal representative agrees to continue the Company, and to admit itself or its nominee or designee as a
  member, effective as of that event.

The personal representative must make the agreement in (b) within that year if no one has been admitted under (a). The Member intends
this to bind the Member's estate.

**9.5 Incapacity.** The Member is Incapacitated when a court adjudges the Member incompetent to manage the Member's person or property, or
when two licensed physicians state in signed writings that the Member can't manage the Member's business affairs. While the Member is
Incapacitated, the Successor acts for the Company with the powers and limits in Section 9.3, with the Member's legal representative in
place of the personal representative. The Member stays the member. After a court's ruling, the representative it appoints may exercise
the Member's rights to administer the Member's property. The Successor's authority ends when a court finds the Member competent again, or
a licensed physician states in a signed writing that the Member can manage the Member's business affairs again.

## 10. Dissolution

**10.1 When.** The Company dissolves on the Member's written decision, if it has no members and Section 9.4 doesn't apply, or on a court's
decree. The Member's death, incapacity, bankruptcy or transfer of Units doesn't dissolve it.

**10.2 Winding up.** The Managing Person winds up the Company. After the Member's death, the Successor does, with the personal
representative's written consent. The person winding up collects the assets, pays or provides for the liabilities, distributes what
remains to the holders of the Units, and then files a certificate of cancellation.

## 11. General

**11.1 Amendments.** Only a writing that says it amends this Agreement, signed by the Member, amends it. While a Manager serves, the
Company gives the Manager a copy.

**11.2 Severability.** If a court finds part of this Agreement unenforceable, the rest stays in effect, and that part is enforced as far as
the law allows.

**11.3 Who may enforce it.** Only the Member, the Company, a Manager, a Successor and the people Article 7 protects may enforce this
Agreement. It gives no rights to a creditor of the Company or the Member.

**11.4 Law.** Delaware law governs this Agreement, without regard to conflict of laws rules.

**11.5 Signing.** This Agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

## Signing checklist

- Sign on or soon after the filing date. The Agreement can take effect as of the filing ([18-201(d)][d6s2]).
- Open a bank account in the Company's name, and move the contribution into it (Section 5.2(a)).
- Give the Successor a copy, and tell the Successor where the records are.
- Make your will, trust or transfer-on-death papers leave the Units to match Article 9.
- Tell your personal representative about Section 9.4.
- Put June 1 on the calendar for the Delaware tax.
- Keep the signed Agreement with every later written decision.

## Drafting notes

|---|---|---|---|
| Who manages (3.1) | Member-managed; manager-managed | **Member-managed if the owner runs the business.** Members manage unless the agreement provides for a manager ([18-402][d6s4]). Each member and manager can bind the Company unless the agreement says otherwise (same section). Choose a Manager if someone else runs it day to day. The Member may delegate further ([18-407][d6s4]) | Internal spending limits don't bind an outsider without notice of them |
| Which writings amend (4.3) | Any agreement, written, oral or implied, which is the Act's definition ([18-101(9)][d6s1]); only a writing that says it amends | **Only a writing that says so.** Otherwise a signed email about the business could change the Agreement without anyone noticing | Whether Delaware courts enforce a clause barring oral amendment of a one-member agreement wasn't researched |
| Separateness (Article 5) | A list of habits; nothing | **A list.** The Act makes the Company's debts its own ([18-303(a)][d6s3]), and the Company's own records are its best evidence that it is run that way. 5.3 keeps the list from becoming a promise a creditor can sue on | Delaware veil-piercing cases, which weren't read for this template |
| Tax classification (6.3) | Disregarded; corporation; S corporation | **Disregarded, the default** ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)(ii)][e7701]). Electing needs Form 8832 ([301.7701-3(c)(1)(i)][e7701]). A timely S election counts as the corporate election ([301.7701-3(c)(1)(v)(C)][e7701]). Either way, the Company is a corporation for employment taxes ([26 CFR 301.7701-2(c)(2)(iv)][e7702]) | Have your own tax professional review any election and its timing |
| Distribution limit (6.2) | State it; leave it to the Act | **State it.** The Act bars a distribution that leaves liabilities above the fair value of assets ([18-607(a)][d6s6]), and a member who knew must repay ([18-607(b)][d6s6]). Saying so keeps it in front of the owner | How to value assets for the test |
| Duties (7.2) | Keep default duties; restrict; eliminate | **None for the Member, loyalty and gross-negligence care for a Manager.** Duties may be expanded, restricted or eliminated, but not the implied covenant ([18-1101(c)][d6s11]). Liability may be limited, except for a bad faith violation of that covenant ([18-1101(e)][d6s11]). A sole owner's duties run to the owner's own company, so they protect no one. A Manager who isn't the owner should keep them | Default fiduciary duties of a Delaware manager, which weren't researched |
| Indemnification (7.3, 7.4) | Narrow; broad, with advancement | **Broad, with advancement.** The Act lets the Company indemnify anyone against any claim, subject to the agreement's own limits ([18-108][d6s1]). 7.3 excludes what 7.2(d) leaves the person liable for | Insurance and how it fits with 7.3 |
| Transfer of all Units (8.2) | Keep the Act's rule; require consent | **Keep it.** A sole member's voluntary transfer of all interests to one person makes that person a member, unless the agreement opts out by naming that subsection or the transfer itself provides otherwise ([18-704(a)(3)][d6s7]). Transferring all Units ends the old membership ([18-702(b)(3)][d6s7]), so the Company is never left without a member | Tax on a transfer |
| A second member (8.3) | Admit under this Agreement; require a multi-member agreement first | **Require one first,** such as the [agreement for two or more members](delaware-llc-operating-agreement.md). After formation, admission follows the agreement ([18-301(b)(1)][d6s3]), so the owner can attach that condition. A second member makes the Company a partnership for tax ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(f)(2)][e7701]), and this Agreement has none of the terms that needs | Tax on admitting the second member |
| Creditors (8.4) | Restate the Act; leave it out | **Restate the Act.** A charging order is the exclusive remedy, "whether the limited liability company has 1 member or more than 1 member" ([18-703(d)][d6s7]). The creditor gets only distributions ([18-703(a)][d6s7]) and no rights in Company property ([18-703(e)][d6s7]). Colorado's Act, by contrast, contemplates foreclosure ([7-80-703][t7]) | Case law on charging orders against a single-member LLC, which wasn't researched |
| Resigning (8.5) | The Act's default; allowed only with a replacement | **Only with a replacement.** By default a member can't resign before dissolution and winding up, and the agreement sets when one may ([18-603][d6s6]). A sole member resigning alone would leave no members | None |
| Bankruptcy (8.6) | The Act's default; override it | **Override it.** By default a member who files for bankruptcy, or makes an assignment for creditors, stops being a member unless the agreement says otherwise ([18-304][d6s3]). For a sole member that would leave no members | How the Bankruptcy Code treats this clause, which wasn't researched |
| Successor at death (9.1 to 9.4) | Admit a named Successor and bind the estate; leave it to the estate; nothing | **Both routes, with one year.** The Act saves a company with no members if a member is admitted under a clause that "specifically provides" for it, or if the personal representative agrees to continue, and the agreement may oblige it to agree ([18-801(a)(4)][d6s8]). Either way, the agreement may set the period. A member may be admitted without an interest ([18-301(d)][d6s3]), so the Successor keeps the Company running while the estate settles who takes the Units. The personal representative keeps all the Member's rights to settle the estate ([18-705][d6s7]) | Whether the period chosen fits the estate plan. How Delaware and the owner's home state treat the personal representative's duty in 9.4 |
| Incapacity (9.5) | A named Successor acts; wait for a court | **A named Successor acts.** After a court adjudges a member incompetent, the representative may exercise all the member's rights ([18-705][d6s7]). Until then no one could act for a member-managed Company. The physician route avoids waiting for a court | Whether the physician test fits the owner's own estate plan |

## What couldn't be checked

- **Delaware veil-piercing case law** wasn't read. Article 5 lists ordinary separateness habits, not tests drawn from cases.
- **Binding the personal representative.** [18-801(a)(4)][d6s8] lets the agreement oblige the personal representative to agree. Whether the owner's home state probate court enforces that duty wasn't researched.
- **Probate, guardianship and transfer-on-death law** in the owner's home state wasn't read. How Units pass at death rests on it.
- **Charging orders against a single-member LLC.** Only the text of [18-703][d6s7] was read. No case was read.
- **Bankruptcy.** Whether Section 8.6 holds up in a federal bankruptcy case wasn't researched.
- **Default fiduciary duties** of a Delaware manager weren't researched. Section 7.2 sets the Manager's duties by contract instead.
- **Delaware's registered agent rules and franchise tax filing steps** weren't read beyond [18-1107][d6s11].
- **Federal tax beyond 26 CFR 301.7701-2 and 301.7701-3.** Election timing, S corporation eligibility and how an owner is paid weren't researched.
