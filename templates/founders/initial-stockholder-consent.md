# Initial Consent of the Stockholders

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

Use this once the founder shares are issued, so the people signing are holders. Colorado's statute calls them shareholders and Delaware's stockholders.

**The state-law rules:**

| | Colorado | Delaware |
|---|---|---|
| Who must sign | **Everyone entitled to vote,** unless the articles expressly allow the needed votes ([7-107-104(1)][t7]). If cumulative voting is on, electing or removing directors takes everyone ([7-107-104(1.5)][t7]) | Holders of the votes needed at a full meeting, unless the certificate says otherwise ([228(a)][d8s7]) |
| Time limit | All needed consents within 60 days of the first one received ([7-107-104(2)][t7]) | Within 60 days of the first one delivered ([228(c)][d8s7]) |
| Effective | When the company receives the last needed consent, unless all state another date ([7-107-104(2)(b)][t7]) | When enough consents are delivered |
| Notice to holders who didn't sign | Upon receipt of all documents, with what a meeting notice would have carried ([7-107-104(5.5)][t7]) | Prompt notice ([228(e)][d8s7]) |
| Your own articles may require more | Check for a notice clause | Check for a notice clause |

**Have every founder sign,** whichever rule applies. It's one page, and it's the cleanest record of the ratification.

## Signing checklist

- Each holder signs and dates it, and the Company records the date it received each one.
- A holder who didn't sign gets the notice the statute requires.
- The plan and agreements went to each holder with the consent.

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ratifying founder sales | Include; leave out | **Include.** Approval by a vote of disinterested shareholders is one route under [7-108-501(2)(b)][t7], and approval after disclosure helps under [144][d8s4]. Where every holder is also a buyer, no holder is truly disinterested, so this is evidence that everyone knew, not a full safe harbour. Fairness is the fallback ([7-108-501(2)(c)][t7]) | Whether it does more than record knowledge |
| Plan approval | Now; within 12 months | **Now if a plan is adopted.** It's required within 12 months before or after adoption for incentive stock options ([422(b)(1)][us422]), and it must follow your charter, bylaws and state law on how approval is given ([1.422-3][e422]) | Who counts as a holder if shares vest |
| Electing directors here | Include; leave out | **Include.** It puts the board in the holders' own names rather than resting only on the incorporator. If Colorado's cumulative voting is on, consent to elect directors takes every holder ([7-107-104(1.5)][t7]). In Delaware, a less-than-unanimous consent replaces the annual meeting only in limited cases ([211(b)][d8s7]) | Directors elected by a class later |

## What couldn't be checked

- **How far ratification by interested holders helps** under Colorado's or Delaware's conflict sections. The text is clear about disinterested votes, and nothing on a formation with no disinterested holders was found.
