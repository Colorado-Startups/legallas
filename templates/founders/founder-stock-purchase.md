# Founder Stock Purchase Agreement, with vesting

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template. Have your own tax professional review the 83(b) election too.

## Before you use this

This is the document that decides what a founder keeps if they leave or are pushed out. Each founder signs one. It's for a corporation. An LLC handles
this in the [operating agreement](colorado-llc-operating-agreement.md).

**The one deadline that can't be fixed.** If shares can be forfeited, the founder has **30 days from the date of transfer** to file a section 83(b) election.
It's filed with the IRS, on [Form 15620][f15620] or by a statement, with a copy to the company, and it can't be revoked without IRS consent ([26 U.S.C.
83(b)][us83]; [26 CFR 1.83-2][e83-2]). Miss it, and each vesting date can become taxable income on the value then. File it, and you take on two other risks: tax now on any amount by which the value exceeds what you paid, and no
deduction if the shares are later forfeited ([83(b)(1)][us83]). The company can help, but filing is the founder's own step.

**In order:** board approves the sale ([initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md)), founder signs this and the [invention assignment](invention-assignment.md),
founder pays, founder files the 83(b) election and keeps proof of mailing.

**Read it from both sides.** Today you're the founder. Later you may be the remaining founder deciding what happens to someone else. Pick terms you'd
accept in either seat.

## The document

<!-- TEMPLATE START -->

**FOUNDER STOCK PURCHASE AGREEMENT**

This Agreement is made on {{SIGNING_DATE}} between {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a {{STATE_OF_INCORPORATION}} corporation (the "Company"), and {{FOUNDER_NAME}} (the
"Founder").

**1. Purchase.** On {{PURCHASE_DATE}} (the "Purchase Date"), the Company sells and the Founder buys {{NUMBER_OF_SHARES}} shares of the Company's common stock
(the "Shares") at ${{PRICE_PER_SHARE}} per share, for a total of ${{TOTAL_PRICE}}, paid in cash. The Shares remain subject to this Agreement if they are
split, combined or exchanged, and anything received for them does too.

**2. Vesting.**

[OPTION A: Four years, with a one-year cliff, from the start date. Recommended.]
The Shares start unvested. 25% of the Shares vest on the first anniversary of {{VESTING_START_DATE}} (the "Vesting Start Date"), and 1/48 of the Shares vest on
each monthly anniversary after that, so all Shares are vested on the fourth anniversary, in each case only if the Founder's Service has not ended before that
date.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Credit for work already done.]
{{CREDITED_PERCENT}}% of the Shares are vested on the Purchase Date. The rest vest in equal monthly installments over the following 36 months, beginning one month
after the Purchase Date, in each case only if the Founder's Service has not ended before that date.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: No vesting.]
All Shares are vested on the Purchase Date. Sections 3, 4 and 6 don't apply, but the definitions in sections 3 and 5 stay.
[END OPTION C]

"**Service**" means work for the Company or a subsidiary as an employee, officer or consultant. Service does not include serving only as a director. Service does
not end because of a change in title or a change from one of those roles to another.

**3. Repurchase of unvested Shares.**

(a) When the Founder's Service ends for any reason, the Shares that are unvested on that date ("Unvested Shares") become subject to repurchase at the price the
Founder paid per share or, if lower, their fair market value on that date (the "Repurchase Price").

(b) Within 10 days after the Founder's Service ends, the Company shall give the Founder a written statement of how many Shares are vested and how many are
unvested, how it calculated those numbers, and the Repurchase Price.

(c) The Company buys the Unvested Shares on the later of the 90th day after the Founder's Service ends and any later date section 4 sets, unless the Disinterested Directors decide otherwise in writing before
then. The Company shall pay the Repurchase Price in cash within 30 days after that. If it doesn't, the purchase of the unpaid Shares is cancelled and they vest.

(d) The Company may buy only as the law allows. A repurchase must meet the limits on distributions and on buying its own stock in the law of the state where the
Company is incorporated. If those limits prevent it, the Disinterested Directors may assign the right to buy to the Company's other stockholders in proportion to
their holdings, and they may buy on the same terms during the 90 days after the date the Company would have bought. Any Unvested Shares nobody buys by the end of that period vest.

(e) "**Disinterested Directors**" means the directors other than the Founder, and other than any director who has a personal stake in the decision apart from
holding stock on the same terms as other holders. If there are none, a decision for the Disinterested Directors is made by the holders of a majority of the shares
held by stockholders other than the Founder and anyone else with a personal stake. If there are no such stockholders, it's made by a Neutral. If the Founder is one of only two directors, the other director has a personal stake in any decision about Cause, and a Neutral makes that decision.

(f) A "**Neutral**" means an independent lawyer or retired judge who has not worked for the Company or for any director or Founder involved. The Founder and the Disinterested Directors shall choose the Neutral together within 10 business days. If they don't, a recognized mediation or arbitration provider in the county of the Company's principal office chooses, at either side's request. The Company pays the Neutral.

**4. Acceleration.**

[OPTION A: Protection on removal and on a sale. Recommended.]
(a) *Removal.* If the Company ends the Founder's Service without Cause, or the Founder resigns for Good Reason, the Shares that would have vested in the next
{{REMOVAL_ACCELERATION_MONTHS}} months vest on the date Service ends.

(b) *After a sale.* If the Company ends the Founder's Service without Cause, or the Founder resigns for Good Reason, within 3 months before or 12 months after a
Change of Control, all Unvested Shares vest on the later of that date and the Change of Control. The Company shall not buy Unvested Shares until 3 months after Service
ends, to allow for this.

(c) Neither paragraph requires the Founder to sign a release.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Protection only on a sale.]
If the Company ends the Founder's Service without Cause, or the Founder resigns for Good Reason, within 3 months before or 12 months after a Change of Control, all
Unvested Shares vest on the later of that date and the Change of Control. The Company shall not buy Unvested Shares until 3 months after Service ends, to allow
for this.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: Vesting speeds up on a sale alone.]
All Unvested Shares vest immediately before a Change of Control.
[END OPTION C]

[OPTION D: No acceleration.]
No acceleration applies. Section 5 stays for its definitions.
[END OPTION D]

**5. What "Cause" and "Good Reason" mean.**

(a) "**Cause**" means only that the Founder:

(i) has been convicted of, or pleaded guilty or no contest to, a felony or a crime involving fraud or dishonesty;

(ii) committed fraud or embezzlement against the Company, or intentionally took material Company assets for personal use;

(iii) engaged in willful misconduct that materially harmed the Company, including harassment, unlawful discrimination or unlawful retaliation, as found in written
findings by an independent investigator;

(iv) materially breached the Founder's Invention Assignment or this Agreement; or

(v) willfully and repeatedly refused to follow a lawful, reasonable and specific written direction of the board that was consistent with the Founder's role.

(b) Cause does not include: disagreement about strategy, poor results, or anything else short of the list above; good faith reporting of a suspected violation of law
or cooperating with an investigation; refusing to do something the Founder reasonably believes is illegal; exercising rights as a stockholder or director, including
asking to see records or voting against a proposal; or bringing a claim against the Company.

(c) **The process.** A finding of Cause needs all of these: written notice to the Founder saying which paragraph applies and the facts relied on; for (iv) and (v), 30 days
to cure, if the matter can be cured; a chance for the Founder and the Founder's lawyer to be heard by the Disinterested Directors; and a written decision by two-thirds of
the Disinterested Directors, stating its reasons. The Company may place the Founder on paid leave while this happens, and the leave isn't itself Good Reason. The notice must be given before Service ends or within 30 days after. For (a)(i) or (ii), if the facts were hidden or the conviction or plea came later, it may instead be given within 60 days after the Disinterested Directors learn them.

(d) **When a finding takes effect.** A finding of Cause takes effect only when it's final: 60 days after the written decision if the Founder hasn't started a claim by then, or when a court or arbitrator upholds it or the claim is dropped. A court or arbitrator decides whether there was Cause on its own view of the facts, without deference to the decision. Until the finding is final, the Company shall not buy any Shares because of it, or any Shares section 4 would vest if there were no Cause, and those Shares don't vest. This hold starts with the notice in (c) and ends if no written decision is made within 60 days after that notice.

(e) **If there was no Cause.** If a court or arbitrator finds there was no Cause, or a finding doesn't become final, the Founder is treated as having left without Cause on the date Service ended. The Company shall restore the Shares section 4 would then have vested, against a refund of any Repurchase Price paid for them. If those Shares can no longer be restored, the Company shall pay their fair market value on the date of that finding, less that refund.

(f) "**Good Reason**" means any of these without the Founder's written consent: a material reduction in base pay, other than one applying in the same proportion to all
executives; a material reduction in title, authority, duties or reporting line; moving the Founder's main place of work more than 50 miles; or the Company's material
breach of this Agreement or the Founders' Agreement. The Founder must give written notice within 90 days after the condition first exists, the Company has 30 days to cure, and
the Founder must resign within 30 days after that period ends.

(g) "**Change of Control**" means a sale of all or substantially all of the Company's assets, or a merger or sale of stock after which the people who held the Company's
voting stock just before it hold less than a majority of the voting power of the Company or its successor. A financing whose main purpose is raising money isn't a Change of
Control.

**6. How Unvested Shares are held.** The Company records the Shares in its stock records with a note of this Agreement. The Founder signs the stock power attached as Exhibit
A, which the Company may use only to record a repurchase this Agreement allows, after giving the statement in section 3(b). The Founder may vote all the Shares, vested or
not, and receives any dividends, until they are bought. Dividends paid in stock on Unvested Shares stay subject to this Agreement.

**7. Vested Shares.**

[OPTION A: No right to buy vested Shares. Recommended.]
The Company has no right to buy vested Shares, except under the right of first refusal in section 8.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Right to buy at fair value, only on a final finding of Cause.]
If there is a finding of Cause under section 5(a)(i) or (ii), and it's final after any challenge, the Company may buy the Founder's vested Shares at their fair market value on
the date of that finding, set by an independent appraiser the Disinterested Directors choose. It must decide within 90 days after the finding is final.
[END OPTION B]

Before the Company or anyone acting with it buys Shares from the Founder under this Agreement other than at the Repurchase Price, the buyer shall disclose to the Founder all
material facts it knows about the Company's business, financing and value.

**8. Transfers.**

(a) The Founder may not transfer Unvested Shares, except to a trust or family member for estate planning, where the holder agrees in writing to be bound by this Agreement
and the Founder keeps the right to vote those Shares. A transfer that breaks this Agreement is void, and the Company shall not record it.

(b) **Right of first refusal.** Before transferring vested Shares for value, the Founder shall give the Company written notice of the buyer, the number of shares, the price and
the other terms. The Company may buy all or part of those shares on the same terms by notice within 30 days. The Disinterested Directors may assign that right to the other
stockholders in proportion to their holdings. Any shares not bought may be sold to the named buyer on those terms within the following 60 days, if the buyer agrees to be bound by
this section. After that, the Founder must offer again.

(c) Paragraph (b) does not apply to a transfer to the Company, a transfer in a Change of Control, or an estate planning transfer of vested Shares that meets (a). Paragraph (b) ends when the Company's
stock is first publicly traded or on a Change of Control. Paragraph (a) continues while any Unvested Shares remain.

(d) The Company may record these restrictions on any stock certificate and in the statement it sends for uncertificated shares, as follows: "These shares are subject to transfer
restrictions, a repurchase right and a right of first refusal under an agreement with the Company, a copy of which the Company will provide without charge on request. They have not been
registered under the Securities Act of 1933 and may not be sold without registration or an exemption."

**9. Public offering.** In the Company's first registered public offering, if the managing underwriter asks and all officers and directors and holders of 1% or more are bound by the same
terms, the Founder won't sell any Shares for up to 180 days after the offering.

**10. Founder's representations.** The Founder represents that: the Founder is buying for the Founder's own account for investment and not to resell; the Founder is a director, officer or
other person with access to all the Company's information, and has been able to ask questions; the Founder can bear the risk of losing the whole investment; the Shares are not registered and
cannot be sold without registration or an exemption, and there may never be a market for them; and the Founder lives at the address shown in section 14.

**11. Tax.** The Founder understands that section 83 of the Internal Revenue Code applies to the Shares. If any Shares are subject to forfeiture, the Founder alone is responsible for filing an
election under section 83(b) with the Internal Revenue Service **within 30 days after the Purchase Date,** even if the Company offers to help, and for giving the Company a copy. The Founder has
been advised to get the Founder's own tax advice and isn't relying on the Company for it. If the Company must withhold tax on the Shares, the Founder shall pay it or arrange to pay it.

**12. Service.** Nothing here is a promise of continued Service. The board may remove the Founder from any office, as the law and the Company's bylaws allow. That doesn't affect the Founder's rights
under this Agreement or any other contract, or the Founder's rights as a stockholder or director. Wages, expenses and any severance are separate from this Agreement, and are payable as the law and
any other agreement require.

**13. Protected rights.** Nothing in this Agreement limits a right that the law says cannot be waived, or stops the Founder from communicating with a government agency, giving truthful testimony or
disclosing what the law protects.

**14. General.** This Agreement is governed by the laws of {{GOVERNING_STATE}}, without its conflict of law rules. Notices are in writing and go to the addresses here or a later one given by notice: the
Company at {{COMPANY_ADDRESS}}; the Founder at {{FOUNDER_ADDRESS}}. Notice by email counts if the recipient confirms it or if a copy also goes by a tracked courier. This Agreement binds successors and permitted
assignees. The Company may assign its rights only as this Agreement allows, and to a successor in a Change of Control. Only a writing signed by the Founder and the Company, and approved by the Disinterested
Directors, can amend it. If a court finds a term unenforceable, the rest stands. This is the whole agreement on its subject, along with the Founders' Agreement, if any, which controls if the two conflict while it's in
effect. Disputes follow the steps in the Founders' Agreement if there is one. It may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

{{COMPANY_NAME}}

By: ______________________________ (an officer other than the Founder, if there is one)

______________________________
{{FOUNDER_NAME}}

**Exhibit A: Stock power.** For value received, {{FOUNDER_NAME}} transfers to {{COMPANY_NAME}} or its permitted assignee the Shares described in a repurchase notice under the Founder Stock Purchase Agreement, and appoints the
Company's Secretary to record the transfer. This power may be used only for a repurchase that Agreement allows.

______________________________ (signed, undated)

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vesting | A, four years with a cliff; B, credit; C, none | **A, or B if a founder has already worked for a real period.** A cliff means nothing vests until that date. Law firm guides describe this schedule as typical, though no data measures it ([document map](../../docs/design/document-set-and-law.md)). Vesting protects the founders who stay from someone leaving with everything, and investors may ask for it anyway. **C** avoids the 83(b) election, but it leaves no answer when a co-founder walks at month three, and investors may ask to re-vest | Whether a later re-vest counts as a new transfer for tax |
| Does board service count as Service? | Exclude; include | **Exclude.** A director who no longer works in the business shouldn't vest, and the removal protection in 4(a) handles the unfair case. Including it means a founder can keep vesting by keeping a seat, and it makes every fight about the seat | A founder who stays as a paid adviser |
| Repurchase price | Paid price, or lower of paid price and fair value | **Lower of the two,** as drafted. Unvested stock isn't really earned yet, and it matches custom | A founder who paid real value, for example by contributing technology |
| Automatic or optional repurchase | Automatic unless directors decide otherwise; company must act | **Automatic.** A company in a fight forgets deadlines, and if the option lapses, the shares stay with the person who left. But it does take a decision to waive: the people left decide | Repurchase limits: [7-106-401][t7]; [8 Del. C. 160][d8s5] |
| Acceleration | A, removal and sale; B, sale only with a termination; C, sale alone; D, none | **A.** The risk this addresses is being removed just before a cliff or a sale. **B** is what law firm guides describe investors as accepting, though no data measures it, so you may be asked to drop 4(a) at a priced round. **C** gives a buyer no way to keep the founder, so expect resistance. **D** leaves a removed founder only what has vested. Set the months in 4(a): **12** protects against a removal just before the cliff, and a smaller number is easier to defend to an investor. It's a judgment call | Whether investors will accept it; interaction with severance and [26 U.S.C. 409A](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s409A/) |
| No release required | Keep; require a release | **Keep at formation.** A release condition gives a hostile board something to bargain with when the founder has the least power. Investors may ask for it | What a release could lawfully waive: fraud rights can't be waived in advance ([15 U.S.C. 78cc(a)][us78cc]) and wage rights are limited |
| Cause | Narrow list with process, as drafted; broader | **Keep it narrow and add the process.** Who decides is what matters. A vague cause clause, decided by the people who want you out, is little protection. The exclusions in 5(b) stop a pretext. Note (iii) lets a board act on real misconduct through an independent investigator, which protects employees and the company too. No public founder form with this definition could be read, so this is first-principles drafting | **Whether the process is workable, and whether firing someone in order to trigger a repurchase breaches good faith. That's judge-made and needs a lawyer** |
| Good Reason | As drafted; none | **Keep it.** Without it, a board can cut a founder's pay and role until they quit, and quitting forfeits everything. The notice and cure steps follow the pattern in the section 409A rules, including notice within 90 days of the condition first existing, which your own tax professional should confirm | [26 CFR 1.409A-1(n)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.409A-1) |
| Vested shares (section 7) | A, no right to buy; B, fair value on a final serious finding | **A.** Earned stock stays earned. If investors insist on B, hold to these limits: fair value, independent appraisal, and only for crime or fraud. A repurchase of vested stock below value is the harshest term here. Forfeiture only on a for-cause discharge doesn't count as a substantial risk of forfeiture for tax ([1.83-3(c)(2)][e83-3]), so B shouldn't by itself delay vesting for tax, which your own tax professional should confirm | Any buy-back of vested stock. It's a securities sale: [Rule 10b-5][e10b5] and [11-51-501, -604][t11]. Delaware's own Act needs a real link to Delaware beyond incorporation ([FdG Logistics][fdg]). In a Colorado closed corporation, an officer or director buying from a holder must disclose what's material ([Van Schaack][vanschaack]) |
| Non-compete | None here | **Leave out.** For a Colorado worker, a non-compete is void except in narrow cases, and needs a separate signed notice ([8-2-113(2), (4)][t8]). The sale-of-business exception is capped for a minority owner who got equity for services ([8-2-113(3)(c)][t8]), and that cap can't be calculated at formation | A real sale of a business |
| Spouse's consent | Leave out; add | **Leave out for Colorado.** Add one if a founder lives in a community property state | Any founder outside Colorado |
| Transfer restrictions | As drafted | **Keep them, and note them.** They bind someone who didn't know only if noted on the certificate or in the required statement ([7-106-208(2), 7-106-207(2)][t7]; [202(a)][d8s6]) | Whether a future investor agreement replaces them |

**Qualified small business stock.** Founder stock can qualify if the company does, and it's acquired at original issue ([26 U.S.C. 1202(c)][us1202]). Keep the 83(b) election and purchase records. A company's significant repurchases can disqualify stock issued around them ([1202(c)(3)][us1202]), though a purchase from an employee or director on termination of services is generally disregarded ([26 CFR 1.1202-2(d)(1)][e1202-2]). The paragraph for independent contractors is reserved, so a founder who is only a consultant may not fit. A buy-back at a financing needs your own tax professional's review.

**Securities.** The sale is covered in the [initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md). Founder shares are restricted, and [Rule 144][e144] holding periods apply.

**Wages.** If the founder is an employee, final pay is due at once on discharge in Colorado ([8-4-109][t8]). This template doesn't treat unvested stock as wages. A Colorado Court of Appeals decision treated stock options as compensation under the wage law while calling a nonvested interest an expectancy ([Montemayor][montemayor]), so a lawyer should confirm how equity is treated in a dispute.

**When investors arrive.** Expect a priced round to restate vesting, add investor-signed terms on stock sales, and possibly revise sections 4 and 7. Don't sign changes without reading the [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md)'s section on investors.

## What couldn't be checked

- **Enforceability of the Cause process and the 5(d) remedy.** No Colorado or Delaware decision on terms like this was checked.
- **Whether terminating a founder to trigger a repurchase breaches good faith.** It's judge-made in both states.
- **Whether a Colorado court would treat founder stock as wages.** [Montemayor][montemayor] concerned stock options and was read through passages only.
- **What's customary** rests on general law firm guidance recorded in the [document map](../../docs/design/document-set-and-law.md), not data. Terms sold as "market" vary. No law firm's form was read or used.
- **Tax treatment of section 4(a), section 7 and the good reason steps** is for your own tax professional to review.
