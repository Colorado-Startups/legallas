# Articles of Incorporation (Colorado corporation)

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

- **Decide first.** Read the [comparison](entity-comparison.md). This template is for a Colorado C corporation. "C corporation" is the
  federal tax default for a corporation, so nothing in the articles says it.
- **Two clauses belong in the original articles:** who can act by written consent, and cumulative voting. Colorado's defaults are the
  opposite of Delaware's, and getting them later takes a shareholder vote. Before shares are issued, the board or incorporators can
  still amend without a shareholder vote ([7-110-105][t7]).
- **Filing.** You file online with the Colorado Secretary of State. The [document map][map] records the fee as $50 on 2026-11-01. The online form has
  fields for the required items, and the extra clauses here go in as an attachment. The live form wasn't opened for this template, because the site's
  terms bar robots. Check how it takes attachments.
- **A person files it, not software.** The Secretary of State's terms prohibit robots, so the product prepares this and you press submit.
- **There's no signature.** The person who causes delivery affirms under penalty of perjury that the document is the act of the company,
  that the facts are believed true and that it complies with the law ([7-90-301.5][t7]). Their name and mailing address go on the
  filing ([7-90-301(8)][t7]). Don't name anyone without their consent, including the registered agent ([7-90-701(3)][t7]).
- **After filing:** bylaws and the [incorporator action](incorporator-action.md), then the [initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md).

## The document

<!-- TEMPLATE START -->

**ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION OF {{COMPANY_NAME}}**

These Articles of Incorporation are delivered to the Colorado Secretary of State for filing under sections 7-102-101 and 7-102-102 of the
Colorado Revised Statutes.

**1. Name.** The domestic entity name of the corporation is {{COMPANY_NAME}}.

**2. Shares.** The corporation is authorized to issue {{AUTHORIZED_COMMON_SHARES}} shares of common stock, without par value. The common
stock has unlimited voting rights and is entitled to receive the net assets of the corporation on dissolution.

**3. Registered agent.** The name of the corporation's initial registered agent is {{REGISTERED_AGENT_NAME}}. The street address of the
registered agent is {{REGISTERED_AGENT_STREET_ADDRESS}}, and its mailing address is {{REGISTERED_AGENT_MAILING_ADDRESS}}. The person named
as registered agent has consented to being so appointed.

**4. Principal office.** The street address of the corporation's initial principal office is {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_STREET_ADDRESS}}, and its
mailing address is {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_MAILING_ADDRESS}}.

**5. Incorporator.** The true name and mailing address of the incorporator are {{INCORPORATOR_NAME}}, {{INCORPORATOR_MAILING_ADDRESS}}.

**6. Cumulative voting.**

[OPTION A: No cumulative voting. Recommended.]
Cumulative voting is not desired in the election of directors, and shareholders may not cumulate votes.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Keep cumulative voting.]
Shareholders may cumulate their votes in the election of directors as provided in section 7-107-209 of the Colorado Revised Statutes.
[END OPTION B]

**7. Shareholder action without a meeting.**

[OPTION A: The needed votes may act, with advance notice. Recommended for two or more founders.]
Any action required or permitted to be taken at a shareholders' meeting may be taken without a meeting if shareholders holding shares
having not less than the minimum number of votes that would be necessary to authorize or take the action at a meeting at which all of
the shares entitled to vote on it were present and voted consent to the action in writing. A consent given by fewer than all shareholders
entitled to vote takes effect only if the corporation or the shareholders proposing the action gave every shareholder entitled to vote
written notice describing the proposed action at least {{CONSENT_NOTICE_DAYS}} days before the corporation receives the last consent
necessary to take the action. A shareholder may waive that notice in writing, before or after the action. A failure to give it to a shareholder
doesn't affect the action if that shareholder waives it. The corporation or the shareholders taking the action shall also give the notice that
section 7-107-104 (5.5) of the Colorado Revised Statutes requires. This article doesn't change section 7-107-104 (1.5) of the Colorado Revised
Statutes.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: The needed votes may act, with notice only afterward. Recommended for a single founder.]
Any action required or permitted to be taken at a shareholders' meeting may be taken without a meeting if shareholders holding shares
having not less than the minimum number of votes that would be necessary to authorize or take the action at a meeting at which all of
the shares entitled to vote on it were present and voted consent to the action in writing.
[END OPTION B]

[OPTION C: Keep the default.]
Action without a meeting requires the written consent of all shareholders entitled to vote.
[END OPTION C]

**8. Removal of directors.**

[OPTION A: With or without cause. Recommended.]
Shareholders may remove a director with or without cause, as the Colorado Business Corporation Act provides.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: For cause only.]
Shareholders may remove a director only for cause.
[END OPTION B]

**9. Greater voting requirements.** Bylaws adopted by the shareholders may provide for a greater quorum or voting requirement for
shareholders or voting groups than the Colorado Business Corporation Act provides.

**10. Limit on director liability.** A director of the corporation is not liable to the corporation or its shareholders for money damages
for any action taken, or any failure to take any action, as a director, except liability for: (a) the amount of a financial benefit
received by a director to which the director is not entitled; (b) an intentional infliction of harm on the corporation or the
shareholders; (c) a violation of section 7-108-405 of the Colorado Revised Statutes; or (d) an intentional violation of criminal law. If
Colorado law later permits a further limit on director liability, a director's liability is limited to the fullest extent then permitted.
A change to this article does not affect a director's protection for an act or omission that occurred before the change.

**11. Indemnification and advancement.** The corporation shall indemnify each director and officer, and advance that person's reasonable
expenses, to the fullest extent permitted by law, and for an officer who isn't a director, to the same extent as a director.

As section 7-109-109 (4) of the Colorado Revised Statutes allows, these articles limit every right to indemnification or an advance of expenses created by or pursuant to article 109 of
title 7 of the Colorado Revised Statutes, including under sections 7-109-103 and 7-109-105 and any bylaw or contract: no individual has a right to indemnification or an advance of expenses in a
proceeding the individual starts, unless the board authorized it or it is brought to enforce these rights.

[OPTION A: Advancement applies even when the corporation is the one suing. Recommended.]
That is the only limit. An advance of expenses applies whoever else brings the proceeding, including the corporation.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Not when the corporation sues.]
In the same way, no individual has a right to an advance of expenses in a proceeding the corporation brings against that individual, if a majority of the disinterested directors approved bringing it.
[END OPTION B]

A right under this article that is in effect at the time of an act or omission may not be eliminated or impaired for that act or omission by a later change to these articles or the
bylaws.

**12. Initial directors.**

[OPTION A: The incorporator elects them. Recommended.]
The initial directors aren't named in these articles.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Name them here.]
The names and addresses of the individuals who are elected to serve as the initial directors are: {{INITIAL_DIRECTOR_NAMES_AND_ADDRESSES}}.
[END OPTION B]

**13. Person causing delivery.** The true name and mailing address of the individual causing this document to be delivered for filing
are {{FILER_NAME}}, {{FILER_MAILING_ADDRESS}}.

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authorized shares | Any number; the articles must state it ([7-106-101][t7]) | **10,000,000** leaves room for founders and an option pool. That's a judgment call. Colorado has no franchise tax on authorized shares ([comparison](entity-comparison.md#cost-to-form-and-keep-up)), so a larger number costs nothing yearly. A later increase needs a shareholder vote on at least 30 days' notice at a meeting ([7-107-105][t7]) and a separate class vote ([7-110-104(1)(a)][t7]) | Whether your planned cap table fits |
| Par value | None, or a stated par ([7-102-102(2)(b)(IV)][t7]) | **None.** Par does little in Colorado: shares may be issued below par unless the articles or bylaws say otherwise ([7-106-202(6)][t7]). | Only if an investor's form assumes par |
| Cumulative voting | A, opt out; B, keep | **A.** Without this sentence, cumulative voting is mandatory ([7-102-102(3)][t7]), and then electing or removing directors by consent needs every shareholder, even with article 7 ([7-107-104(1.5)][t7]). If a smaller founder needs an assured seat, give it by a [voting agreement](founders-agreement.md) ([7-107-302][t7]), which is specifically enforceable and easier to tailor. **Keep it (B)** only if there's no agreement and a holder of about 25% or more must keep a seat on a three-seat board. Know what you give up: with it off, a director loses the protection that enough votes to have elected them can block removal ([7-108-108(3)][t7]) | How it interacts with any seats promised to investors |
| Written consent | A, needed votes with advance notice; B, needed votes with notice afterward; C, everyone | **A for two or more founders, with 10 days' notice.** A majority can still act without a meeting, and nobody finds out after the fact. **The risk in A:** a missed notice could void a corporate action unless the shareholder waives it, so send it to everyone and keep proof. It also sits beside the statute's rule that a consent takes effect when the last one arrives ([7-107-104(2)(b)(I)][t7]). **B for one founder.** C gives every holder a veto over acting without a meeting, and a meeting is then the route, on 10 to 60 days' notice ([7-107-105][t7]). The consent clause only works if it's "expressly provided for" ([7-107-104(1)(b)][t7]). Investors at a priced round may ask to drop the advance notice | **Whether the advance notice condition in A is valid.** The text allows provisions regulating shareholder powers ([7-102-102(2)(b)(III)][t7]), but no decision on such a condition was found |
| Removing directors | A, with or without cause; B, for cause only | **A.** Cause-only ([7-108-108(1)][t7]) strongly protects a sitting founder-director, but it also entrenches a director who has behaved badly, and it can't be undone without an amendment. Colorado allows it. Delaware generally doesn't for an ordinary board ([8 Del. C. 141(k)][d8s4]). Investors may refuse it, though no data measures how often. Protect a founder's seat by voting agreement instead | Removing a director by consent: [7-108-108(4)][t7] says at a meeting, while [7-107-104(1), (1.5)][t7] appear to allow consent. No decision found |
| Greater voting requirements | Keep article 9, or add a specific supermajority in the articles | **Keep article 9.** It lets shareholders add a requirement in the bylaws later ([7-107-208(1)][t7]) without changing the articles. Don't add a specific supermajority at formation without a lawyer: it's easy to create a deadlock | Any supermajority |
| Director liability | Keep article 10, or delete it | **Keep.** It tracks the statute's list ([7-102-102(2)(d)][t7]). Colorado's statute names directors only; no equivalent for officers was found. The liability standard itself is in [7-108-402][t7] | Whether to change the standard of liability under [7-108-402(1)(c)][t7] |
| Indemnification | A, advance even when the corporation sues; B, not then; or leave to bylaws | **A, in the articles.** A provision obligating indemnification "to the fullest extent permitted by law" also obligates advancement unless it says otherwise ([7-109-109(1)(b)][t7]), which matters most to a founder in a dispute. Only the articles can limit these rights ([7-109-109(4)][t7]), so the carve-out for suits a person starts belongs here, and it's what makes the [bylaws](colorado-corporation-bylaws.md) sections 5.2 and 5.4 work. Use B only if the bylaws use B. Advances still need a written affirmation and undertaking ([7-109-104][t7]). Without the same-extent words, non-director officers could get more than directors ([7-109-107][t7]) | Scope for officers; any insurance |
| Initial directors | A, incorporator elects; B, name them | **A.** Names stay out of the public record, and the board can be set right after filing ([7-102-105][t7]) | None |
| Preferred stock now | Leave out, or add board-set classes ([7-106-102][t7]) | **Leave out.** Add it when there's a real financing. A new class prior or equal to the common needs a separate common vote, and that vote can't be removed by making shares nonvoting ([7-110-104(1)(f), (4)][t7]). Board-set series may be a route around it, because articles of amendment setting them are effective without shareholder action ([7-106-102(4)][t7]). That's a large power to hand a future board | Whether board-set series avoid the class vote; how to protect the common if they do |

**Name.** The name must contain "corporation", "incorporated", "company", "limited" or an abbreviation of one, and must be distinguishable on the
Secretary of State's records ([7-90-601][t7]). Check availability first. A name that clears the Secretary of State can still infringe a
trademark.

**Registered agent.** An individual here, or an entity in good standing with a usual place of business here, and the company itself can
serve if it qualifies ([7-90-701][t7]). The agent's address becomes public, so many founders use a service or an office.
