# Bylaws (Delaware corporation)

>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

- **Start with the [certificate](delaware-corporation-certificate.md).** Bylaws can't contradict it or the law ([109(b)][d8s1]). These
  bylaws rely on its articles: Sixth (the board can amend bylaws, except one that says the board may not change it), Seventh (no written
  ballot), Ninth (indemnification and advancement, with its limit for a proceeding a person starts), Twelfth (the number of directors) and,
  if you keep it, Tenth (advance notice of stockholder consents).
- **Who adopts them, and when.** The incorporator, or the board before the company has been paid for any stock ([109(a)][d8s1]). These
  templates use the [incorporator action](incorporator-action.md). **Do it before any stock is issued.** Article 7's transfer limits don't bind
  earlier shares unless the holder signed an agreement containing them or voted for them ([202(b)][d8s6]).
- **Authorize shares without certificates separately.** Section 6.2 assumes a board resolution ([158][d8s5]). The
  [initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md) has one.
- **What Delaware bylaws can't do.** They can't require notice before stockholders act by consent, because only the certificate can
  restrict consent ([228(a)][d8s7]). They can't change who removes a director or the vote to do it ([141(k)][d8s4]). They can't give a
  director more or less than one vote; only the certificate can ([141(d)][d8s4]). They can't make a stockholder pay the company's legal
  fees over an internal claim, or bar Delaware's courts ([109(b)][d8s1], [115(a)][d8s1]).
- **A board can change bylaws quickly.** Article Sixth gives it the power, except over a bylaw that says the board may not change it. Section
  11.2 Option A only slows a change; Option C bars one. A protection a founder relies on also belongs in the certificate or a contract. A promise of a seat goes in a
  [voting agreement](founders-agreement.md) ([218(c)][d8s7]). Severance and vesting go in that founder's own agreements.
- **Two seats can't act against either director.** Quorum and the vote both need both ([141(b)][d8s4], Section 3.11). On a two-seat board, the board can't
  remove a director from an office, freeze that director's authority or start a misconduct review without that director's own vote. Use three seats, or
  have a lawyer add more than one vote per director to the certificate ([141(d)][d8s4]). The note on Section 3.2 has the numbers and the fallback.
- **This isn't the [Colorado bylaws](colorado-corporation-bylaws.md) with the words changed.** Delaware has no statutory meeting demand for
  10% holders, no statutory notice period for board meetings, no oppression ground and no fair-value buyout, and it tests repurchases
  differently. Where that could mislead, the notes say so. See the [comparison](entity-comparison.md#when-founders-disagree).
- **A priced round may change these.** Investors may ask to rewrite the board, transfer and notice sections. No data measures how often, so this and every
  other statement here about investors is a judgment call. Decide now, knowing that.
- **Words.** Delaware says stockholder and certificate of incorporation where Colorado says shareholder and articles of incorporation.
- **Skipping the annual meeting has a limit.** With no date set, if there is no meeting or consent electing directors "for a period of 13 months after the latest to occur of the organization of the corporation, its last annual meeting or the last action by written consent to elect directors in lieu of an annual meeting", the Court of Chancery may order one on any stockholder's or director's application ([211(c)][d8s7], read 2026-11-07).

**BYLAWS OF {{COMPANY_NAME}}**

A Delaware corporation (the "Corporation"). In these bylaws, the "General Corporation Law" means the General Corporation Law of Delaware.

### Article 1. Offices, notices and signatures

**1.1 Offices.** The Corporation must keep a registered office and a registered agent in Delaware. The board may change either by making the
required filing. The board sets the Corporation's principal place of business, which need not be in Delaware.

**1.2 Notice to stockholders.** The Corporation may give a stockholder notice in writing to the mailing address in its records, by email to
the email address in its records, or by another form of electronic transmission the stockholder has consented to. Notice is given as section
232 of the General Corporation Law provides: if mailed, when deposited in the U.S. mail, postage prepaid; if sent by courier, at the earlier
of when it is received and when it is left at the address; and if sent by email, when directed to the email address. An email notice must
include a prominent legend that it is an important notice regarding the Corporation. The Corporation may not use email for a stockholder who
has objected to it, and must stop using an electronic transmission when section 232(e) requires, after two consecutive notices could not be
delivered that way.

**1.3 Notice to directors.** Notice to a director may be given in person, by telephone, by courier, or by email to the address the director last
gave the Corporation. It is given when delivered in person or by telephone, when left at the director's address, or when sent to that email address.

**1.4 Documents and signatures.** Except for the documents listed in section 116(b) of the General Corporation Law, a document under these
bylaws may be in electronic form, may be signed by a manual, facsimile, conformed or electronic signature, and may be delivered as section 116
allows. The Corporation's records may be kept electronically if they can be converted into clearly legible paper form within a reasonable time.

### Article 2. Stockholders

**2.1 Annual meeting.** Unless directors are elected by consent in place of an annual meeting under Section 2.11(k), the Corporation must hold an
annual meeting of stockholders to elect directors, on a date and at a time the board sets. Other proper business may be conducted at it. Failing
to hold it does not affect otherwise valid corporate acts.

**2.2 Special meetings.**

(a) The board, the chair of the board or the chief executive officer may call a special meeting.

(b) Only business within the purposes described in the notice may be conducted at a special meeting.

(c) If the Corporation has no directors in office, any officer, any stockholder, or an executor, administrator, trustee, guardian or other
fiduciary of a stockholder may call a special meeting.

[OPTION A: Holders of a stated percentage may call a meeting. Recommended for two or more founders.]
(d) Holders of record of at least {{SPECIAL_MEETING_PERCENT}} percent of the votes entitled to be cast on a matter may also call a special meeting
on that matter, by one or more signed requests delivered to the Secretary that state the purpose of the meeting.

(e) The Secretary must give notice of the meeting under Section 2.4 within {{SPECIAL_MEETING_NOTICE_DAYS}} days after the Corporation receives
requests from enough holders, for a date no more than sixty days after the notice. If the Secretary does not, the holders who made the requests
may give the notice themselves, for a meeting at the Corporation's principal place of business. The Corporation must give them the information
from the stock ledger they need to do so, and must pay the reasonable cost.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Only those named above. Recommended for a single founder.]
(d) No one else may call a special meeting.
[END OPTION B]

**2.3 Place and remote participation.** The board sets the place of each meeting, in or out of Delaware. If the board sets none, the meeting is held
at the Corporation's principal place of business. The board may, in its sole discretion, let stockholders and proxy holders take part by remote
communication, or hold the meeting only by remote communication, if the Corporation takes the measures section 211(a)(2) of the General Corporation
Law requires: reasonable measures to verify that each remote participant is a stockholder or proxy holder, reasonable measures to give each a
reasonable opportunity to participate and vote, including to read or hear the proceedings substantially at the same time they occur, and a record of
any vote or other action taken remotely.

**2.4 Notice of meetings.**

(a) The Corporation must give each stockholder entitled to vote at the meeting, as of the record date for notice, notice of each annual and special
meeting no fewer than ten and no more than sixty days before the meeting, unless the General Corporation Law sets a different period.

(b) The notice must state the place, if any, date and hour of the meeting; the means of remote communication, if any; the record date for voting, if
it differs from the record date for notice; for a special meeting, the purposes of the meeting; and anything else the law requires.

(c) If a meeting is adjourned to another time or place, new notice is not needed if the time, the place, if any, and the means of remote communication,
if any, are announced at the meeting, are displayed during the time scheduled for the meeting on the same electronic network used for remote participation,
or are stated in the notice of the meeting. If the adjournment is for more than thirty days, the Corporation must give notice of the adjourned meeting to
each stockholder of record entitled to vote. If a new record date for voting is fixed, the board must also fix a new record date for notice, and the
Corporation must give notice to each stockholder of record entitled to vote as of that date.

**2.5 Waiver of notice.** A written waiver signed by the person entitled to notice, or a waiver by that person by electronic transmission, given before or
after the time stated in it, is equivalent to notice. Attending a meeting waives notice of it, unless the person attends for the express purpose of objecting,
at the beginning of the meeting, to the transaction of any business because the meeting is not lawfully called or convened. A waiver need not state the
business or purpose of the meeting, unless these bylaws require it.

**2.6 Record date.**

(a) **Meetings.** The board may fix a record date for notice of a meeting. It may not come before the date of the board's resolution, and must be no more than
sixty and no fewer than ten days before the meeting. It is also the record date for voting, unless the board decides when it fixes the date that a later date,
on or before the meeting, applies to voting. If the board fixes none, the record date for notice and voting is the close of business on the day before notice
is given or, if notice is waived, the day before the meeting. A record date applies to any adjournment, unless the board fixes a new one.

(b) **Consents.** Section 2.11(h) sets the record date for action without a meeting.

(c) **Dividends and other action.** For a dividend, a distribution or any other lawful action, the board may fix a record date that does not come before the date
of its resolution and is no more than sixty days before the action. If it fixes none, the record date is the close of business on the day the board adopts the
resolution.

**2.7 Stockholder list.** No later than the tenth day before each meeting, the Corporation must prepare a complete list of the stockholders entitled to vote at the
meeting, in alphabetical order, showing each stockholder's address and number of registered shares. If the record date for voting is fewer than ten days before the
meeting, the list must show the stockholders entitled to vote as of the tenth day before the meeting. For ten days ending on the day before the meeting, the list must
be open to any stockholder, for any purpose germane to the meeting, either on a reasonably accessible electronic network, with the access information given in the
notice of the meeting, or during ordinary business hours at the Corporation's principal place of business.

**2.8 Quorum.** Holders of a majority of the shares entitled to vote, present in person or represented by proxy, are a quorum. Where a class or series votes separately,
holders of a majority of the outstanding shares of that class or series, present in person or represented by proxy, are a quorum for that vote. A share present for any
purpose counts toward the quorum for the rest of the meeting, and for any adjournment for which no new record date is fixed. At a meeting the Court of Chancery orders under
section 211(c) of the General Corporation Law, the shares represented are a quorum, whatever these bylaws say.

**2.9 Voting.**

(a) Each stockholder has one vote for each share, unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise.

(b) If a quorum is present, the affirmative vote of a majority of the shares present in person or represented by proxy and entitled to vote on the matter is the act of the
stockholders, unless the law, the certificate of incorporation or these bylaws require a different vote. Where a class or series votes separately, the same rule applies within
that class or series. Section 3.6 sets the vote to remove a director.

(c) Directors are elected by a plurality of the votes of the shares present in person or represented by proxy and entitled to vote on the election. Elections need not be by
written ballot. Stockholders may cumulate votes only if the certificate of incorporation adopts cumulative voting.

**2.10 Proxies.** A stockholder may authorize another person to act by proxy, at a meeting or on a consent, in any way section 212 of the General Corporation Law allows, including by
a signed document or by an electronic transmission from which it can be determined that the stockholder authorized it. A proxy may not be voted or acted on more than three years after
its date, unless it provides for a longer period. A proxy is irrevocable only if it says so, and only for as long as it is coupled with an interest sufficient in law to support an
irrevocable power.

**2.11 Action without a meeting.**

(a) **The right to act by consent.** Unless the certificate of incorporation provides otherwise, any action that must or may be taken at an annual or special meeting of stockholders may
be taken without a meeting, without prior notice and without a vote, if consents setting out the action are signed by holders of outstanding stock having at least the minimum number of votes
that would be needed to take it at a meeting at which all shares entitled to vote on it were present and voted, and are delivered to the Corporation as this Section requires. Under section
228 of the General Corporation Law only the certificate of incorporation can restrict this, so nothing in these bylaws requires notice before a consent or delays one.

(b) **The certificate's notice.** If the certificate of incorporation requires advance notice of action by consent (see its article Tenth, if kept), a consent takes effect only as that article
provides.

(c) **Form.** A consent must be set out in writing or in an electronic transmission, and be signed.

(d) **Delivery.** A consent must be delivered: (1) to the Corporation's principal place of business; (2) to an officer or agent having custody of the book in which proceedings of stockholder
meetings are recorded; (3) to the registered office in Delaware, by hand or by certified or registered mail, return receipt requested; or (4) by electronic transmission to {{CONSENTS_EMAIL}},
which the Corporation designates as its information processing system for receiving consents. A consent delivered under (1), (2) or (3) must be reproduced and delivered in paper form. A consent
delivered under (4) must set out, or come with, information that lets the Corporation determine the date of delivery and the identity of the person giving it. A consent given by proxy must also
comply with section 212(c)(2) and (3) of the General Corporation Law.

(e) **Record of delivery.** The Secretary must record the date and time each consent is delivered. No officer may delay or refuse that record.

(f) **Time limit.** A consent has no effect unless consents signed by enough holders are delivered within sixty days of the first date on which a consent is delivered.

(g) **Later effect and revocation.** A person signing a consent may provide that it takes effect at a future time, including on an event, no later than sixty days after the person so provides,
if the Corporation receives evidence of that provision. A person who is not a stockholder of record when signing may give a valid consent only if a stockholder of record on the record date.
Unless it says otherwise, a consent may be revoked before it takes effect.

(h) **Record date.** The board may fix a record date for a consent. It may not come before the date of the board's resolution, and must be no more than ten days after that date. If the board fixes
none and no prior board action is required, the record date is the first date on which a signed consent setting out the action is delivered. If the board fixes none and prior board action is required,
it is the close of business on the day the board adopts the resolution taking that action.

(i) **Word to the directors.** Within one business day after the Corporation first receives a consent on an action, the Secretary must send a copy to each director. This is not a condition of any
consent, and it does not delay one.

(j) **Notice afterward.** If an action is taken by less than unanimous consent, the Corporation must give prompt notice of it to the stockholders who, as of the record date for the action, did not
consent and would have been entitled to notice of a meeting on the action. The Corporation must give that notice within two business days after the action takes effect and send a copy to each director
at the same time. This time limit is not a condition of the action.

(k) **In place of an annual meeting.** Stockholders may elect directors by consent. A consent that is less than unanimous takes the place of an annual meeting only if all the directorships to which
directors could be elected at an annual meeting held when the action takes effect are vacant and are filled by the action.

**2.12 Running a meeting.** The chair of the board presides at stockholder meetings or, if there is none or the chair is absent, the chief executive officer. If neither is present, holders of a majority
of the votes present choose who presides. The Secretary keeps the minutes. Holders of a majority of the votes present may adjourn a meeting, with or without a quorum. The person presiding may not adjourn a
meeting without their approval.

**3.3 Qualifications.** Each director must be a natural person. A director need not be a stockholder or a Delaware resident.

**3.4 Election and term.** Directors are elected at each annual meeting, or by consent under Section 2.11(k). Each director holds office until a successor is elected and qualified, or until the director's
earlier resignation or removal. A decrease in the number of directors does not shorten a sitting director's term.

**3.5 Resignation.** A director may resign at any time by notice in writing or by electronic transmission to the Corporation. The resignation takes effect when delivered, unless it states a later date or
an effective date set by an event.

**3.6 Removal.**

(a) The holders of a majority of the shares then entitled to vote at an election of directors may remove any director or the entire board, with or without cause. This means a majority of those shares, not a
majority of the shares present at a meeting.

(b) These exceptions apply only if the Corporation's documents set them up: (1) if the board is classified, stockholders may remove a director only for cause, unless the certificate of incorporation provides
otherwise; (2) if the certificate of incorporation adopts cumulative voting and less than the entire board is being removed, a director may not be removed without cause if the votes against removal would be enough
to elect that director if cumulatively voted at an election of the entire board or of that director's class; and (3) if holders of a class or series elect a director under the certificate of incorporation, the vote
on removing that director without cause is the vote of that class or series.

(c) The board may not remove a director. Only the stockholders, or the Court of Chancery under section 225(c) of the General Corporation Law, may do so.

(d) Stockholders may remove a director by consent under Section 2.11. Nothing in this Section delays or limits that. Only the certificate of incorporation can.

**3.11 Quorum and voting.**

(a) A quorum is a majority of the total number of directors.

(b) The vote of a majority of the directors present at a meeting at which a quorum is present is the act of the board, unless the law, the certificate of incorporation or these bylaws require a greater vote.

[OPTION A: No greater vote. Recommended.]
(c) A tied vote fails. No director has a second or casting vote.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Two-thirds for the matters that decide a dispute. Needs Option A in Section 3.9.]
(c) A tied vote fails. No director has a second or casting vote. The affirmative vote of two-thirds of the directors in office is required to act on a matter in Section 3.9(c)(2), (5), (7) or (8).
[END OPTION B]

(d) A director who disagrees with an action may have the dissent entered in the minutes. Section 174 of the General Corporation Law says how a director avoids liability for an unlawful dividend or repurchase.

(e) If the certificate of incorporation gives a director more or less than one vote on a matter, each majority or other proportion of the directors in these bylaws means that proportion of the directors' votes on that matter.

**3.12 Action without a meeting.** Any action that may be taken at a meeting of the board or a committee may be taken without a meeting if all its members consent in writing or by electronic transmission. A consent may be documented, signed and delivered as
section 116 of the General Corporation Law allows. A person, whether or not then a director, may provide that a consent takes effect at a future time, including on an event, no later than sixty days after so providing, if the person is then a director and has not
revoked it. A consent may be revoked before it takes effect. After the action is taken, the consents must be filed with the minutes.

**3.13 Committees.**

(a) The board may designate committees of one or more directors. A committee has the authority the board's resolution gives it, but it may not approve, adopt or recommend to stockholders any action or matter, other than the election or removal of directors, that
the General Corporation Law expressly requires to be submitted to stockholders for approval, and it may not adopt, amend or repeal any bylaw.

(b) A majority of the directors serving on a committee is a quorum, and the vote of a majority of the members present at a meeting with a quorum is the committee's act. The notice, waiver and consent rules of this Article apply to committees.

**3.14 Conflicts of interest.**

(a) A director, an officer or a controlling stockholder with an interest in an act or transaction involving the Corporation must disclose the material facts to the board before the board acts.

(b) The Corporation may go ahead with an act or transaction between it and a director or officer, or an entity in which a director or officer is a director, stockholder, partner, manager, member or officer or has a financial interest, only by one of these routes:

(1) with the material facts disclosed or known to all members of the board or a committee, the board or committee, in good faith and without gross negligence, authorizes it by the affirmative votes of a majority of the disinterested directors then serving on it, even if they are
fewer than a quorum. If a majority of the directors are not disinterested, a committee of two or more directors, each of whom the board has determined to be disinterested, must approve it or recommend it for approval;

(2) an informed, uncoerced, affirmative vote of a majority of the votes cast by the disinterested stockholders approves or ratifies it; or

(3) it is fair as to the Corporation and its stockholders.

(c) A transaction with a controlling stockholder or control group, as section 144 of the General Corporation Law defines them, must follow a route section 144(b) or (c) provides.

(d) Interested directors may be counted toward a quorum. "Disinterested" has the meaning in section 144.

**3.15 Looking into a report of misconduct.**

(a) If the board receives a credible report that a director or officer has engaged in fraud, dishonesty, a serious breach of duty or a serious violation of law, it must appoint a committee of one or more disinterested directors to look into it. If there is no disinterested director, the
board must hire independent counsel to report to the stockholders. If the board cannot do either because the person concerned is needed for a quorum or a vote (Section 3.11), any other director may tell the stockholders what was reported, so they can act under Section 2.11 or 3.6, and must send the person concerned a copy.

(b) The committee may hire lawyers and accountants at the Corporation's expense and may see any record of the Corporation.

(c) The person concerned does not sit on the committee. The person must get a fair description of the concerns and a chance to respond before the committee reports a finding against that person.

(d) The committee reports to the board. If the person concerned is a director, action by the board on the report needs, in addition to the vote in Section 3.11(b), the affirmative vote of a majority of the other directors in office.

(e) This Section does not take away any right under Section 3.16, Article 5 or Article 9.

## How these bylaws work in a fight

These bylaws set the process. They slow a bad move and give people notice. The result is decided mostly by who holds the votes, and by the [certificate](delaware-corporation-certificate.md), the [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md), the [stock agreement](founder-stock-purchase.md) and, at a raise, the investor documents.

| Fight | What these bylaws do | What they don't do |
|---|---|---|
| **Founders fall out** | Notice of Sensitive Matters (3.9), no removing a founder who is a director from an office without the other directors, or on two seats without that founder's own vote (3.11, 4.5), tied votes fail (3.11), directors keep their information (3.16), a holder at the threshold can call a meeting (2.2) | Break a 50/50 deadlock. Delaware has only a custodian ([226(a)][d8s7]), joint-venture dissolution ([273][d8s10]) and a court-ordered annual meeting ([211(c)][d8s7]). That's the founders' agreement |
| **A founder is removed** | As an officer: notice and a chance to speak (3.9(d)), a majority of the other directors (4.5), removal isn't cause (4.6). As a director: longer meeting notice (3.6), a copy of any consent within a day (2.11(i)). Either way: legal fees are advanced (5.2), and records stay open while they hold shares (Article 9) | **Stop holders of a majority removing the director by consent the same day** ([141(k)][d8s4], [228(a)][d8s7]). Only article Tenth of the certificate gives notice first. Save unvested shares. Provide a buyout |
| **Investors act on a founder's misconduct** | A freeze of authority on a day's notice (3.9(e)), an independent look (3.15), a chance to respond (3.15(c)), a majority of the other directors for action on the report (3.15(d)), advancement continues (5.2) | Work over that founder's objection on a two-seat board, where it takes holders of a majority (3.6). Decide what counts as cause, or take back shares. Get a court to remove a director, except on the narrow facts in [225(c)][d8s7] |
| **Investors try to take over** | Board size and vacancies held by stockholders (3.2, 3.7), notice of new share issues (3.9(c)(7)), a holder's meeting (2.2), notice and a greater vote for board changes to these sections (11.2), transfer limits (Article 7), records (Article 9) | Stop holders with the votes, who can amend these bylaws ([109(a)][d8s1]) and remove directors, by consent. Stop a board majority rewriting them under article Sixth, unless 11.2 Option C lists them. Seats and vetoes belong in the certificate, a voting agreement ([218(c)][d8s7]) or a stockholder contract ([122(18)][d8s2]) |

## What couldn't be checked

- **Case law. None was read,** and no decision is cited, because this template was drafted from the statute alone. Delaware has many decisions on these points, and every reading here should be run against them. At least: whether a bylaw can affect the stockholders' removal power or vote; bylaws that slow a consent; who can remove an officer; whether board action without the notice a bylaw requires is void or voidable; a director's access to meetings and advice; and fees for enforcing advancement.
- **Whether a greater board vote can sit under article Sixth.** Section 11.2 Option A and Section 3.2 Option A are readings of [109][d8s1] and [141(b)][d8s4] together. The certificate grants the board the power except over a bylaw that says the board may not change it, and no text says a bylaw can otherwise restrict it, apart from [216][d8s7].
- **Article Sixth's exception and Section 11.2 Option C** read [109(a)][d8s1] as letting a certificate confer less than the whole power over bylaws, and as letting that turn on a bylaw's own words. That is a reading of the text.
- **The added majority of the other directors.** Sections 3.15(d), 4.5 and 7.2 read it as a greater vote under [141(b)][d8s4], not a change in voting power under [141(d)][d8s4]. That is a reading of the text.
- **More than one vote per director.** No such article has been drafted. That it also changes the quorum is a reading of the last sentence of [141(d)][d8s4].
- **What investors do.** Every statement here about what investors may ask for is a judgment call. No data measures it.
- **Paragraphs 2.11(i) and (j)** read as duties on the Corporation that don't restrict consent under [228][d8s7]. That is a reading of the text.
- **Longer notice of a removal meeting.** Section 3.6 Option A reads [222(b)][d8s7] as a floor that bylaws can raise, within 60 days.
- **The holders' own notice** in Section 2.2(e). [232(a)][d8s7] speaks of notice given by the corporation.
- **Listing the federal court** in Article 10 as a court "in this State" under [115(a)][d8s1] is a reading of the text.
- **When the current text applies.** The headings of sections 144, 220, 228 and 232 point to session laws on their application. Those weren't read. The 2025 changes to 144 and 220 are recent. The [comparison](entity-comparison.md#what-couldnt-be-checked) and the [document map][map] record that Delaware's Supreme Court upheld section 144's safe harbours. That opinion wasn't read here.
