# Delaware LLC or Colorado LLC

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this comparison. Fees and rules change, so every figure carries its date.

For a company run from Colorado that wants an LLC. The [entity comparison](entity-comparison.md) weighs an LLC against a corporation; this
page adds the Delaware LLC it leaves out.

## Side by side

| | Colorado LLC | Delaware LLC | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Form it** | Articles of organization, "$50.00" online | Certificate of formation, "$110.00": the statute's "$70" filing fee plus a municipality fee | [Fees][sosfees]; [Division fees][defee]; [18-1105(a)(3)][d6s11] |
| **Work in Colorado** | Nothing more | A statement of foreign entity authority, "$100.00" online | [7-90-801(1)][t7]; [fees][sosfees] |
| **Every year** | Periodic report, "$25.00" | $400 Delaware tax by June 1, plus the $25 Colorado report | [Fees][sosfees]; [18-1107(b), (c)][d6s11] |
| **Minimum yearly total** (calculated here) | **$25** | **$425** | as above |
| **Registered agents** | One, in Colorado | One in Delaware and one in Colorado | [18-104(a)][d6s1]; [7-90-701(1)][t7] |
| **Who manages** | The articles must say | The agreement says; the certificate needn't | [7-80-204(1)(e)][t7]; [18-201(a)][d6s2] |
| **The agreement** | "maximum effect to the principle of freedom of contract" | The same words, "and to the enforceability of limited liability company agreements" | [7-80-108(4)][t7]; [18-1101(b)][d6s11] |
| **Limiting duties** | Allowed if "not manifestly unreasonable"; good faith stays | Allowed; the implied covenant of good faith stays | [7-80-108(1.5), (2)(d)][t7]; [18-1101(c)][d6s11] |
| **Courts** | Colorado courts, or as the agreement says | Court of Chancery. A member who isn't a manager can't waive Delaware courts on internal affairs, except by agreeing to arbitrate | [18-109(d)][d6s1], [18-111][d6s1], [18-802][d6s8] |
| **Federal tax** | Partnership with two or more members; disregarded with one | Same | [26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)][e7701] |
| **Qualified small business stock** | Not while taxed as a partnership. Converting to a corporation starts the clock | Same | [26 U.S.C. 1202][us1202] |
| **Becoming a corporation** | Conversion, as the agreement says | Conversion "as specified in the limited liability company agreement" | [entity comparison](entity-comparison.md#converting-later); [18-216(b)][d6s2] |
| **Documents here** | [Articles](colorado-llc-articles.md), [agreement](colorado-llc-operating-agreement.md) | [Certificate](delaware-llc-certificate.md), [agreement](delaware-llc-operating-agreement.md), [one-member agreement](delaware-llc-agreement.md) | |

## What Colorado companies chose

From the [entity comparison's count](entity-comparison.md#what-sec-filings-show) of new Form D notices by Colorado issuers, 2024 Q4 to 2026 Q3,
counted 2026-11-01:

| Group | Colorado LLC | Delaware LLC |
|---|---:|---:|
| Technology and life sciences, 241 companies | 14 (6%), median $125,000 sold | 7 (3%), median $850,000 sold |
| All operating companies, 604 | 123 (20%) | 56 (9%) |

Small counts. They show what was filed, not why, and not what investors required.

## Reading it

- **Delaware costs $400 a year more** (calculated here) for a company working in Colorado, plus a second agent.
- **What it buys** is Delaware's statute, its Court of Chancery and its case law on LLC agreements. Colorado's statute uses the same
  freedom of contract words.
- **Neither is a corporation.** For a company expecting venture fund money, see the [entity comparison's recommendations](entity-comparison.md#recommendation-by-situation).
- **Profits Interest Units** in a Delaware LLC are held back with the equity plan in Legallas, so a lawyer reviews any grant.
