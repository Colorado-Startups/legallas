# Certificate of Incorporation (Delaware corporation)

> Legallas is an AI agent for legal work, not a lawyer or law firm. Using it creates no attorney-client relationship. AI can be wrong. Have your own lawyer review before you sign, file or rely on anything.
>
> No lawyer has reviewed this template.

## Before you use this

- **Decide first.** Read the [comparison](entity-comparison.md). For a company run from Colorado, Delaware adds a second state's filings.
- **You need a Delaware registered agent** with a Delaware address ([8 Del. C. 102(a)(2)][d8s1], [131][d8s3]). You don't need to be in Delaware.
- **To hold the name while you prepare,** reserve it. The Secretary of State reserves an available name "for the use of the applicant for a period of 120 days" ([8 Del. C. 102(e)][d8s1], read 2026-11-07).
- **Filing.** Upload a PDF to the Division of Corporations or mail it. Staff review each filing. The [document map][map] records the fee as starting
  at $109 on 2026-11-01. The portal has a human check (a CAPTCHA), so a person files it, or your registered agent does.
- **The incorporator signs** ([8 Del. C. 103(a)(1)][d8s1]). That person needn't be a founder, and electronic signatures work under section 103.
- **Then, in Colorado.** Before transacting business here, file a statement of foreign entity authority, about $100 ([7-90-801][t7]). Skipping it
  has a cost ([7-90-802][t7]).
- **Every March 1:** an annual report and franchise tax, filed "on or before March 1" ([8 Del. C. 502(a), 503, 504][d8c5]). See [the tax choice below](#drafting-notes).
- **A late report:** the company "shall pay the sum of $200", added to the tax ([502(a)][d8c5], read 2026-11-07).
- **Once the tax is estimated to be $5,000 or more:** estimated payments of "Forty percent of the estimated tax on June 1 of the current year", 20% by September 1 and 20% by December 1, with the rest on March 1 ([504(a)][d8c5]).
- **Also by March 1, not a tax:** an unclaimed property report, if the company holds property presumed abandoned and the owner has no known address, a last known address in Delaware, or one in a state or country that doesn't take such property. Delaware may take custody of it because of that Delaware address or, in the other cases, because the company is incorporated there ([12 Del. C. 1130, 1140 to 1142, 1144][d12c11]).
- **A refund or a lower tax:** if the tax, penalties or interest were fixed or paid erroneously or illegally, petition the Secretary of State by March 1 of the second calendar year after the tax year ([8 Del. C. 505][d8c5]).
- **After filing:** [bylaws](delaware-corporation-bylaws.md) and the [incorporator action](incorporator-action.md), then the [initial board consent](initial-board-consent.md).

**CERTIFICATE OF INCORPORATION OF {{COMPANY_NAME}}**

**First.** The name of the corporation is {{COMPANY_NAME}}.

**Second.** The address of the corporation's registered office in the State of Delaware is {{REGISTERED_OFFICE_STREET}}, in the City of
{{REGISTERED_OFFICE_CITY}}, County of {{REGISTERED_OFFICE_COUNTY}}, Delaware {{REGISTERED_OFFICE_ZIP}}. The name of its registered agent at that

**Third.** The purpose of the corporation is to engage in any lawful act or activity for which corporations may be organized under the
General Corporation Law of Delaware.

**Fourth.** The corporation is authorized to issue one class of stock, common stock. The total number of shares it may issue is
{{AUTHORIZED_COMMON_SHARES}}, each with a par value of ${{PAR_VALUE}}.

**Fifth.** The name and mailing address of the incorporator are {{INCORPORATOR_NAME}}, {{INCORPORATOR_MAILING_ADDRESS}}.

**Sixth.** The board of directors may adopt, amend or repeal the bylaws, except a bylaw that says the board may not change it. The stockholders keep their power to adopt, amend or repeal bylaws.

**Seventh.** Elections of directors need not be by written ballot unless the bylaws so provide.

**Eighth.** To the fullest extent the General Corporation Law of Delaware permits, a director or officer of the corporation is not personally
liable to the corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty as a director or officer. A change to this
article does not affect a director's or officer's protection for an act or omission that occurred before the change.

**Ninth.** The corporation shall indemnify each person who is or was a director or officer, and advance that person's expenses, to the fullest extent the General
Corporation Law of Delaware permits. These rights don't apply to a proceeding the person starts, unless the board authorized it or it is brought to enforce these rights.

[OPTION A: Advancement applies even when the corporation is the one suing. Recommended.]
That is the only limit. An advance of expenses applies whoever else brings the proceeding, including the corporation.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Not when the corporation sues.]
The corporation need not advance expenses in a proceeding it brings against the person, if a majority of the disinterested directors approved bringing it.
[END OPTION B]

A right under this article may not be eliminated or impaired by a later change to this certificate or the bylaws for an act or omission that occurred before the change.

**Tenth. Stockholder action without a meeting.**

[OPTION A: Advance notice to every stockholder. Recommended for two or more founders.]
Stockholders may act by consent without a meeting under section 228 of the General Corporation Law of Delaware. A consent signed by fewer than
all stockholders entitled to vote takes effect only if the corporation or the stockholders proposing the action gave every stockholder of record on the date of the notice a notice describing the proposed action at least {{CONSENT_NOTICE_DAYS}} days before the last consent necessary to take the action is
delivered to the corporation. If a stockholder proposing the action asks, the corporation shall send that notice to the other stockholders within 2 business days. A stockholder may waive that notice in writing, before or after the action, and a failure to give it to a stockholder doesn't affect the action if that stockholder waives it. This article is in addition to the notice section 228(e) requires.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Keep Delaware's default. Recommended for a single founder.]
Stockholders may act by consent without a meeting as section 228 of the General Corporation Law of Delaware provides.
[END OPTION B]

**Eleventh. Class votes on authorized shares.**

[OPTION A: Leave the statute's rule in place at formation. Recommended.]
Section 242(b)(2) of the General Corporation Law of Delaware applies as written.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Opt out now.]
The number of authorized shares of common stock may be increased or decreased, but not below the number then outstanding, by the affirmative
vote of the holders of a majority of the stock entitled to vote, irrespective of section 242(b)(2) of the General Corporation Law of Delaware.
[END OPTION B]

**Twelfth. Number of directors.**

[OPTION A: The bylaws set it. Recommended.]
The bylaws fix the number of directors, or say how it is fixed.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: The number is here.]
The board has {{BOARD_SIZE}} directors. Changing that number takes an amendment to this certificate.
[END OPTION B]

## Drafting notes

| Choice | Options | Recommendation and why | Where a lawyer should look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authorized shares and par | Any number; par or no par | **10,000,000 shares at $0.00001 par.** Don't choose no-par. The Division says the authorized shares method always gives the lesser tax for no-par stock, which at 10 million shares is about $85,165 a year ([method][defrcalc]). With par stock you can report issued shares and gross assets and owe as little as $400, plus the $50 report fee ([503][d8c5]). Par sets the floor on the price ([8 Del. C. 153][d8s5]), and at this par founders can pay $100 for 10 million shares | Whether a larger share count suits your plan and raises filing fees |
| The tax report | Authorized shares method; assumed par value capital method | **File the numbers.** Without a statement of issued shares and gross assets, you pay on authorized shares ([503(b)][d8c5]), and at 10 million shares the bill looks alarming | Your accountant, for gross assets |
| Written consent | A, advance notice; B, default | **A for two or more founders, with 10 days' notice.** By default the needed votes can act with no prior notice ([228(a)][d8s7]), so a founder could lose a board seat and learn of it afterward. Section 228 lets the certificate provide otherwise, and bylaws can't do this. **The risks in A:** a missed notice could void an action unless the stockholder waives it, and the notice warns the person a majority wants to remove, which matters if that person is doing harm. Investors at a priced round may ask to delete it, so also put it in the [founders' agreement](founders-agreement.md). **B for one founder** | Whether the advance notice condition is valid under section 228; how it fits a future voting agreement |
| Removing directors | Default only | **Nothing to choose here.** Stockholders may remove with or without cause. Delaware lets cause-only apply only with a classified board or cumulative voting ([141(k)][d8s4]), unlike Colorado | Whether a classified board suits your team; it's unusual at this stage |
| Cumulative voting | Leave out; adopt | **Leave out.** It applies only if the certificate adopts it ([214][d8s7]). Give a smaller founder a seat by [voting agreement](founders-agreement.md) ([218][d8s7]) | Any seat promise |
| Class vote on authorized shares | A, leave out; B, opt out | **A.** Common is the only class at formation. Financing documents often add the opt-out that [242(b)(2)][d8s8] allows, and the [document map][map] records that the NVCA and Series Seed forms do. A founder holding most of the common gives up a veto when that clause goes in, so read it then | The class vote when preferred arrives |
| Exculpation | Directors and officers; directors only; none | **Directors and officers.** [102(b)(7)][d8s1] permits both, with limits: no protection for loyalty breaches, bad faith or improper benefit, and none for officers in a claim by or for the corporation | Which officers are covered |
| Bylaw power | Give to the board, or keep with stockholders | **Give to the board.** Once the company has received payment for stock, only stockholders can amend bylaws unless the certificate says otherwise ([109(a)][d8s1]). Stockholders keep the power too. In a fight, a board can change bylaws quickly, so put anything that protects a founder in the certificate or in a contract | Bylaws a founder relies on |
| Initial directors | Leave out and let the incorporator elect them; name them | **Leave out.** Names stay out of the public filing ([102(a)(6)][d8s1], [107, 108][d8s1]) | None |
| Preferred stock the board can define later | Leave out; authorize | **Leave out.** It's added at a real financing ([151][d8s5]) | Financing documents |
| Written ballot | Keep the Seventh article; delete it | **Keep.** Without it, elections of directors must be by written ballot ([211(e)][d8s7]) | None |
| Indemnification | A, advance even when the corporation sues; B, not then | **A.** It makes advancement mandatory, which it isn't by default ([145(e)][d8s4]), and protects past acts from a later change ([145(f)][d8s4]). That matters most to a founder in a dispute. Bylaws can't cut below the certificate ([109(b)][d8s1]), so the limits are here, and they're what make the [bylaws](delaware-corporation-bylaws.md) sections 5.2 and 5.5 work. Use B only if the bylaws use B | Whether a former officer's rights need more |
| Number of directors | A, bylaws; B, in the certificate | **A.** Under B, a change needs an amendment, so a board or a bare majority can't quickly change the board's size ([141(b)][d8s4]). Use B only if a founder relies on the size. It's also where more than one vote per director would go ([141(d)][d8s4]), which can help on a two-seat board. A lawyer should draft that | A board where one person's vote decides |
| Company name | | It must include a word like "Corporation", "Company" or "Incorporated", or an abbreviation ([102(a)(1)][d8s1]), and be distinguishable on Delaware's records | Trademark check is separate |

**Internal law stays Delaware.** After foreign authority in Colorado, Delaware law still governs internal affairs and owner liability ([7-90-805(4)][t7]).
Colorado's wage and non-compete laws still apply to people working here. See the [comparison](entity-comparison.md#when-founders-disagree).

## What couldn't be checked
