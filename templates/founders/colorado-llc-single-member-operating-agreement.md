## Before you use this

| | |
|---|---|
| **It fits** | A Colorado LLC with exactly one member. By default the IRS disregards it, so its income goes on the owner's return ([26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(1)(ii)][e7701]). Section 6.3 also covers electing corporate tax treatment |
| **It doesn't fit** | Two or more members: use the [two-member agreement](colorado-llc-operating-agreement.md). A Delaware LLC: use the [Delaware one-member agreement](delaware-llc-agreement.md). A company about to take venture fund money: see [the comparison](entity-comparison.md) |
| **You already have one** | For a one-member LLC, three things count as part of the operating agreement: any writing the member signs about the company's affairs, any written agreement between the member and the company, and, if a manager who isn't the member runs it, even an oral agreement between them ([7-80-102(11)(b)][t7]). This document makes it deliberate. Section 4.3 says which ones change it |
| **Without it, death can end the company** | The estate gets only a transferee's powers ([7-80-704(1)][t7]). Every holder of the last member's interest may agree to admit a member ([7-80-701(2)][t7]). Otherwise the company dissolves on day 91 ([7-80-801(1)(c)][t7]). Article 9 admits a Successor at once and stretches the window to one year, the most the Act allows ([7-80-108(2)(d.5)][t7]) |
| **The liability shield** | Courts apply Colorado's corporate veil-piercing case law to LLC members ([7-80-107(1)][t7]). Skipped formalities alone aren't a ground ([7-80-107(2)][t7]). Article 5 lists the habits that keep the Company separate from its owner |
| **Match your articles** | The articles must say whether members or managers manage ([7-80-204(1)(e)][t7]). Pick the same answer in Section 3.1 |
| **Match your estate papers** | Name the Successor in Section 9.1. Leave the Units to the same person in your will, trust or transfer-on-death papers, or Section 9.2 runs two steps instead of one |
| **Delete** | Every option you don't choose, with its markers, and everything outside the template markers |

# Operating Agreement of {{COMPANY_NAME}}

This Operating Agreement is made as of {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}} by {{MEMBER_NAME}} (the "Member") and by {{COMPANY_NAME}}, a Colorado limited
liability company (the "Company").

## 1. The Company

**1.1 Formation.** The Company was formed by filing articles of organization with the Colorado Secretary of State on
{{ARTICLES_FILING_DATE}}, under the Colorado Limited Liability Company Act, C.R.S. 7-80-101 and following (the "Act").

**1.2 Name and office.** The Company's name is {{COMPANY_NAME}}. Its principal office is at {{PRINCIPAL_OFFICE_ADDRESS}}. The Company
keeps a registered agent in Colorado as the law requires.

**1.3 Purpose.** The Company may carry on any lawful business. Its current business is {{BUSINESS_DESCRIPTION}}.

**1.4 Effect of this Agreement.** This Agreement is the Company's operating agreement, and the Company is bound by it. It takes effect on
the date above and also covers the time since formation. Where this Agreement and the Act differ, this Agreement controls unless the Act
says it can't be changed. Where this Agreement is silent, the Act applies.

**1.5 One member.** This Agreement is written for a Company with one member. Section 8.3 says what happens before a second member joins.

## 2. Units and contributions

**2.1 Units.** The Member's interest in the Company is divided into Units. The Member holds {{MEMBER_UNITS}} Units, which are all the Units
issued, as Schedule A shows. Units carry every economic and management right in the Company.

**2.2 Contributions.** The Member has contributed {{MEMBER_CONTRIBUTION}}. The Member needn't contribute more. A promise to contribute
more binds the Member only if it is in a writing the Member signs.

**2.3 Loans.** The Member may lend money to the Company. A loan is a debt of the Company, not a contribution, if a note signed for the
Company states its interest and repayment terms.

**2.4 No certificates.** Units aren't certificated unless the Member decides otherwise under Section 4.1.

## 3. Management

**3.1 Who manages.**

[OPTION A: Member-managed. Recommended where the Member runs the business.]
The Member manages the Company and is its agent for its business. The articles of organization say the members manage.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Manager-managed. Use where someone other than the Member runs the business day to day.]
{{MANAGER_NAME}} is the Company's manager (the "Manager"), runs the business and is the Company's agent. The Member isn't an agent of the
Company just for being the Member. The Manager needs the Member's written decision to: spend, borrow or guarantee more than
{{SPENDING_LIMIT}} in one transaction or a series of related ones; sell, lease or pledge all or substantially all of the Company's
assets; merge, convert or dissolve the Company; admit a member or issue Units; amend the articles; or start a bankruptcy. The Member may
remove or replace the Manager at any time by a written decision. A Manager who is an individual must be at least 18. The articles of
organization say managers manage.
[END OPTION B]

In this Agreement, the "Managing Person" means the Member if the Member manages, or the Manager if a Manager does.

**3.2 Officers.** The Managing Person may appoint officers, give them titles and authority, and remove them. An officer has only the
authority given in writing.

**3.3 Pay.** Pay for services to the Company, including the Member's, is set in a written decision. The Member may also take distributions
under Section 6.2.

## 4. Decisions and records

**4.1 How the Member decides.** The Member decides by signing a written decision, on paper or electronically. No meeting or notice is
needed. A decision takes effect when signed, or at a later time it states.

**4.2 What needs a written decision.** Each of these needs one:

- admitting a member or issuing Units;
- selling, leasing or pledging all or substantially all of the Company's assets;
- a merger, conversion or dissolution;
- amending the articles or this Agreement;
- a tax classification election;
- naming or removing a Manager or a Successor; and
- any act a bank, lender or other party asks the Company to show was authorized.

**4.3 What changes this Agreement.** The Act counts these as part of the operating agreement: a writing the Member signs about the
Company's affairs, a written agreement between the Member and the Company, and, while a Manager who isn't the Member runs the Company,
any agreement between them, even an oral one. The Member intends that only a signed writing that says it amends this Agreement changes
it. Other writings and agreements, such as emails, contracts and filings, record decisions and don't amend this Agreement.

**4.4 Records.** The Company keeps, in one place, paper or electronic: its articles and amendments; this Agreement and amendments; every
written decision; Schedule A; its tax returns and financial statements for each year; and a record of each contribution, distribution and
loan between the Company and the Member. The Member may inspect and copy any record at any time.

## 5. Keeping the Company separate

**5.1 A separate entity.** The Company is a legal entity separate from the Member, whatever its tax classification.

**5.2 Habits the Company keeps.** The Company:

- (a) holds its money in its own accounts, in its own name, and never mixes it with the Member's money;
- (b) pays its own debts from its own money;
- (c) signs contracts, invoices and filings in its own name, with the signer's title;
- (d) keeps its own books, recording each contribution, distribution and loan between it and the Member;
- (e) puts any deal with the Member in writing, on terms fair to the Company;
- (f) uses its own name with customers, suppliers and the public;
- (g) keeps capital and insurance reasonable for its business; and
- (h) doesn't guarantee the Member's debts. The Member guarantees the Company's debts only by signing a separate guarantee.

**5.3 Effect.** A failure to follow this Article doesn't by itself make the Member liable for the Company's debts. This Article creates no
rights for anyone else.

## 6. Money and tax

**6.1 Profits and losses.** All profits, losses and distributions belong to the holder of the Units.

**6.2 Distributions.** The Managing Person may make distributions to the Member at any time. The Company makes no distribution if,
afterwards, its liabilities would exceed the fair value of its assets, each counted as the Act counts them. A Member who knowingly receives
a distribution that breaks this rule must return it.

**6.3 Tax classification.**

[OPTION A: Disregarded, the default. Recommended unless a tax professional advises otherwise.]
For federal income tax, the Company is disregarded as separate from the Member, as the default rule provides. The Company makes no
election to change that without a written decision. For employment taxes the Company is treated as a corporation, so it reports and pays
those taxes for its employees as their employer.
[END OPTION A]

[OPTION B: Taxed as a corporation or an S corporation.]
The Company elects to be classified as an association taxable as a corporation, either by filing Form 8832 or by a timely S corporation
election, which counts as that election. The Member chooses which in a written decision, after advice from the Member's own tax
professional.
[END OPTION B]

Every Article other than this Section applies whatever the Company's tax classification.

**6.4 Fiscal year.** The Company's fiscal year is the calendar year, unless a written decision changes it.

## 7. Liability, duties and indemnification

**7.1 No personal liability.** Neither the Member nor any Manager is liable for a debt, obligation or liability of the Company just for
being the Member or a Manager.

**7.2 Duties.** A managing Member and any Manager owe the Company the duties the Act sets: to account for its property, profits and
opportunities; not to deal with it for an adverse party; not to compete with it before dissolution; and a duty of care limited to
avoiding grossly negligent or reckless conduct, intentional misconduct and knowing violations of law. Everyone exercises rights under this
Agreement consistently with good faith and fair dealing. A deal between the Company and the Member that meets Section 5.2(e) is allowed.

**7.3 Reimbursement and indemnification.** The Company reimburses each current or former Member, Manager, officer and Successor for
payments made, and indemnifies each one against liabilities incurred, in the ordinary course of the Company's business or to preserve its
business or property, if made or incurred without a violation of that person's duties to the Company. The Company also indemnifies them,
to the fullest extent the law allows, against reasonable costs of a legal proceeding they're made part of because of their role, if they
acted in good faith and reasonably believed they were acting in the Company's best interests.

**7.4 Advancing legal costs.** The Company pays the reasonable legal costs of each current or former Manager, officer and Successor in any
proceeding about the role, as they're incurred, within 30 days after each invoice. The person must first give a written promise to repay
if it's finally decided that the person isn't entitled to indemnification. The promise needs no security.

**7.5 Lasting effect.** These rights continue after a person leaves. A later amendment can't reduce them for anything that has already
happened. The Company pays them only from its own assets, and the Member doesn't have to contribute to fund them. The Company may buy
insurance for them.

## 8. Transfers, new members and creditors

**8.1 Transfers.** The Member may transfer any or all Units. A person who receives Units becomes a member only as Section 8.2 or Article 9
provides. Until then, that person has only a transferee's rights: distributions, but no vote or role in management.

**8.2 Transfer of all Units.** If the Member transfers all Units to one person, that person is admitted as the sole member when the Member
signs the transfer and that person signs Exhibit 1. The Member then stops being a member.

**8.3 A second member.** The Member's consent to admit a second member, or to transfer only some Units, takes effect only when the Member
and each new member have signed an operating agreement written for two or more members. Once the Company has two or more members, it is a
partnership for federal income tax unless it has elected corporate treatment.

**8.4 Creditors of the Member.** A court may charge the Member's Units with a judgment against the Member. To the extent charged, the
creditor has only a transferee's rights: it may receive distributions, but it doesn't manage, vote or become a member. A buyer of Units at
a foreclosure sale has only a transferee's rights unless admitted as a member under this Agreement.

**8.5 No resignation without a replacement.** While the Member is the only member, the Member may resign only if another member is
admitted at the same time.

## 9. Death or incapacity of the Member

**9.1 Successor.** The Member names {{SUCCESSOR_NAME}} as the Successor. If that person has died, declines or can't act,
{{ALTERNATE_SUCCESSOR_NAME}} is the Successor. The Member may change either name at any time by a written decision. The latest one
controls.

**9.2 Admission at death.** The Member consents now to each admission in this Section. This Section is the consent of all members that
the Act requires.

- (a) At the moment of the Member's death, the Successor is admitted as a member. If the Successor doesn't take Units from the Member, the
  Successor is a member without Units or any economic right, and holds only the powers in Section 9.3.
- (b) Each person who takes Units from the Member, by will, trust, beneficiary or transfer-on-death designation, or by law, is admitted as
  a member for those Units on signing Exhibit 1. The Member's personal representative signs for an estate that holds Units.
- (c) When a person is admitted under (b), a Successor who holds no Units stops being a member.
- (d) If two or more people are admitted under (b), they sign an operating agreement written for two or more members within 90 days.
  Until then, "the Member" in this Agreement means the holders of a majority of the Units, and distributions are shared by Units.

**9.3 Running the Company meanwhile.** From the Member's death until a person is admitted under Section 9.2(b), the Successor makes the
decisions this Agreement gives the Member. If the Member was the Managing Person, the Successor also runs the business in the ordinary
course. The Successor needs the written consent of the Member's personal representative to: sell, lease or pledge all or substantially all
of the Company's assets; merge, convert or dissolve the Company; issue Units or admit anyone except under Section 9.2; or amend this
Agreement or the articles. Distributions go only to the holders of the Units. The Successor reports to the personal representative each
month and gives it access to every record. The Company may pay the Successor reasonably for this work.

**9.4 More time before dissolution.** If the Company ever has no members, it doesn't dissolve for that reason until the first anniversary
of the day the last member stopped being a member, unless a person is admitted as a member before then. During that time, the people
holding the last member's Units may admit members by unanimous consent.

**9.5 Incapacity.** The Member is Incapacitated when a court appoints a guardian or general conservator for the Member, or when two
licensed physicians state in signed writings that the Member can't manage the Member's business affairs. While the Member is
Incapacitated, the Successor acts for the Company with the powers and limits in Section 9.3, with the Member's legal representative in
place of the personal representative. The Member stays the member, and the Member's legal representative has a transferee's powers over
the Units. The Successor's authority ends when a court ends the guardianship or conservatorship, or a licensed physician states in a signed
writing that the Member can manage the Member's business affairs again.

## 10. Dissolution

**10.1 When.** The Company dissolves on the Member's written decision, after Section 9.4's time runs out, or as the Act otherwise requires.
The Member's death, incapacity, bankruptcy or transfer of Units doesn't dissolve it.

**10.2 Winding up.** The Managing Person winds up the Company. After the Member's death, the Successor does, with the personal
representative's written consent. The person winding up files a statement of dissolution, collects the assets, pays or provides for the
liabilities, and distributes what remains to the holders of the Units.

## 11. General

**11.1 Amendments.** Only a writing that says it amends this Agreement, signed by the Member, amends it. While a Manager serves, the
Company gives the Manager a copy.

**11.2 Severability.** If a court finds part of this Agreement unenforceable, the rest stays in effect, and that part is enforced as far as
the law allows.

**11.3 Who may enforce it.** Only the Member, the Company, a Manager, a Successor and the people Article 7 protects may enforce this
Agreement. It gives no rights to a creditor of the Company or the Member.

**11.4 Law.** Colorado law governs this Agreement, without regard to conflict of laws rules.

**11.5 Signing.** This Agreement may be signed in counterparts and electronically.

## Signing checklist

- Sign on or soon after the filing date. The Agreement can take effect as of formation ([7-80-108(1)(c)][t7]).
- Open a bank account in the Company's name, and move the contribution into it (Section 5.2(a)).
- Give the Successor a copy, and tell the Successor where the records are.
- Make your will, trust or transfer-on-death papers leave the Units to match Article 9.
- Keep the signed Agreement with every later written decision.

## What couldn't be checked

- **Colorado veil-piercing case law.** [7-80-107(1)][t7] sends these claims to corporate case law, which wasn't read. Article 5 lists ordinary separateness habits, not tests drawn from cases.
- **Admission at death on consent given during life.** No authority was read on whether Section 9.2 counts as the "consent of all members" [7-80-701(1)][t7] needs, once the consenting member has died. Section 9.4 keeps the estate's own route under [7-80-701(2)][t7] as a backstop.
- **Probate, guardianship and transfer-on-death law** (C.R.S. title 15) wasn't read. How Units pass at death, and whether a personal representative can be bound by Section 9.3, rest on that title.
- **Charging orders against a single-member LLC.** Only the text of [7-80-703][t7] was read. No case was read.
- **Federal tax beyond 26 CFR 301.7701-2 and 301.7701-3.** Election timing, S corporation eligibility and how an owner is paid weren't researched.
- **Duties once the Company can't pay its debts** weren't researched.
