# What makes a great data room: evidence for the Legallas quality site

Researched 2026-11-01. Public sources about investing only. No repo files were changed. Everything below is paraphrased except one marked quotation in Section 2.1. Model documents and firm checklists are summarized and counted, not reproduced, so the site should link to them rather than copy them (the repo already records Cooley GO and NVCA as link only).

## What the evidence says, in brief

1. **A data room is two rooms in time.** Before a term sheet, investors want a short business set. After it, their lawyers send a long legal request list. a16z names five files and tells founders to leave out legal documents, tax returns and minutes unless asked. YC tells founders not to over-build for seed. Gunderson places full legal diligence after the signed term sheet, and Fenwick has room access running alongside the term sheet and document negotiation. YC and Cooley then list 47 and 43 legal and business requests. A scorer that ignores this will fail good seed rooms and pass bad ones.
2. **Numbers that do not tie are the best-sourced red flag.** a16z, Klehr, the UK Space Agency and SEC complaints all name it. An SEC complaint filed 2026-10-02 alleges a seed company's data room held inflated spreadsheets falsely marked as the bookkeeper's, while the bookkeeper's real records showed far less.
3. **"NDA before access" is contradicted by investors themselves.** Cooley, Golden Seeds and the Canadian angel guide say investors generally refuse, and YC lists asking for one as a mistake. On the other side, the UK Space Agency wants one before sensitive files, and Klehr accepts an NDA or serious interest for its middle layer. A mandatory NDA gate should count against a product, not for it.
4. **Tiered access is contested too.** The UK Space Agency and Klehr recommend tiers. White Star Capital, an actual investor, recommends a single tier because of friction.
5. **Most controls are not investor demands.** Investors' own request lists say nothing about logs, watermarks or expiry, and two sources accept plain file sharing (SC Launch in 2026, Perkins Coie in 2015). The controls mostly serve the founder. Score them as founder protection, not investor expectation, and keep "per-investor viewing data" separate because the only investor source for it is advice to founders.
6. **No primary source gives a document count.** Counts of 15 to 50, 50 to 70 and 20 to 30 trace to vendors. Primary lists count request lines, and those lines run from 2 to 62 depending on stage and investor (table in Section 2.1). Test coverage of requests, not number of files.
7. **Time in the room has no primary source.** Time in diligence does: VCs average 118 hours and 83 days (early-stage firms 81 hours and 73 days), and angels in groups report a median of 20 hours.
8. **An Angel Capital Association list does exist.** The first pass missed it. It is a 2004 paper reproducing the Tech Coast Angels checklist, and the same site hosts the Golden Seeds playbook.
9. **The repo's investor folders are almost entirely legal and compliance.** None of the 62 items is a deck, model, projection, metric, use of funds, debt schedule, team bio or reference list. Details in Section 6.

What supports a size rule: the only direct statements are about excess, not number. YC says, in the one quotation in this file, "Do not spend too much time developing diligence documents for a seed round." a16z warns against wasting time on documents investors will not read. The UK Space Agency says a concise repository beats an oversized one and names hundreds of unfiltered files as a red flag. **Suggested scoring:** coverage of the stage's request lines, plus a penalty for irrelevant files, drafts and duplicates. Never a file count.

### 2.6 How the two skills scale down

**`private-equity:dd-checklist`**

- **Scales down:** the status path (not started, requested, received, in review, complete, red flag), priority marks for items gating the deal, a running red flag log with severity and fix, cross-checking room contents against the list, and noting a slow-to-respond seller. Golden Seeds makes the same responsiveness point about founders. For SaaS at Series A: ARR quality, cohorts, hosting costs.
- **Does not scale to seed:** quality of earnings, working capital normalization, EBITDA adjustments, capital expenditure splits, pension, union agreements, environmental review, culture assessment, and the deal types it assumes (platform, add-on, carve-out). SOC 2 appears only in this skill among my sources, so it stays unverified as an investor expectation. Its Excel output is not needed for a room.

**`corporate-legal:closing-checklist`**

- **Scales down, for a priced round:** tracking conditions and deliverables with responsible person, due date, blocking flag and source section. NVCA's closing conditions map onto it (compliance certificate, qualifications, opinion, board, indemnification, investors' rights agreement). So does its critical path idea: an item whose lead time exceeds the time left goes first. Example: a Delaware ratification with stockholder approval needs at least 20 days' notice (DGCL), so a defective issuance found late can move the closing. Also useful: de-duplicating by counterparty and action, and keeping a consent's conditions together.
- **Does not scale:** HSR, material adverse change analysis, escrow, payoff letters, 280G, and the assumption of a signed purchase agreement. A SAFE round has almost nothing to track, because YC says signing and funding should take minutes. Its post-closing shape still fits Form D within 15 days.

## 5. Still unverified, or does not exist

**Does not exist, as far as I could find:**

- **A target document count from any primary source**, at any stage. "15 to 50", "50 to 70" and "20 to 30" are not in any investor, law firm, angel group, academic or government source I opened. The vendor figure V-PE is unverified, and I found no study behind it.
- **A time-in-room benchmark from any primary source.** DocSend's minutes concern pitch decks, not rooms. Peony's dwell-time buckets have no method shown. Actual diligence hours are real data, but they measure the whole process (GGKS, WB).
- **An NVCA diligence checklist.** The current index lists financing documents, a confidentiality agreement for life sciences, and questionnaires for directors, officers and 5 percent holders, but no request list (NVCA-IDX). The closest substitute is the representations in NVCA.
- **A YC seed checklist.** SC Launch's link text says the Series A list also gives a seed overview, and the page I read does not. YC's seed guide says the opposite of a checklist: keep it minimal.
- **An investor survey on data room controls.** Nothing measures what share of investors want watermarks, logs, view-only or tiers. Section 4's verdicts come from individual statements.
- **A primary source on whether view-only slows investors' lawyers**, or on connecting AI tools to a room.

**Exists, but limited:**

- **Angel Capital Association list.** This corrects the first pass. ACA04 exists but is from 2004 and reproduces Tech Coast Angels' own checklist. I found no current ACA-authored list. ACA's resource page sends visitors to sign-in, and I did not follow it. Several PDFs sit under a `/data/Secure/` path but opened without sign-in. The site should paraphrase and link, and someone should confirm ACA is content with that before publishing a link to those paths.
- **Keiretsu.** KF is a copy hosted by TiE Chennai, so provenance is not confirmed by Keiretsu itself. It says the content began in the group's first two years after 2000. Its reference to a Gust deal room is older practice. A separate Keiretsu handbook was only mentioned in snippets.
- **Golden Seeds.** Dated 2009 and 2010, labeled generic.
- **Tech Coast Angels.** Only through ACA04, plus an unopened 2006 handbook. Its list refers to software of its time.
